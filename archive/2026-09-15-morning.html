Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Monday evening IRGC Navy claims a mine, not a drone strike, hit MT El Gaia in waters it "controls" — CENTCOM directly disputes it. Salalah: still no date. Petroline: still closed, still unconfirmed.

The information war over Hormuz is the real story, not a resolution of anything pending. IRGC Navy publicly claimed MT El Gaia struck a naval mine in a "restricted area" it controls, declaring "the Strait of Hormuz is closed and remains under our smart control." US CENTCOM directly rebutted this on the record, calling the claim false and attributing the hit to an Iranian drone strike. This is the sharpest dueling-narrative moment logged this cycle — not a single-sourced claim to discount, but two state actors making flatly contradictory public statements about the same incident. The IRGC's language ("control" of the strait) is a materially more aggressive posture than anything the Iranian government side has said. MT El Gaia's missing 14th Indian crew member remains unresolved — not confirmed dead.

The factional split just got more visible, not less. In the same window, Pezeshkian told India Today TV (remarks dated Saturday, surfaced only now) that the strait "would be opened up" if the US ends its blockade — a conditional, diplomacy-first line from the civilian government side. IRGC Navy asserting operational "control" while Pezeshkian offers conditional de-escalation is the clearest illustration yet of the standing IRGC-hardliner-vs-government split this file has tracked all cycle.

Salalah and Petroline: both still unresolved, no new confirmation. Reporting on why the Iran-GCC meeting was postponed sharpened in wording — Saudi Arabia reportedly submitted "proposed amendments to a draft framework" that GCC states feared would lock in a new legal status quo — but this traces to the same 9/13-9/14 Gulf-sourced reporting cluster, not fresh confirmation. No new meeting date. The Saudi Petroline pipeline remains closed with no Aramco or Saudi Energy Ministry confirmation of Energy Secretary Wright's "soon" language.
