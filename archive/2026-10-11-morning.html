Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Saturday morning: The war's deadliest single attack. Trump publicly weighing direct US strikes. Polymarket's Hormuz contract breaking its flatline in response.

A Houthi missile struck Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Saturday — the third missile strike on that specific airport this week, and the deadliest attack on Saudi Arabia in more than a decade. Saudi officials put the toll at 12 killed and 309 wounded, a cross-national casualty list: four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, one American, one Egyptian, one Jordanian, one Syrian, one Palestinian, one Sudanese. This is categorically bigger than the Oct 7-8 strike this file already had logged (3 killed) — same target, same week, an order of magnitude more lethal. The airport stayed closed through Sunday evening, Riyadh schools moved to online instruction, and more than 290 flights were canceled in the prior 24 hours alone, with Air India, flydubai, Lufthansa Group, and Pakistan International Airlines all suspending Riyadh service.

The more structurally important fact is the US response. Asked directly whether the United States would join Saudi Arabia's strikes on the Houthis, Trump said: "We may. We're going to look at it... We just found out about the recent attack. So we'll make a decision. We move very quickly." That is a real shift from the standing US posture — intelligence-sharing and targeting support for the Saudi-led coalition — toward weighing direct American military action in a theater distinct from Iran itself. It does not technically contradict Thursday's "we will not be attacking Iran" pledge, since the Houthis are an Iran-backed proxy rather than Iran directly. But it is a genuine, on-record expansion of what's on the table, landing just 72 hours after that pledge's own reversal (logged Saturday).

The structural read

This is the clearest evidence yet that Saturday's "stayed within the existing paradigm" framing — logged Friday evening, after the Saudi coalition's retaliatory strikes on Sanaa — was too comfortable. A third strike on the same airport in one week, at twelve times the prior death toll, is not the same conflict intensity as the Oct 7-8 strike; it's a step change. And the fact that it's Trump himself now publicly weighing direct US strikes, not just Saudi retaliation, is the real delta: a president now 0-for-2 this week on statements implying restraint (Thursday's no-strikes-on-Iran pledge reversed within a day, and now a "we may" on joining a different war entirely).

The one hard, falsifiable fact in this weekend's update is Polymarket's. The Hormuz Dec-31 contract — flat at 19% through every escalation this file threw at it for more than a week — finally moved, down to 17%. That's the first real break in the "calibrated indifference" pattern this file has tracked for weeks, and it arrived on exactly the news that broke the Trump-pledge bull case too. Two independent signals — a US president's public posture, and a prediction market's own price — moved the same direction at the same time, which is a meaningfully stronger joint signal than either alone.
