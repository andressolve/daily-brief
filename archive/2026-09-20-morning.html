Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Saturday Iran's SNSC secretary publicly transmitted war-end conditions via Qatar, paired with a "decisive war" threat — the hardline security apparatus setting terms, not the diplomatic track. Polymarket's Hormuz contract didn't move up on it.

Mohsen Rezaei — IRGC veteran, SNSC secretary, close ally of Supreme Leader's son Mojtaba Khamenei, and a hardliner who has previously opposed ceasefire extensions — told Al Jazeera in an exclusive interview Saturday that Iran sent seven conditions to Washington through Qatari mediators. In his own words, three are: an end to the war on all fronts, release of Iran's frozen funds, and an end to the naval blockade (Al Jazeera). Separate reporting compiles three more conditions attributed to other officials — firm guarantees against future military aggression, recognition of Iran's "legitimate rights," and reparations for damage since Feb 28 (India TV) — six of seven now named across sources, one still fully undisclosed. Rezaei paired the offer with an explicit threat: "Trump's threats will not achieve any results ... we are prepared for a decisive war" if Washington rejects the terms. A former Pentagon official (David Des Roches) told Al Jazeera Washington would likely read these terms as tantamount to "surrender" — though he flagged that Rezaei's own three conditions notably omit reparations, a possible real softening relative to the fuller compiled list.
