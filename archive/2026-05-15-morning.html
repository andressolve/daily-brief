Summit Post-Mortem: Process Without Mechanism

The two-day Trump-Xi summit in Beijing ended without an Iran breakthrough. Al Jazeera's headline captures it: "How Xi-Trump summit failed to yield Iran war breakthrough." CNN reported Trump's delegation "left China with few deals to show for it."

What was announced: Xi offered to help broker peace, pledged no military equipment to Iran, agreed Hormuz must stay open, signaled interest in buying US oil. Trump said China would purchase 200 Boeing aircraft. Trump is considering lifting sanctions on Chinese companies buying Iranian oil — a new, small carrot. No chip export controls were discussed at the bilateral level.

Structural read: Xi's mediation offer is cheap talk until China actually does something — cuts its own Iranian oil purchases, pressures Tehran with economic leverage, or commits enforcement capacity. None of that happened. The summit produced the appearance of process without the substance of mechanism. The congressional enrichment constraint (229 lawmakers) remains the structural blocker on the US side. Tehran's counterproposal was rejected as "garbage" on May 10. The zero-overlap problem persists.

Hormuz: Physical Reality Worsening

Open transits remain at near-zero since May 6 (~5% of pre-war levels). 1,550+ vessels stranded, 22,500 mariners trapped. Project Freedom paused. DHL forecasts 4-6 months to normalization even if conflict ends next month. UK deploying drones, fighters, and a warship for an international defensive mission. The physical reality is diverging further from diplomatic rhetoric.

Lebanon: T-3 to Ceasefire Expiry

The Yield Surge Is the Story

The bond market is repricing the Fed path. CNBC: "Treasury yields surge as inflation data points to tricky rates path." CPI 3.8% + PPI 6.0% + oil >$100 + Hormuz closure = inflation is not coming down. CME FedWatch: 51.3% chance of NO change by December (was 23.5% a week ago). Rate hike odds have climbed to ~40-45% for some hike this year. This is a structural repricing, not a one-day blip.

The four-day record run was built on the assumption that rate cuts were coming and AI growth would power through any headwinds. Today, the bond market broke that assumption. The question now: is this an orderly repricing (gradual, 50-100bp over weeks) or disorderly (sharp, forced selling, margin calls)?

Gold Crash: Anomalous

Gold crashed $155 to $4,548 — a 3.3% drop. In a risk-off environment with oil spiking and equities selling, gold should bid higher, not lower. Possible explanations: margin-call liquidation from equity selloff, dollar strength, or profit-taking after extended run above $4,700. This is worth monitoring — if gold continues falling alongside equities, it signals broad deleveraging rather than simple risk rotation.

Warsh Day 1

Private Credit Inflection

Delta since last run: Bloomberg milestone — BDC redemptions exceeded fundraising for the first time ever in Q1 2026. Rate cut odds collapsing adds new variable.

Bloomberg (May 14): Non-traded BDC redemptions exceeded fundraising for the first time on record in Q1 2026. Gross sales: $4.9B (down 46% QoQ, down 59% YoY). Redemptions: $6.9B. Net outflows: ~$2B.

Four major funds met <50% of redemption requests: Blue Owl OCIC, Blue Owl OTIC, Ares Strategic Income, and Apollo Debt Solutions. Overall sector met 74% of requests.

Structural read: This is the inflection we've been tracking. When redemptions exceed fundraising, the liquidity mismatch becomes a structural drain — funds must sell assets (at discounts) to meet redemptions while simultaneously unable to raise new capital. This accelerates NAV declines, which triggers more redemptions. The reflexive loop is now active.

Rate path complication: With rate cut odds collapsing (51.3% no change by Dec), the "higher for longer" thesis reverses the NII headwind — positive for floating-rate income but negative for credit quality as borrowers face higher debt service. Net effect depends on which force dominates. Watch default rates.
