Iran / Middle East — Weekend Priority Beat

Delta since Saturday morning: No formal fuel-price announcement, but the rial hit a fresh record and a genuine internal-division signal emerged on hike timing. Rezaei/IRGC hardened rather than softened. No new Hormuz kinetic incident, no China bank escalation.

The rial crossed a fresh record this weekend, and the pace of the move is itself notable. The dollar hit ~208,000 tomans (~2.08M rial) on 8/30, up from ~206,400 at Friday's close and further from the ~2.02M level tracked as of 8/24 — a real, accelerating decline, not a plateau at the prior record. Reporting links the move explicitly to fuel-crisis strain and fear of another price shock. Read: this is a market-based vote on the fuel-crisis-and-delay dynamic this file has tracked all week — the currency is pricing continued uncertainty and inflation risk in real time, ahead of any actual policy announcement. The "prepare public opinion" delay itself carries an economic cost the regime is absorbing whether or not it ultimately pulls the trigger on a formal hike.

A senior establishment conservative just publicly hedged against a near-term hike — a genuine internal-division signal, not just delay. Gholam-Ali Haddad-Adel (former Majlis speaker, Khamenei's son-in-law) said publicly that people "should not wake up on Friday morning [9/4] and see that gasoline has become more expensive," insisting any move first needs "national unity and cohesion." Separately, MP Abbas Goudarzi claimed the government has "backed away" from a price-based hike altogether in favor of ID-linked rationing quotas instead. Read: both data points cut against a clean resolution to the standing fuel-crisis watch item. Haddad-Adel is not a marginal voice — an in-law of the Supreme Leader publicly contradicting the government's own signaled urgency is a genuine coherence problem, not spin. The more likely near-term outcome now looks like a slipped or reshaped policy (quotas rather than a price shock) rather than the straightforward price-hike announcement this file has been watching for.

Rezaei and the IRGC hardened their public posture this weekend rather than building on Thursday's corridor opening. Rezaei reiterated Iran "will not yield" and threatened to further restrict the strait if regional neighbors help the US apply economic pressure on Iran; the IRGC spokesman separately repeated that the strait "will under no circumstances be reopened" absent US compliance with Iran's conditions. Iran's FM described the Oman-mediated talks as continuing "in all seriousness" but delayed by unnamed "actors trying to exert influence." Read: this is the opposite direction from what a reader might expect after Rezaei's 8/28 conditions-list confirmation — rather than the corridor story advancing toward implementation, the weekend produced harder rhetoric and a vague, unexplained accusation of third-party interference. Worth treating the corridor as still gated behind the maximalist conditions list, with no forward motion this weekend.

The scale of 2026's execution total is worth naming explicitly, not just the four-day count already logged. Rights groups now put the cumulative 2026 total at 900-916 executions since January, including at least two Jan-2026-protest-linked prisoners hanged in Isfahan this window. Read: the 23-in-four-days figure already tracked was itself a real acceleration; seeing it against a 900+ year-to-date backdrop sharpens rather than changes the read — this is a regime running an unusually high repression baseline all year, consistent with the IRGC's own admission (8/27) that domestic unrest is its top fear. China bank sanctions and Hormuz kinetic activity: no change — no major Chinese bank named since 8/25's list, no new kinetic incident since ~8/26.
