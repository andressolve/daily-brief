Iran / Middle East

Delta since last run (8/27 evening): Oman corridor moved from disputed to confirmed-but-gated. US publicly denies talks despite intensifying shuttle diplomacy. Fuel crisis: no material change. No new kinetic incidents.

Oman corridor: confirmed real, but reframed as leverage rather than de-escalation. Mohsen Rezaei, Secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council — a senior, authoritative voice, not just an IRGC spokesman — publicly confirmed on 8/28 that Iran and Oman have an arrangement on shared Hormuz waters and revenue. That resolves the IRGC-vs-diplomat contradiction this file flagged on 8/27 morning. But Rezaei added the deal only activates fully once the US "fulfils Iran's conditions," and the IRGC spokesman specified those explicitly: (1) lift the naval blockade of Iranian ports, (2) pay war-damage compensation, (3) remove sanctions. Qatar's FM separately discussed the corridor and mine-clearing with Tehran — a third channel still active alongside Oman and Pakistan.

Read: this is a genuine resolution of yesterday's open contradiction, but not in the bullish direction a first read might suggest. Iran isn't disputing the corridor exists — it's using confirmation of the corridor as a platform to formalize a maximalist counter-demand. Functionally, Rezaei's conditions list is Tehran's answer to the Pakistan-delivered US offer: a formalized, still-unaccepted counter-demand, not the "stall" this file logged 8/26-27, but not acceptance either. (Al Jazeera, Al-Monitor, Middle East Eye)

A notable tension worth naming: the White House continues to publicly deny any active US-Iran negotiations even as Pakistan, Oman, and Qatar all actively mediate the same underlying Hormuz-normalization problem. Read as both sides preferring deniable, indirect channels over a formal bilateral track right now — this cuts against reading the multi-channel diplomatic activity as necessarily close to a breakthrough. It looks more like parallel positioning than converging negotiation. (CNN)

Fuel crisis: no material change overnight. No formal price hike announced, no protest triggered. Officials continue to call a hike "inevitable" while withholding timing, citing 2019-unrest fears explicitly. Petrol queues persist in Tehran. The 10-15 day "prepare public opinion" window flagged 8/27 evening stands unchanged. (NCRI, Iran Focus)

No new Hormuz kinetic incidents since the 8/26-27 unclaimed-projectile tanker strike. Minor, tangential item: the UK reportedly warned Israel against removing UK/European officials from the Gaza ceasefire coordination center — not a core-Iran development, logged for completeness only.

Markets / Macro

Delta since last run (8/27 evening): Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote was a genuine hawkish surprise — this file's own "priced-in neutral" baseline was wrong. Fed hike odds jumped across three instruments. New semiconductor-tariff policy thread emerged. Oil and gold little changed.

Warsh's keynote: a genuine hawkish surprise against this file's own baseline, worth naming as a calibration miss, not just a data point. This file's 8/27 entries carried a BofA-survey-sourced expectation that markets had priced in a "mostly neutral" speech. Instead, Warsh explicitly said inflation is "still too high," underlying trends "have not meaningfully improved," and the Fed has "work to do" — while offering no forward guidance, forcing markets to reprice on tone alone. He called the economy "remarkably resilient," which reads as a hawkish "no urgency to cut, and possibly room to hike" framing. (Fed transcript, CNBC, Bloomberg)

September hike odds: a real, large, multi-instrument move. Polymarket's combined hike probability jumped from ~30.85% last night to ~47.95% this morning (+17.1pp) on $4.31M volume; CME FedWatch independently jumped to ~45.7%. Bond market bear-flattened cleanly (2Y +~8bps to a one-month high, 10Y +~2bps, 30Y -~1.5bps) — the textbook signature of a near-term-specific hawkish repricing, not a broader growth-scare move. Worth naming explicitly: the ~45-53% secondary-citation figure this file dismissed as a bad data point on 8/26-27 landed almost exactly where the real number moved today — a near-miss worth flagging, not smoothing over. Markets are now pricing something close to even odds on a September hike, an unusual stance this deep into a cutting-oriented cycle expectation. See Polymarket section below for full detail.

Equities: muted reaction at the speech itself, no confirmed close data. Pre-speech futures were mixed; cash open showed Dow +0.1%, Nasdaq -0.3%. This file does not yet have a confirmed post-speech close — flagged as an open item for tonight's run rather than guessed at.

New, material cross-beat thread: broader semiconductor tariffs reportedly under consideration. Politico (8/27) reports the Trump administration is weighing expanding chip-related tariffs to laptops, servers, and gaming consoles — and potentially scrapping the January 2026 data-center exemption that has been the operative baseline all cycle. Amazon and Microsoft are specifically named. A rollback of the data-center exemption would touch the AI-capex buildout directly (AMZN, MSFT, and by extension NVDA/MU/TSMC/AMD supply chains) — still a "weighing," not a decision. (CNBC, Tom's Hardware)

Oil: essentially flat. Brent ~$86.44, WTI ~$82 — little changed from Thursday. Cross-ref Iran/Oman corridor above; not a fresh catalyst either way. Gold and BTC little changed; VIX Friday-morning print not confirmed — flagged as a gap, not guessed. No major scheduled data Friday beyond final University of Michigan consumer sentiment.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last run (8/27 evening): NVIDIA paused part of its own revenue-share financing program — first NVIDIA-side retreat this cycle. Marvell beat-and-raised but sold off on timing, not demand. Anthropic-MatX deal abandoned. SK Hynix broke ground in Indiana.

NVIDIA pauses parts of its own revenue-share compute-financing program — a genuine first, worth reading carefully. WSJ reports NVIDIA paused parts of the vendor-financing structure disclosed in its own Q2 FY27 10-Q: six-year committed compute agreements with select AI cloud partners totaling $36B, with NVIDIA earning 50% revenue share above a threshold. The pause reportedly happened the week of 8/20-21, driven by internal antitrust concerns over NVIDIA's control of partner business practices. NVIDIA's statement: the underlying $36B commitment book is unaffected. No primary source names specific paused counterparties.

Read: all cycle, this file has tracked NVIDIA's demand-capture mechanisms only ever scaling up — this is the first pullback, even partial, from one it built itself. It reads as a governance/antitrust-risk pause on program structure, not a demand pullback, but it's still a genuinely new data point on the standing "is this circularity fragile" question — logged as Moderate disconfirming to the ai-ecosystem-analyst's sub-thesis #5. (WSJ via Reuters/Yahoo Finance, cross-referenced via ai-ecosystem-analyst research/2026/2026-08-28-update.md)

Market reaction outran the primary reporting. CRWV, NBIS, and IREN all fell on the WSJ story despite no company confirming counterparty status: IREN hit hardest (-7%+ to ~$38), CRWV -4%, NBIS as much as -14% intraday before partially rebounding. This is the market pricing the worst-case read faster than reporting can confirm it — treat the sell-off as sentiment, not evidence. (TipRanks)

Marvell: the beat-and-raise was real; the sell-off is a timing story, not a demand story. Q2 FY2027: record revenue $2.739B (+37% YoY), data center revenue $2.17B (+46% YoY), FY2028 guide raised to ~$18B (from $16.5B). Stock fell ~7.9-8% anyway: nearly all revenue under the Google attach-silicon warrant deal vests starting August 1, 2026 — the last day of the just-reported quarter — so most Google-related revenue won't land until FY2029. This is the same "world vs. market" pattern flagged repeatedly this cycle (AMAT, TSMC) — real demand, market reaction confirms expectations had run ahead of near-term recognition. Logged Strong supporting to the custom-silicon sub-thesis. (CNBC, BNN Bloomberg)

Anthropic held, then abandoned, a ~$7B MatX chip-startup acquisition. Reuters (single-sourced) reports Anthropic pivoted to a partnership/customer relationship instead. Mildly consistent with staying compute-vendor-diversified — context only, no change to the standing training-compute-mix probability tracker.

SK Hynix breaks ground on first US facility. Groundbreaking at Purdue Research Park, Indiana, for an advanced-packaging/R&D center, >$4B investment, HBM4E volume production targeted Q3 2029. Moderate supporting evidence for the structural memory-undersupply thesis, but the 2028-2029 timeline means it doesn't move the near-term supply picture.

Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

The macro story quietly became the day's biggest catalyst: a Fed contract that shrugged off NVDA's earnings, the AI-capex debate, and a week of Iran headlines just repriced +17.1pp in one session on a Jackson Hole speech this file itself expected to be neutral. That's the calibration lesson to carry forward — the "priced-in" read on Fed communication has been wrong twice now (7/26-27 dismissal of the 45-53% figure, and today's "mostly neutral" baseline), and both times the real number ended up close to what the dismissed source said.

On the portfolio, nothing here changes the zero-position stance — the NVDA-pause sell-off in CRWV/NBIS is running on sentiment ahead of confirmation, the new tariff threat is a "weighing" not a decision, and Iran's Oman corridor news is a formalized counter-demand, not a breakthrough. The one live risk worth tracking into next week is whether a near-coin-flip September hike probability starts to show up in equity pricing beyond the bond market's already-clean bear-flattener.
