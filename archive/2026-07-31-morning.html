Iran / Middle East

Delta since last run: Iran's army claims (unconfirmed) strikes on two Kuwaiti air bases hosting US forces; new fronts in Bahrain and Egypt; Araghchi pressuring Bulgaria/Cyprus over US basing rights.

Iran's army says it struck Ahmad al-Jaber and Ali Al Salem air bases in Kuwait overnight — both host US forces. Neither CENTCOM nor the Kuwaiti government has confirmed this claim as of this writing. New fronts opened simultaneously in Bahrain and Egypt, and Araghchi is now pressuring Bulgaria and Cyprus over US basing rights on their territory — a geographic widening that outpaces any single confirmed kinetic event.

Saturday's proposed talks, the Momeni/Mojtaba succession thread, and Houthi posture are all stale — no movement since last night's entry.

Structural read: this looks like an escalation-dominant phase, not an escalation-effective one. Iran is testing breadth — more claimed targets, more fronts — without yet claiming to have degraded actual US capability anywhere. That distinction matters: a regime broadening a war it can't intensify is signaling something different than one that's actually landing blows.

Bear case on this read: if the Kuwait claim is confirmed later today, this whole framing inverts — a confirmed strike on a US-hosting air base in a third Gulf state is a materially different animal than proxy skirmishing, and the "testing breadth not depth" read would look complacent in hindsight.

Bear case: a market this calm heading into an escalating war is either correctly reading the Kuwait claim as noise, or complacent. Polymarket's -1.0pp fade (not a rally) leans slightly toward the complacent read, not the noise read.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last run: AMZN's beat is mostly a non-cash markup; the Korea/Taiwan rally was a leverage unwind, not demand; Meta's capex silence is the standout fragility signal.

AMZN's Q2 print is real on operations — AWS grew 36.7%, FY26 capex was raised to ~$220B, and Jassy said outright that AWS still doesn't have enough capacity to meet demand. But the headline net-income beat is misleading: $53.4B of the $62.6B net-income increase is a non-cash markup on Amazon's Anthropic stake, not earned profit. Core operating profit still grew a real 43% to $27.46B, so this isn't a false beat — but this morning's market-cap move is pricing more certainty than the number actually supports.

Korea and Taiwan's overnight +26-30% reversal was leverage mechanics, not fundamentals: a forced liquidation at Situational Awareness LP cascaded into a Korean leveraged-ETF short squeeze. This is a "world vs. market" case — don't read it as a China/chip-demand signal.

The item that should worry the bulls most: Meta's call gave no FY27 capex number for the first time this cycle, stood up a second BlackRock SPV, and priced a $12.5B bond at 7.53% — richer than prior vendor-financing paper. Logged as a Moderate disconfirming data point under the standing vendor-financing-fragility thesis (sub-thesis #5). New watch item: Google is backstopping a $15B loan for Anthropic's Texas data-center campus (Nexus Data Centers) for ~20% equity — worth tracking whether this is Google absorbing Anthropic balance-sheet risk off its own capex line.

Calibration flag (per house rules): the ai-ecosystem-analyst's own P008 prediction (Iran/oil-driven capex risk) was walked back 68%→63% overnight. That 68% upgrade on 7/27 assumed a genuine ceasefire — reality has since falsified that premise via resumed, CENTCOM-confirmed US strikes. Flagging explicitly rather than absorbing silently. Open tension carried to tonight: unclear whether that 63% already reflects this morning's Kuwait claim or predates it.

Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

Two stories are running in parallel and mostly not talking to each other. On the geopolitical side, Iran is widening a war it can't yet prove it's winning — more claimed fronts, no confirmed hits on hard US targets — and the market that should price that risk (Hormuz-normalization) actually moved in the calmer direction. On the portfolio side, the AI-capex trade had a genuinely good morning (AMZN, Korea/Taiwan, MU, NBIS all green) but the two moves that mattered most — AMZN's beat and the Korea/Taiwan rally — are both softer than the tape suggests: one is mostly a non-cash markup, the other is a leverage unwind. The one number that should actually move the thesis is Meta's silence on FY27 capex and its richer bond pricing — the first sign that vendor-financing costs are creeping up, not the war headlines. Still zero positions, still watching.
