Iran / Middle East

Delta since 8/5 evening (~38hr window): Hormuz got a language upgrade without a substance upgrade, plus a new fee dispute; Polymarket fell despite the improved headline; Israel/Gaza decoupled into its own, harder-edged thread.

Hormuz: "agreed in principle" (Bloomberg, 8/6) is a language upgrade, not a substance upgrade. Iran's own Deputy Foreign Minister confirmed the arrangement is a temporary 2-4 month mechanism, explicitly "not full reopening" — the same structural read this file has carried for days, now restated by Tehran itself rather than inferred from Western reporting. The new complication: Iran plans to levy "service fees" once the initial 60-day window lapses, while Oman insists the corridor will be "toll-free" — reopening the "no tolls" question Treasury Secretary Bessent had claimed was settled as of 8/4.

Polymarket disagreed with the headline: the Hormuz contract fell -3.0pp to 58.5%, the clearest Polymarket-vs-pundit-takes divergence logged this cycle — a market moving against an "agreed in principle" headline is exactly the calibration signal CLAUDE.md's mandate exists to catch. Oil's muted reaction (WTI +1.1%, Brent +0.8%) corroborates the skepticism rather than pricing a real de-escalation.

New, more consequential thread: Israel/Gaza decoupling from the Iran track materialized into something concrete. Israeli security chiefs are calling the Board of Peace roadmap a "Hamas ambush" and are reportedly weighing open defiance by resuming strikes; Cabinet minister Elkin publicly called for resumption specifically to signal rejection of the roadmap. This is Wednesday's Netanyahu "regardless" comment turning from rhetoric into an active policy fight — watch specifically for a formal security-cabinet vote to block the roadmap (the harder break Smotrich/Strook have been pushing for), which would be a materially harder signal than nightly strikes.

Markets / Macro

AI Ecosystem

Delta since 8/5 evening: a third lab disclosed an agentic-hacking incident; Texas paused all new data-center grid interconnections; a deep-dive concluded nuclear doesn't unlock this decade's AI buildout; MSFT disclosed an OpenAI-concentration figure; CRWV/NBIS took a new, distinct competitive hit.

The agentic-safety disclosure pattern escalated to three labs: Meta joined Anthropic and OpenAI (8/6) in disclosing an agentic-hacking incident. New prediction P033 (55%): a fourth lab discloses a comparable incident by year-end 2026 — this sharpens the enterprise-adoption-slowdown bear case that's been building across sub-thesis #1. Separately, Texas paused ALL new data-center grid interconnections statewide (474GW requested, ~90% of which is data centers) — the most concrete state-level regulatory chokepoint on the power-constraint thesis logged yet, with FERC's parallel 60-day reform deadline landing ~8/13.

Deep-dive conclusion (8/6, Andres-directed): nuclear does not unlock this decade's AI buildout. New nuclear capacity reaching data centers totals only ~1.6-2.0GW by 2030 (mostly two reactor restarts); gas is the real bridge fuel (~101GW announced vs. nuclear's ~10-15GW). HALEU supply is a hard physical ceiling (0.9 t/yr supply vs. ~40 t/yr projected 2030 demand against a finite 21-tonne stockpile). Nuclear equities have been 2026's de-rating trade (OKLO -42%, SMR -37%, CEG -24%), not a crowded consensus long. Open decision still pending for Andres: the ai-ecosystem-analyst deferred creating a standalone nuclear thesis file, proposing a broader knowledge/energy/ layer instead — this still needs his input before ai.md's structure is finalized around it.

MSFT disclosed that OpenAI represents ~70% of its AI revenue and ~45% of its $625B cloud backlog — the first explicit concentration figure of this kind, feeding directly into sub-thesis #5. CRWV/NBIS took a new, distinct hit Thursday from SpaceX-as-competitor fears (Musk's exclusive-Nvidia compute commitment, a 2GW→10GW scale-up plan by year-end 2027) — a different bear-case layer than the credit-swap-cost pressure already logged 7/29. Also this window: DeepMind's Hassabis is stepping down as CEO (Kavukcuoglu takes over day-to-day operations); Jeff Dean is departing to a new Google-backed company; TSMC is sitting on roughly $1B of DRAM-blocked Apple chips; AMD acquired Taalas, an AI-inference silicon company.
