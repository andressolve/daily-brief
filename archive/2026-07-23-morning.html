Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night New tanker strikes off Saudi Arabia are Houthi-claimed, not direct IRGC action; Trump extended his retaliation doctrine to hold Iran responsible for Houthi attacks; no strike executed under either doctrine yet; still no formal Iranian ceasefire response.

Two tankers (Encelia and Layla) were hit in the Red Sea roughly 130km off Al Shuqaiq, Saudi Arabia — claimed by the Houthis, not by Iran or the IRGC directly. This is a materially different actor/geography than the Gulf/Hormuz strikes this file has tracked as direct Iranian action; some overnight coverage described the strikes in more ambiguous IRGC-adjacent language, but the correct attribution is the Yemen/Red Sea proxy front (the same Houthi blockade declared 7/20), not a new direct-Iran vector. Trump's doctrine hardened into two explicit parts: the standing threat to destroy an Iranian bridge or power plant for every future tanker attack, and a new extension holding Iran responsible for Houthi attacks specifically — collapsing the proxy/principal distinction rhetorically, even though the strikes themselves are Houthi-claimed. No retaliatory strike has been executed under either doctrine as of this morning. No new formal Iranian ceasefire response — the standing position (won't negotiate while still under attack) carries forward unchanged.

Structural read The three-track model (diplomatic/pragmatic-hawk/IRGC) now has an explicit fourth dimension — a Houthi proxy track that Trump's rhetoric is trying to fold into the "Iran responsible" framework even though operationally it's a distinct actor with its own strike cadence. A Houthi-claimed Red Sea strike is not the same probability-of-US-response event as a direct IRGC Hormuz strike, even if the rhetoric treats them as equivalent — worth watching whether a future US response targets Yemen specifically or escalates against Iran directly.
