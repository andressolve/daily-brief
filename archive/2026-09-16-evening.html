Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since this morning A second front opened at the highest-stakes level yet: a claimed strike near Mecca. Petroline's "days vs. weeks" gap sharpened into a direct, same-day contradiction. China's ask to Iran got concrete.
Escalation, not yet resolved Saudi Arabia accused the Houthis of targeting Mecca — Islam's holiest city — with a drone, intercepted by a Saudi-led coalition, no casualties. Riyadh called the security of the holy sites a "red line" and vowed retaliation. The Houthis deny targeting Mecca specifically, saying the actual targets were Saudi oil and military facilities "far removed from the holy places." The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation condemned the attack regardless of attribution. This is the first such claim against Mecca in nearly a decade, and it raises retaliation and regional-legitimacy stakes well beyond the war's existing shipping-and-pipeline frame.

That's a sharper, more optimistic claim than his prior "soon" language — and it stands directly against the independent, satellite-imagery-based 4-6 week repair estimate this file backfilled on 9/14 (pumping-station electrical/control damage across two regions, wartime contractor-access and parts-logistics constraints). Oil sold off hard on Wright's claim today (see Markets). Treat this as an administration optimism claim the market chose to believe, not as newly confirmed fact.

China's Wang Yi/Araghchi meeting sharpened from a vague mediator gesture into a concrete two-part ask. Wang explicitly urged Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and return to the June US-Iran memorandum of understanding, backed by China's own "four-point peace proposal," and raised a "post-war security architecture" for the region. Araghchi said Tehran does not want the conflict to continue and wants a diplomatic solution while defending its rights. Still no operational movement — this is China stating a position, not brokering a deal.
