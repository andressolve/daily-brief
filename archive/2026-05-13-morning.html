Trump-Xi Summit (T-1)

Trump landed in Beijing for a two-day state visit with meetings scheduled Thursday-Friday. The delegation includes Secretary of State Rubio, Defense Secretary Hegseth, and a CEO contingent: Elon Musk, Tim Cook, and Jensen Huang. Iran is expected to dominate the summit agenda. The core ask: Trump wants Xi to use China's leverage over Tehran — China purchases 80%+ of Iran's shipped crude exports — to push Iran back to negotiations and reopen Hormuz. (CNN, Al Jazeera, NPR)

Bessent and He Lifeng completed three hours of talks in Seoul at Incheon Airport, laying the economic groundwork for the summit. Xinhua described the exchanges as "candid, in-depth, and constructive" — positive atmospherics but no Iran-specific commitments surfaced. (Xinhua, Korea Herald)

UN Hormuz Resolution: 112 Nations

A Bahrain-led draft UNSC resolution calling for freedom of navigation through Hormuz has gained 112 co-sponsors. Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and Kuwait joined as principal sponsors alongside Bahrain and the US. India, Japan, South Korea, and most EU members signed on. This is the second attempt; the first was vetoed by China and Russia. No vote date set. (Al Jazeera, UN News)

Structural read: 112 co-sponsors is near-universal. But diplomatic isolation doesn't change Iran's calculation — the PGSA toll model is already operational, bilateral deals (Iraq, Pakistan) are legitimizing it in practice, and IRGC institutional interests are locked in. This is the gap between law and power that the Goldman "sloppy peace" framework predicted.

Iran Hardening Pre-Summit

Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi: "True peace cannot be built with the language of humiliation, threat, and forced concession-taking." Accused the US of seeking "capitulation rather than peace." The IRGC framing is dominant heading into Trump-Xi. (Al Jazeera)

Hormuz: Bilateral Deals Legitimizing PGSA

Iraq secured safe passage for two crude carriers (~4M barrels) through IRGC-authorized routes. Pakistan continues LNG imports. Turkey's FM Fidan traveled to Doha warning Hormuz "must not be used as a weapon." Each bilateral deal that uses IRGC-authorized routes legitimizes Iran's sovereignty claim in practice, even as the UN resolution challenges it in law. (Al Jazeera)

PPI: The Second Hammer

April PPI surged +1.4% MoM and +6.0% YoY — the largest monthly increase since March 2022. Estimates were +0.5% / +4.8%. Nearly 60% of the increase came from services (+1.2%). Industrial chemicals, diesel fuel, freight trucking, gasoline, jet fuel, and internet advertising all rose. Stage-1 goods inputs jumped +2.7%. (BLS)

Structural read: CPI 3.8% showed inflation reaching consumers. PPI 6.0% shows it's in the production pipeline at every stage. This is not a demand shock — it's supply-driven (Hormuz → energy → freight → everything). The Fed cannot cut its way out of supply-side inflation. Warsh's options are: hold (let it run), hike (tank growth further), or cut (which Trump wants, but which feeds inflation). All three are bad.

Warsh Confirmed

Senate confirmed Warsh to the Fed Board of Governors 51-45 on Monday. Fetterman was the lone Democratic yes vote. The chair vote is expected as soon as today, ahead of Powell's term expiration on Friday. (CNBC, Al Jazeera)

Cross-beat: Iran → oil → CPI + PPI → Fed → rates → everything. This chain is now printing at every stage. The question is no longer "will stagflation arrive?" but "what does Warsh do about it?"

The Meta Deal and Pennsylvania Factory

NBIS disclosed a $27 billion, 5-year Meta deal — the largest single AI infrastructure contract in history. It comprises $12B in compute capacity purchases starting early 2027 plus an additional $15B flexible contract. Separately, NBIS secured 1.2 GW of power and land in Pennsylvania for a new AI factory, its second US gigawatt-scale site after Independence, Missouri. (BusinessWire, Nebius)

Structural read: NBIS just proved the neocloud model works at scale. After CRWV's -12% post-earnings fade, the NBIS beat is the opposite: beat on every metric plus a transformative anchor contract. The question has shifted from "is the backlog real?" to "they're monetizing faster than expected." The $27B Meta deal validates the computational abundance thesis at the infrastructure layer.

Bear case: Net income of $621M includes a $780.6M revaluation gain on equity securities — strip that out and operating losses persist. But the bull case is now stronger: $9.3B cash, $27B Meta anchor, EBITDA turning positive.
