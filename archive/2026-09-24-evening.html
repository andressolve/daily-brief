Iran / Middle East Red Sea

Delta since morning run Saudi Arabia says it intercepted six Houthi ballistic missiles fired at Taif and Yanbu. Brent settled ~$106.50 (+~$3.50, highest close since 9/15); WTI ~$94.50, ending a six-session ~-13% slide (single search summary). No formal Iranian reply to the US response found. The "reopen Hormuz within 7 days if the US eases pressure" offer dates to 9/22, so it is not new today.

Structural read. Yanbu is the Red Sea terminus of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, the main bypass for Hormuz. A strike aimed at it is the second-chokepoint lever in action, one day after Yahya Safavi (senior adviser to the Supreme Leader) warned "a new front may open." The faction question is whether Tehran directed it or the Houthis acted on their own authority. Public sources cannot answer that tonight, and it matters: directed action reads as a coordinated escalation price; autonomous action reads as Tehran having less control over its coalition than its rhetoric implies.

Two calendars. Rezaei's "three or four days" clock, started ~9/23, expires ~9/26–27. Trump's stated post-midterm timeline does not overlap it. An incident on the water or a Houthi hit that kills Saudi civilians before then is the likeliest way the two get reconciled. Bear case on the diplomatic read: oil rose 3% on the strike even with a phased-reopening headline in the tape; the physical market is not pricing a deal.

Bull case for the diplomatic read: Polymarket disagrees with the oil tape. Hormuz-normal by Dec 31 went to 22.5% on real volume, after sliding from 24% on 9/22 to 20.5% on 9/23. The market is pricing the mediated track above the escalation, for at least one session.
