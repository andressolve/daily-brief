Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since this morning: Israel's election resolved into deadlock, not a winner — but Netanyahu's own coalition collapsed from 68 to 50 seats.

Final exit-poll/early-count picture: Eisenkot's opposition bloc leads 52-54 seats to Netanyahu's bloc's 50, neither reaching the 61-seat majority threshold — the same stalemate this morning's pre-vote polling suggested. But the headline bloc-vs-bloc number obscures a much sharper underlying result: Netanyahu's governing coalition lost 18 seats in raw terms from the outgoing Knesset (68 → 50). Eisenkot has publicly called on Netanyahu to accept defeat and step down, saying the public "voted for change." Record turnout reached 77.7%, the highest in over two decades. This is now a coalition-formation fight, not a same-night resolution — Eisenkot has all but ruled out governing with Arab parties, which complicates his own path to 61 despite leading. No new Hormuz/Houthi/Tehran-track development found tonight; Hormuz Polymarket held flat at 12% through the day. Oil continued its modest drift lower, easing into tomorrow's Fed decision.

The structural read

Tonight's real news is that the headline bloc math (52-54 vs. 50, barely different from pre-vote polling) sits on top of a much bigger shift than that comparison implies — an 18-seat collapse in Netanyahu's own coalition would normally produce a clean transfer of power, except Israel's multi-party system means no bloc actually crossed 61. Bear case: a coalition-formation fight that drags on for weeks, with Netanyahu possibly refusing to concede despite his bloc's collapse, adds genuine domestic-political uncertainty to Israeli decision-making on Iran at exactly the moment the Tehran diplomatic track is trying to find traction — a government in flux has less unified authority to either restrain or escalate. Bull case: Eisenkot's bloc leading, even narrowly, plus Netanyahu's 68-to-50 collapse, is the clearest electoral rebuke of the war-era government this file has tracked — if a change-oriented coalition eventually forms, it could shift Israeli posture toward the diplomatic track for reasons independent of anything Iran itself does.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since this morning: Anthropic's S-1 filing was the day's dominant catalyst, not this morning's four circular-financing deals.

Anthropic filed its S-1 — $16.3B in H1 2026 revenue, an $80.7B net loss (mostly noncash accounting charges), seeking to list Class A shares on Nasdaq as "ANT." The filing lifted the entire complex: Nasdaq +1.18%, chip names broadly green (NVDA +0.90%, AMD +5.45%, MU +2.77%), and the rally extended into Asia overnight prep (KOSPI +3.1%, Nikkei +1.7%). Broadcom rose +4% specifically on today's confirmed $63B Anthropic chip-financing deal — this morning's file flagged that deal's structure (Broadcom as vendor, financing arranger, and credit guarantor simultaneously) as a bear-case balance-sheet risk; today's market reaction treated it as a bull signal instead. CRWV and NBIS extended their green streak to a third straight session, this time sharply (+7.94%/+11.22%), on CoreWeave bringing a Denmark data center mostly online and Nebius raising ~$279M for its France site — company-specific operational news, not just sentiment. AI-bubble-burst Polymarket eased to 8% (Dec 31, from 9.4%) despite the headline-grabbing $80.7B net-loss figure.

The structural read

Today tests whether this morning's "four circular-financing deals in 24 hours" framing should be read as escalating risk or as the market's own vote of confidence — and the market voted confidence, hard, treating the Broadcom-Anthropic deal's confirmation and the S-1 filing itself as reasons to buy. That doesn't resolve the underlying structural question (is the system financing its own demand), but it's a real data point that the market, today, isn't treating these deals as distress signals. Bear case: an $80.7B net loss is a genuinely enormous number even allowing for noncash treatment, and a market that rallies hard on an IPO filing without waiting for the roadshow's actual scrutiny of that figure risks the same sentiment-outrunning-fundamentals pattern flagged before. Bull case: CRWV/NBIS's third green session, this time on hard operational news rather than just an absence of bad news, is a qualitatively stronger signal than the prior two sessions, and the AI-bubble contract's calm reaction to the net-loss headline is good evidence Polymarket is still discriminating on real criteria, not headline size.
