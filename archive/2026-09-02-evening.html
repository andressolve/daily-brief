Iran / Middle East

Delta since this morning: Iran broadened retaliation to multiple Gulf neighbors today, not just Aqaba/Jordan. Wedding-strike victim details refined — all four now named. No independent verification of the strike surfaced. Full detail in state/iran.md.

Iran broadened its retaliation today, striking multiple Gulf neighbors rather than repeating the single-target Aqaba/Jordan pattern this file logged this morning. NPR: "Iran fires on its Gulf neighbors, retaliating for U.S. strikes after a monthlong lull." Read: this is objectively a wider escalation than what markets were already shrugging off at the open — and equities rallied anyway. This sharpens, rather than resolves, the morning's central open question: the market isn't just failing to catch up to Tuesday-night's escalation, it's actively re-pricing risk lower on a day the geopolitical facts got worse, not better. (NPR)

The Kuhestak/Sirik wedding-strike toll refined slightly, with all four victims now named. Hormozgan governor's office: 4 killed — two women in their 40s (Kolsoum Malahi, 43; Zarkhatoun Taheri, 43), a 4-year-old (Amir Ali Karimi), and a 16-year-old (Mohammad Malahi) — and 68 wounded. Iran's Foreign Ministry maintains the "war crime" characterization; CENTCOM has still not denied a strike occurred at that location, only that it "never targets civilians." Read: no independent (non-Iranian-state, non-US) verification has surfaced tonight either — this remains an unresolved, contested casualty claim rather than a confirmed one, but the naming of all four victims is the kind of detail that tends to harden the narrative regardless of independent verification. (Al Jazeera)

The most analytically important fact tonight is the same one flagged this morning, now with a stronger answer: markets are not pricing this escalation cycle at face value, and the gap between headline severity and market reaction is widening, not narrowing. A naive prior would have equities selling off further given today's broader multi-country retaliation; instead, they rallied on an unrelated (yields) driver. Treat the "market is desensitizing to incremental Hormuz headlines" read as the better-supported hypothesis going into tomorrow, while continuing to watch for the session where this breaks.

Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

Nothing here changes the zero-position stance — 97th consecutive session with no new positions entered. Tonight sharpens, rather than resolves, this morning's central question: markets are not just failing to catch up to escalating Iran headlines, they're actively re-pricing risk lower (equities rallied, oil eased, Fed odds fell) on a day Iran's retaliation objectively broadened to multiple Gulf neighbors. Either this cycle really has reached a desensitization point — plausible given the Larak→tanker→strike-wave→broadened-retaliation sequence has run for over a week without materially closing the strait to global flows — or markets are simply mispricing a risk that eventually reprices sharply and suddenly. On the portfolio-adjacent side: AVGO's clean operating beat (tripling semiconductor revenue, new Apple/OpenAI custom-silicon business) validates the AI-capex demand thesis directly, but the market chose to punish the soft Q4 guide instead — worth watching whether that's CXMT-competitive-thread-related or simply conservative guidance.
