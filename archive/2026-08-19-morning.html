Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since last run: A contested UAE-Iran missile incident plus a confirmed UAE trade embargo is the most material overnight development. Iran-Oman deal downgraded in scope. Bessent sanctions now a fourth consecutive session overdue.

The most consequential overnight story is also the least confirmed. UAE's Ministry of Defence says its air-defense systems intercepted a missile fired from Iranian territory over UAE airspace; Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the claim "baseless" and suggested it could be a "false-flag" operation designed to justify escalation. This file is treating the missile claim as contested, not established — no independent confirmation (satellite imagery, third-party radar, debris recovery) has surfaced. What is not contested: the UAE announced a trade and financial embargo on Iran-linked entities, the first concrete Gulf-state economic-retaliation action this cycle, as distinct from the rhetorical exchanges tracked for weeks. Whether or not the missile claim is ever verified, the embargo changes the Gulf-Iran relationship independent of it.

IRGC rhetoric hardened toward an explicitly offensive framing overnight (comments attributed to Javani and Abdollahi), landing the same night as the contested incident and the embargo — a cluster worth weighting more heavily than any single statement alone, even while none of it individually proves a policy shift.

The Iran-Oman routing deal this file has tracked as "close" for days just got a scope downgrade: Gharibabadi now describes it as only a "temporary route," not the fuller normalization agreement earlier reporting implied was near signature. Separately, Chinese shippers (COSCO, China Merchants Energy Shipping) are confirmed to have been rerouting away from Hormuz since late July — new, independent, non-Western confirmation that the physical disruption is being priced into commercial routing decisions by a major neutral shipping power.

Bessent's "unprecedented" sanctions still haven't landed — now a fourth consecutive session past the 8/14 "next week" framing, meaningfully overdue against its own timeline. Gaza saw incremental process detail (a further Kushner meeting) plus a new Israeli strike, neither a breakthrough nor a collapse.

Markets / Macro

Delta since last run: 30Y yield spiked to a fresh 19-year high then reversed sharply on a Treasury debt-buyback-doubling announcement. Gold spiked. Oil extended to a fourth up day. Equity futures split, not uniformly directional.

The standing rates story reasserted itself violently overnight. The 30-year Treasury yield spiked to a fresh 19-year high of 5.33–5.34% before Treasury announced it would double the size of its debt-buyback operations, from roughly $2B to $4B+ per operation — pulling the yield back to ~5.19%. This is a genuine, distinct policy-response story, not just background noise: it's the clearest sign yet that Treasury is actively trying to manage the long end rather than letting the AI-capex-debt/deficit story run unchecked. The whipsaw itself — a 14–15bp round trip in hours — is a volatility data point worth carrying forward independent of the level.

Gold spiked to ~$4,486–4,488.50, +3.52% (+$152.60) — the largest single-session gold move logged this cycle, consistent with the yield spike, the contested Iran/UAE incident, and broad risk-off positioning before the buyback news calmed things down.

Oil extended to a fourth consecutive up session — Brent trading $91.52–93.60 — consistent with the contested UAE-Iran incident and UAE's trade-embargo response rather than a fresh standalone catalyst.

Equity futures are genuinely mixed this morning, not a single directional read: Dow futures are firmer on the yield relief following the buyback announcement, while Nasdaq futures remain weak, dragged by the severe overnight Asia AI-chip contagion (see AI section below). BTC is roughly flat, still decoupled from the broader risk-off swings — today's White House crypto meeting (SEC Chair Atkins, CFTC Chair Selig) is the first real test of whether that pattern survives a policy-specific catalyst. Separately, the SEC's tokenization rule was delayed, attributed to a "White House mix-up" — a process note, not a substantive reversal.
