The MOU Framework

Axios reports the White House believes it is close to a one-page, 14-point memorandum of understanding to end the war. Key reported terms (Axios, CNN):

US getsIran gets
Iran moratorium on nuclear enrichmentUS lifts sanctions
HEU removed from countryFrozen funds released (billions)
Hormuz restrictions liftedHormuz restrictions lifted (mutual)
30-day nuclear negotiation windowWar ends within 30 days

What's NOT agreed: Iran's 14-point submission also demands reparations, full sanctions removal before nuclear talks, and a new Hormuz governance mechanism — all non-starters for the US in their current form. Many MOU terms are contingent on a final agreement, creating a significant off-ramp for both sides.

The structural shift: Al Jazeera analysis argues the US has implicitly accepted Iran's core demand: resolve Hormuz first, defer nuclear. Rubio declared Operation Epic Fury "concluded." The MOU defines topics for future negotiation rather than resolving them upfront — exactly Iran's position since the three-phase proposal on Apr 27. Andreas Krieg characterized this as "a concession to Tehran," noting Washington accepted that "simultaneous resolution is not currently feasible." (Al Jazeera)

48-hour clock is now ticking. The US expects Iranian responses on several key points by approximately May 8 morning. This is the single most important near-term catalyst.

IRGC Says "Safe Transit" Through Hormuz

The IRGC Navy posted a statement: "With the end of the aggressors' threats and in light of new procedures, safe and sustainable transit through the strait will be facilitated." (PressTV, NPR)

Why this matters more than the Apr 17 opening: That was Araghchi (civilian FM) unilaterally declaring the Strait open — IRGC reversed him within 24 hours. This statement comes from the IRGC Navy itself. If the IRGC is signaling cooperation, the Vahidi veto — the structural barrier we've tracked since Day 1 — may be weakening.

Bear case: "New procedures" is unspecified. Could mean toll regime continuation, escort requirements, or inspection rights — not open passage. The IRGC is leaving itself room to reverse, just as it did with Araghchi. And Hapag-Lloyd still says transits "are for the moment not possible." (NPR)

Araghchi in Beijing — The China Dimension

Araghchi met Wang Yi in Beijing on Wednesday — his first visit to China since the war began. China called for Hormuz reopening "as soon as possible" and a "complete cessation of fighting without delay." (Al Jazeera)

Key context: Trump-Xi summit in Beijing is May 14-15 — only 8 days away. Araghchi is positioning Iran diplomatically before Trump arrives: reaffirming Chinese ties, securing support, ensuring Beijing won't make concessions damaging to Iranian interests. This activates China as a third mediator alongside Pakistan and Oman. (Eurasia Review)

Trump's Carrot-and-Stick

"If they don't agree, the bombing starts, and it will be, sadly, at a much higher level and intensity than it was before." — President Trump, May 6 (NPR)
