Summit Day 1: Principles, Not Mechanisms

Trump and Xi opened a two-day summit in Beijing focused on trade, Taiwan, Iran, and AI. Per CNBC's five takeaways: they agreed on a "constructive strategic stability" framework, both agreed Iran must not have nuclear weapons and that Hormuz must remain open. Xi signaled interest in buying more US oil. But these are principles, not mechanisms. The structural question is unchanged: what does China actually do — cut Iranian oil purchases? Pressure Tehran? Or just issue statements?

Xi's delegation included a Taiwan warning: "if handled badly, the two countries risk collision or conflict." He asked whether the US and China could avoid the "Thucydides Trap." This is Xi framing the relationship as peer-to-peer, not hegemon-to-challenger — a long-term posture shift.

Congressional Constraint: The Zero-Overlap Problem

52 senators and 177 congressmen wrote to Trump on May 14: reject any deal that allows Iran to continue uranium enrichment. This is the most important under-covered story today. Iran's absolute minimum is enrichment sovereignty. Congress's absolute minimum is no enrichment. The overlap is essentially zero. Any deal Trump negotiates must either (a) satisfy Congress and lose Iran, or (b) satisfy Iran and face Senate rejection. The "ugly deal" scenario weight should be lower, and "stalemate extending" should be higher.

IEA: Undersupply Is Structural

The IEA May 2026 Oil Market Report confirms the oil market will be severely undersupplied through Q3 2026 even if the conflict ends by early June. Q2 deficit: 6 million bpd. Cumulative deficit: 900 million barrels by September. ~10.5M bpd of Gulf production remains offline. A coordinated emergency release of 400M barrels is underway, but 500M must come from industry stock draws. $100+ oil is not going away — it's now structural through at least October regardless of ceasefire timing.

H200 China: Approved But Stalled

The US Commerce Department approved ~10 Chinese companies including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, and JD.com to purchase Nvidia's H200 chips — up to 75,000 chips each. Distributors Lenovo and Foxconn also cleared. But not a single chip has been delivered. Chinese firms pulled back after Beijing guidance pushing domestic chip development (Huawei alternatives). Jensen Huang joined Trump's delegation specifically to break this logjam. He says NVDA China revenue has "dropped to zero" and estimates the China AI market at $50B/year.

Structural: The market is celebrating the approval (NVDA +10%) while ignoring the non-delivery. If Jensen can't unlock this at the summit, $0 China revenue persists into the Q1 report on May 20. The gap between what's priced (approval) and what's real (zero delivery) is dangerous ahead of earnings.

Cerebras priced at $185/share (above the $150-160 range), raising $5.55 billion — the largest tech IPO since Uber's $8B in 2019. Fully diluted valuation: $56.4 billion. Nasdaq debut today. The company previously filed in September 2024 but withdrew due to G42/UAE customer concentration concerns. First-day trading will signal appetite for AI infrastructure at these multiples.
