The carrier-missile exchange is a genuine escalation in target selection — Iran's first demonstrated willingness to directly engage a top-tier US naval asset, not just harass around it. CENTCOM says IRGC fired ballistic missiles toward a US aircraft carrier and a destroyer; both ships maneuvered/defended successfully, no hits, no US casualties. IRGC's own account, via Iranian state media, claims the opposite — that its Aerospace Force struck both vessels and forced them to withdraw from the engagement area — directly contradicting CENTCOM and unconfirmed independently either way. Read: regardless of whose battle-damage account is accurate, the targeting decision itself is the real signal. Every prior IRGC provocation this cycle targeted commercial shipping, regional bases, or unclaimed projectiles; a ballistic-missile launch at a carrier strike group shows "tanker for tanker" was never a ceiling, only a floor. (Army Recognition, PressTV)

CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper publicly announced an explicit 3-for-1 retaliation doctrine — the single most consequential new data point of the day. Cooper stated plainly: "If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost — taking out three of yours... we will not hesitate to defend American forces, and if necessary, destroy Iran's limited and exposed oil fleet." Three Iranian crude tankers were destroyed/disabled in the immediate aftermath: M/T Kylo (also called Noxen) completely destroyed in the Gulf of Oman after its crew was ordered to abandon ship; M/T Downy (off Kharg Island) and M/T Stark 1 (near Jask) both permanently disabled but left afloat. CENTCOM labeled all three tankers components of a multibillion-dollar IRGC funding network. Read: this is qualitatively different from everything logged before it. Trump's Kharg Island threat (8/31) was rhetoric that didn't convert into an actual strike on Iranian oil infrastructure — this is a serving four-star publicly codifying an asymmetric, escalatory retaliation ratio as standing doctrine, in the same breath as naming Iran's "limited and exposed" oil fleet as the target. If applied consistently, every future IRGC provocation, however small, becomes a trigger for a proportionally larger US strike on Iran's already-crippled oil-export capacity — a structurally more dangerous dynamic than the tit-for-tat pattern tracked since 9/2. Whether this holds as an actual operating rule, not just a one-time statement, is the single highest-value thing to watch this week. (RedState/CENTCOM, CNN)

IRGC claims a further six-vessel retaliatory wave on 9/6 — unconfirmed by CENTCOM, but a genuine escalation in ambition. IRGC Navy says it struck three more tankers transiting a Hormuz route it deems "unauthorized," plus three US-affiliated vessels elsewhere, warning all shipping to avoid "suspicious movement" through routes Iran hasn't sanctioned. Read: no independent confirmation found — treat as an Iranian claim only, consistent with this file's standing skepticism of unilateral IRGC statements. But the framing is notable: this moves beyond simple retaliation toward Iran attempting to functionally control which lanes of the strait are safe, worth a hard re-check for independent verification (UKMTO, shipping-insurer advisories) before the next run. (Al Jazeera, Middle East Eye)

Ghalibaf's rhetoric is harder and more senior than Aref's, and the two statements landing four days apart is a structural signal worth naming. Aref (First VP, 9/3) framed Iran's posture shift as "asymmetric, multilayered." Ghalibaf (Parliament Speaker, 9/6) went further, declaring the "era of proportionate responses is over" and promising "faster, heavier, more painful" retaliation to any future attack. Read: two of Iran's most senior civilian-facing officials — not IRGC spokesmen — hardening their public posture within the same week reads more like a coordinated regime consensus to escalate than one faction freelancing. This cuts against this file's standing pattern of "rhetoric outrunning follow-through": both sides are now converting hardened rhetoric into action within days, not weeks. Worth watching whether Ghalibaf's threat produces a demonstrated next move — the same test applied to every rhetorical escalation this cycle — but the base rate for follow-through just got worse, not better. (Al Jazeera, Business Recorder)
