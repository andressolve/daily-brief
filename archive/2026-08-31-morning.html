Iran / Middle East

Delta since Sunday morning: First direct US-Iran kinetic exchange in a month. US struck IRGC rocket launchers on Larak Island; Iran retaliated against US-linked bases in Jordan and the UAE. This supersedes the weekend's rhetorical-hardening framing entirely.

The US struck two IRGC rocket launchers on Larak Island Sunday 8/30 — CENTCOM's stated rationale is that Iran was preparing to fire rockets carrying naval mines into the Strait of Hormuz. This is the first US kinetic action against Iranian forces in roughly a month, and it moves the standoff from a rhetorical/economic-pressure phase back into an active kinetic phase. Read: the mine-laying rationale, if accurate, suggests US intelligence assessed an imminent physical threat to strait shipping — a materially higher-confidence trigger than the general "IRGC posturing" pattern logged through the weekend. Whether that assessment holds up to scrutiny in the coming days is itself a thing to watch; strikes justified by preemption claims often get contested after the fact.

Iran retaliated overnight into Monday with missile and drone strikes on US-linked bases in Jordan (King Hussein airbase, Al Azraq) and a drone attack on Al Menhad airbase in the UAE. This is Iran choosing to strike US regional partners' territory directly rather than confining retaliation to the strait itself or to rhetoric. Read: the choice of targets — a US partner in Jordan and the UAE, both outside the immediate Gulf chokepoint — signals Iran wants to demonstrate regional reach without (so far) directly hitting US forces or vessels inside the strait. That is a calibrated, not maximalist, retaliation, but it is still a materially different threat category than anything logged over the weekend's "will not yield" rhetoric.

This kills near-term momentum on the Oman-corridor diplomatic track that Rezaei's conditions-list confirmation had kept nominally alive. A live exchange of strikes makes near-term resumption of the Oman-mediated talks very unlikely — both sides now have a fresh grievance to point to, and any government wanting to justify continued hardline posture (on either side) has a ready-made pretext. Read: this doesn't necessarily mean the diplomatic track is dead — de-escalation after a "both sides get one" exchange is a common pattern — but it does mean the corridor-opening thread from last week is now stale and should not be treated as active until there's a specific signal it has resumed.

Open question for tonight: is this a contained, one-off exchange, or the start of a renewed kinetic phase? Historically these episodes can go either way — a "both sides respond, then step back" pattern versus a genuine escalation spiral. The Larak Island strike/retaliation sequence is the single highest-value thing to watch over the next 24-48 hours. No new data yet on the standing fuel-price-hike watch (Pezeshkian named 10,000 tomans/liter but no formal decree) or on China bank sanctions — both stay parked pending the kinetic situation resolving one way or the other.

Markets / Macro

Delta since Sunday morning: Oil broke its multi-week flat range on the actual strike/retaliation. Fed hike odds jumped to ~56.15% on real Monday liquidity, now above Friday's post-Warsh peak, with Iran/oil as an identifiable driver for the first time this cycle.

Brent jumped above $90/bbl (WTI ~$85-86) — the first real directional move since the Hormuz standoff began weeks ago. This breaks the multi-week flat range this file has tracked (Brent hovering ~$88 through the weekend) on an actual kinetic event rather than diplomatic rhetoric. Read: the standing "shadow-fleet keeps oil unstressed" thesis held for physical flow — no reported disruption to actual tanker traffic — but did not hold for the war-risk premium once real strikes occurred. This is worth treating as a genuine regime change in how the market is pricing Hormuz risk, not noise: rhetoric-only moves got ignored for weeks, and an actual strike moved price by ~$2/bbl within hours.

Fed September hike odds jumped to ~56.15% on real Monday liquidity, up from Sunday's 48.4% (+7.75pp) and now clearly above Friday's post-Warsh peak of 50.5%. Direct gamma-api pull this morning confirms the move. Read: the weekend chop (44-50%) this file logged as directionless drift on thin liquidity has now resolved decisively hawkish on real trading volume — and unusually, the causal driver is identifiable: Iran/oil-shock feeding inflation expectations, not a pure Fed-specific catalyst. This is the first time this cycle two genuinely separate hawkish inputs (Warsh's tone Friday, plus an actual oil shock Monday) are stacking rather than one single story driving the whole move. Worth watching whether this holds through the week or fades as a transient risk-premium spike once the geopolitical situation clarifies.

Semiconductor tariff policy: still no resolution. The Commerce Department's data-center tariff exemption report remains withheld, reportedly "complete internally" but still unpublished — now roughly two months overdue relative to its original July 1 deadline. No change from the weekend.
