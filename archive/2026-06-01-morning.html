The resignation letter: analyst inference becomes publicly-denied-by-Tehran fact

Per Iran International (May 31; secondary citations across Times of Israel, MediaLine, Townhall, IBTimes, The Week), President Pezeshkian formally submitted a letter of resignation to the Office of the Supreme Leader. Key passages, per the secondary citations: the president and his government have been "excluded from major and vital decision-making processes"; hardline IRGC factions have taken control of "key parts of the government"; Pezeshkian is "unable to run the government and carry out his legal responsibilities," and asks to "step down immediately." Iranian authorities denied it Sunday — a presidency deputy comms head dismissed it as "disreputable foreign network rumor-mongering" and said "Pezeshkian will not retreat from serving the people." Mojtaba Khamenei has not publicly acted on the letter.

Even if the letter is fabricated or leaked as leverage, the fact that Iranian authorities had to publicly deny it is itself the signal. The faction-deadlock framework migrates from analyst inference to publicly-denied-by-Tehran fact. If real and accepted by Mojtaba, the deal architecture loses its civilian counterparty. If real and rejected, Pezeshkian's open break-with-IRGC posture is the structural shift. If fake, Iran International's willingness to publish — and Tehran's need to formally deny — confirms the perception is now so widespread it requires institutional response. All three readings make the deal geometry harder, not easier.

Ghalibaf: "missiles, not dialogue"

Sunday's "tangible results before commitments" line escalated overnight. The same Speaker who is Trump's designated contact and led the Islamabad delegation:

Iran will "achieve its rights not through dialogue, but with missiles." Tehran does not trust "guarantees and words," and "will judge by actions." Parliament Speaker Mohammad-Baqer Ghalibaf, Sun–Mon May 31–Jun 1 (via Times of Israel)

The structural read: two consecutive days of public Ghalibaf hardening = the pragmatic-hawk faction, the most deal-friendly Iranian channel with formal authority, is publicly closing. The IRGC doesn't need to weigh in — the pragmatic hawks are doing the work for them.

Kinetics: 3rd round of fire, 3rd UAE attack

US destroyers (USS Truxtun, USS Mason, USS Rafael Peralta) came under "fiercer and more sustained" assault than May 7, per CBS. The UAE intercepted ballistic and cruise missiles plus drones overnight — the 3rd UAE attack since the May 4 Fujairah strike (May 4 + May 8 + Jun 1). US forces returned fire on Iranian drone/missile launch facilities. Kinetics are tracking the IRGC, not the negotiating track — Iran fired the same week Trump tightened the text.

Lebanon: first Beirut Dahieh strikes since Apr 17

A joint Netanyahu/Katz statement Monday ordered IDF strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs ("Dahiyeh") in response to Hezbollah rocket fire on northern Israel and claimed ceasefire violations — the first strikes on Beirut proper since the Apr 17 ceasefire (the May 6 Radwan-commander hit was a single targeted op; this is an ordered campaign). Hezbollah confirmed no military presence in Beaufort when the IDF entered but claimed it destroyed an Israeli tank near the castle, hit IDF forces in Bayada, and clashed in towns north of the Litani near Nabatieh. The Lebanon front has expanded from south-of-Litani land-grab → Beaufort recapture → Beirut-strike retaliation cycle. The MOU's reported "ends Israel–Hezbollah war" clause is now contradicted at three layers: territory (Beaufort), capital (Dahieh), and Hezbollah's stated posture ("weapons not negotiable").

Araghchi's Monday X post made the linkage explicit: "Any violation of this ceasefire on one front shall be considered a violation of it across all fronts." Combined with the Beaufort recapture, the Beirut strikes, and Pezeshkian's parallel Asia diplomacy, Iran is pre-positioning an exit ramp from the MOU citing Lebanon, not the tighter text.

Trump: softer rhetoric, harder text

Trump's Monday Truth Social: "Iran really wants to make a deal, and it will be a good one for the U.S.A. and those that are with us," while attacking "Dumocrats, and various seemingly unpatriotic Republicans" for "negatively chirping." The contractual text got tighter Sunday ("destroy enriched material" + "no money exchanged"); the public rhetoric got softer Monday. Same regime, two different surfaces — the cleanest two-tracking of the war. The market is being asked to price the rhetoric; the analyst weights should price the text.
