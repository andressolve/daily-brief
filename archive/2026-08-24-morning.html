Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Sunday morning: The Kerman pilot story escalated to a national fuel-liberalization signal, the rial hit a fresh record low, and Bessent hedged the China angle he's expected to confirm or deny this afternoon.

The fuel-pricing story escalated from a contained regional pilot to a national policy question overnight. Al Jazeera (8/23) reports Tehran is now signaling a nationwide fuel-price liberalization, not another Kerman-style controlled trial — a materially larger step than the single-province episode this file logged Sunday. The backdrop: 128% food inflation and 88% headline inflation year-over-year. Pezeshkian's own framing has shifted from acknowledging the blockade caused the Kerman cancellation to a broader admission of social strain: "I understand that we have many problems in society now." Read alongside Sunday's MP warnings invoking the 2019 fuel-protest precedent, this is now the file's top domestic-stability thread — a national liberalization attempt carries far higher unrest risk than a single canceled provincial pilot.

The rial hit a fresh record low of 2.02 million to the dollar on the open market as trading opened this morning (the official CBI rate remains ~1.5M, not the rate ordinary Iranians can actually access). This is the clearest single data point yet that the blockade/sanctions squeeze is compounding rather than stabilizing — it sits directly upstream of the fuel-liberalization pressure above: a government running out of forex reserves to subsidize imported gasoline is exactly the mechanism Pezeshkian already described to explain the Kerman cancellation.

Bessent's 2pm ET press conference is happening today as scheduled, but on the specific question of whether China is a named target — the sharpened angle this file logged Sunday from preview coverage — he declined to confirm on the record this morning, saying "many conversations are best to have in private." That is a real hedge against yesterday's sharper China-targeting preview framing, not a confirmation it's off the table. Treat China scope as genuinely unconfirmed until the announcement lands this afternoon; this file's standing base-rate skepticism on Bessent's dated promises (see the Hezbollah-designation precedent) applies here too.

Polymarket: Hormuz rose a third time this week, +3.0pp to 34.5%. Unlike the prior two unexplained flip-flops (31.5%→29.5%→31.5%) logged Saturday and Sunday, this move has a plausible catalyst: pre-positioning ahead of today's 2pm ET announcement, not a reaction to news that already happened. Worth checking the contract's post-announcement level against today's 34.5% pre-positioning baseline this evening.

No material change: no fresh kinetic incident at Hormuz; Gaza's "next phase" track unchanged; UAE-Iran missile claim remains unresolved; the Oman trade-agreement thread (finalized 8/21) carries forward unchanged.
