Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since this morning: both fronts flagged this morning escalated further by the close, and the diplomatic track still has no resolution.

Iran now claims four tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz — not the two "halted" reported this morning — including the first confirmed successful limpet-mine attack of the war: a small boat attached mines to a tanker's hull, disabling it via an engine-room fire (CBS News). US Central Command, under Admiral Brad Cooper, disputes the severity, saying it has cleared the main shipping lanes of IRGC-laid mines; Iran disputes that claim in turn (Gulf News, CNBC). This is now a direct, unresolved factual dispute between the two sides rather than differing framing of the same facts — and it's a harder data point than this morning's "incidents" language.

Iran still has not formally responded to Vance's letter as of Friday evening. Qatari mediators remain in Tehran, with no reported breakthrough or collapse since this morning's "indications of progress" framing. This is the single most decision-relevant item outstanding heading into the weekend — both this morning's and tonight's reads agree on that.

The Houthi/Red Sea front widened rather than staying contained, which this morning's brief flagged as the thing to watch. Houthi-affiliated media claim a drone was intercepted near the Mecca region (Houthi forces deny targeting Mecca specifically); Saudi coalition forces struck al-Jawf, Marib, and Taiz governorates in Yemen; and Houthi forces claim further strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure and airports (Jerusalem Post).

The structural read

Oil's reaction to tonight's mine-strike claims was notably smaller than the claims' severity alone would suggest — Brent and WTI faded off their intraday highs into the close even as the Hormuz story got worse on paper (markets.md). Bear case: that muted reaction may just reflect the competing CENTCOM claim giving traders a reason to discount Iran's number, not genuine de-escalation — worth distinguishing a calmer tape from a calmer reality. Bull case: a market that shrugged off a claimed four-tanker mine attack is itself informative — if CENTCOM's "lanes cleared" claim is closer to the operational truth, today's muted oil reaction may be the more accurate read than Iran's own claim. Either way, the still-unanswered Vance letter means this file has no fresh diplomatic signal to weigh against the operational dispute — only the operational dispute itself.
