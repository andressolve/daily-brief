The structural read: restraint under rhetoric

Delta since this morning Trump named Kharg Island's oil infrastructure as a target; CENTCOM struck the island hard but left the oil terminals untouched. The gap between threat and execution is the story.

Trump named Kharg Island's oil infrastructure explicitly as a target at the NATO summit. CENTCOM struck the island hard overnight — 90+ targets, mine storage, missile bunkers, IRGC naval assets — but by all accounts available tonight, left the oil terminals and storage tanks untouched. That gap between rhetoric and execution is more informative than the rhetoric itself. Two readings: either CENTCOM is deliberately calibrating below the oil-infrastructure threshold, demonstrating reach without triggering the supply-shock consequences of actually hitting the terminal, or this is graduated signaling — a shot across the bow ahead of a further threat if Iran doesn't stand down.

Iran's own response leans toward taking the threat seriously either way: its Armed Forces threatened to turn US-linked firms' oil and energy infrastructure to "ashes" if Iran's own oil facilities are hit, and named UAE ports and cities as retaliation targets — reprising the pattern from May where UAE absorbs retaliation for hosting US basing and overflight access (NBC News). Iran retaliated again against Bahrain and Kuwait overnight; Kuwait intercepted 2 ballistic missiles and 13 drones.

Bear case: restraint today doesn't preclude escalation tomorrow, and Trump's rhetoric sets a public marker that's now hard to walk back from without eventually hitting it.
