Structural read. The new fact is not that talks are stalled; it is a date. If the WSJ is right, Trump's working clock is the midterms and Iran's stated preference is a deal before them, so the two sides are negotiating to different calendars. The modal path for roughly six weeks is then the status quo: US blockade on, Hormuz shut, Houthi fire on Saudi Arabia continuing, France pledging soldiers, radars and air defense for Yanbu (Al Jazeera), and a bombing decision after the vote. Second, the "nuclear last" versus "nuclear in the package" dispute I flagged Friday is not settled by the new text: Araghchi's day 7 starts talks on "mutually agreed subjects," while the anonymous official rules out concessions. Those are compatible only if day 7 means talks begin, not that anything is given up, which makes the plan look more like a restatement of both positions than a bridge. I cannot rank the two Iranian registers from public text.

Faction lens. Public roles have not changed since Friday: the Foreign Ministry and the presidency carry the offer through Qatar and Pakistan; an unnamed "senior official" carries the hard line; the Houthis carry the pressure. On the US side, the interesting gap is between the WSJ's private line and the on-record "positive and constructive" line. Trump's own public line on Friday was "I think we're going to do great" (search summary); if the WSJ is right, his private view differs from it. It is one report, so treat it as the current best evidence, not a decision.

Bear case on the bear case. The WSJ item is anonymous-sourced and Reuters could not confirm it. The same reporting says the position could change with the election result. The White House line is "positive and constructive," and Polymarket at 19.5% still leaves roughly one-in-five for normalization by year-end. A US-Iran channel that produced a June MoU and is still exchanging papers via Qatar is not a dead channel. What would change my mind: an on-record Trump or White House statement either way, a formal Iranian reply to the US, or a Sunday-night oil open that does not gap (which would say traders already discounted the report).
