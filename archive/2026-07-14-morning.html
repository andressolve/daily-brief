Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night The blockade and toll took effect on schedule and are now confirmed, not disputed. Overnight escalation (UAE tanker strikes, ~90 US strike targets) is the largest single-night kinetic exchange logged this cycle.

This is the first confirmed — not contested — hard-policy escalation of the entire 5-month monitoring cycle. CENTCOM confirmed the naval blockade and 20% non-Iranian cargo toll went into force as scheduled at 4pm ET yesterday, resolving last night's "not yet in force" flag with a clean enforcement confirmation. Overnight, Iran struck two UAE-flagged tankers (one fatality reported), and the US struck roughly 90 Iranian military targets in response — a materially larger single-night exchange than anything logged so far this cycle, and the clearest evidence yet that the military and toll/blockade tracks are running in parallel escalation, not one substituting for the other.

Oil moved to the highest sustained level of the entire 5-month cycle — Brent ~$86-87, WTI ~$80.75 — distinct from prior single-day spikes that faded within 1-2 sessions. Still ~$3-9 below the $90-95 P008 watch band and ~$23-24 below the $110 sustained trigger, but this is the closest sustained approach to the watch band yet.

Structural read Al Udeid damage confirmation remains unresolved for a second day, and no direct update was found on Araghchi-Oman Muscat follow-through, though yesterday's "rhetorical judo" framing (Iran as Hormuz's "eternal guardian," the 20% toll "too much," Iran will "be fair") stands as his most recent word. The new cargo toll itself is a genuinely new variable worth tracking going forward — a standing shipping-cost mechanism, distinct from a price spike, not just an oil-price proxy.

Markets / Macro

Delta since last night A cool June CPI print produced a larger, opposite-direction Fed-odds swing than last night's blockade-driven move — the disinflation data won decisively.

The CPI surprise, not the overnight oil escalation, is this morning's real story. June CPI printed well below consensus on both readings: headline -0.4% m/m / 3.5% YoY (vs. 3.8% expected), core flat m/m / 2.6% YoY (vs. 2.8% expected). Fed odds snapped back hard — no-change now 93.5%, hike 6.35% — not just reversing last night's ~11-point hawkish swing (63.5% no-change) but pushing above the pre-blockade baseline (74.5%), meaning the market now reads the CPI surprise as decisively outweighing the oil/blockade inflation-shock fear, at least for the July meeting.

Fed Chair Warsh's first congressional testimony (House, 10am ET yesterday) gave no explicit rate-path signal tying the blockade/toll to policy — a genuine non-event relative to how loaded the setup looked last night. Treasury 10Y sits at 4.579%, resolving last night's data-gap flag. BTC ~$62.5-62.9K. Oil stays elevated on the confirmed Hormuz enforcement plus overnight strikes (see Iran section): Brent ~$86-87, WTI ~$80-80.75 — the highest sustained level of the cycle.

Structural read Two large, opposite-direction macro shocks landed less than a day apart, and the disinflation data won decisively — the CPI-driven reversal (~30 points) is larger than the blockade-driven swing that preceded it (~11 points). The market isn't yet treating the confirmed blockade/strikes as a durable inflation problem the way it briefly did last night; whether that changes if oil pushes toward the $90-95 watch band is the open question.
