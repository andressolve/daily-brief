Iran / Middle East

Delta since Sunday morning: Iran named a formal "restricted zone" outside Hormuz and its Parliament Speaker threatened US-linked energy assets directly; no US response yet, no new strikes, and IRGC's six-vessel claim remains unverified. Full detail in state/iran.md.

Rezaei's restricted zone is the most concrete Iranian escalation of the window — an attempted claim of control over lanes of the strait, not another symbolic threat. SNSC Secretary Mohsen Rezaei announced a zone extending from the US naval blockade line, through the strait, into the Gulf beyond — any vessel transiting without coordinating with Iran risks being placed on an Iranian sanctions list, a direct threat to insurance coverage and future access. Read: this is the doctrinal formalization of the "unauthorized route" enforcement ambition IRGC gestured at with its unverified 9/6 six-vessel claim. Whether Iran has the naval capacity to actually enforce this against US-escorted commercial shipping is genuinely contested — Al Jazeera's own explainer treats enforceability as an open question — but a senior official naming a specific zone is a real declaration of intent, and the gap between that intent and demonstrated capability is the thing to watch. (Washington Times, The National, Al Jazeera)

Ghalibaf's threat against US energy companies, paired with Washington's continued silence, is the sharper of the two new data points. Naming "American oil and gas companies" and their regional facilities as exposed to retaliation for any US strike on Iranian energy assets directly answers Cooper's 3-for-1 doctrine (9/5-6), which named Iran's oil fleet as a target — Tehran is now naming a mirror-image target class of its own. Read: no US government response (Trump, Vance, CENTCOM, State) has surfaced to either this threat or the restricted-zone declaration as of this morning, roughly 48 hours after Cooper's speech. Given how quickly both sides have been converting hardened rhetoric into named doctrine over the past week, this silence is worth treating as an open gap rather than an assumed de-escalation — a natural point for either side to respond is Tuesday's first weekday news cycle. (GlobalSecurity, Iran International)

No new US strike tested the 3-for-1 doctrine, and IRGC's claimed 9/6 six-vessel strike wave remains wholly unverified. No CENTCOM confirmation, denial, or independent (UKMTO, shipping-insurer) corroboration has surfaced for the claim that Iran struck three tankers on an "unauthorized route" plus three US-affiliated vessels — this stays a one-sided Iranian state media claim, consistent with this file's standing skepticism of unilateral IRGC statements. Separately, Oman completed evacuation of 16 crew from the Sidr tanker — closing out the 9/1 incident, operational follow-through rather than new diplomacy. The rial continued sliding to ~2.243-2.267M/USD, from 9/5's ~2.216M.

Nothing here changes the zero-position stance — 103rd consecutive session with no new positions entered. But the structural picture keeps hardening on both sides: Iran has now named a formal enforcement zone and threatened US energy assets directly, the US has named Iran's oil fleet as a retaliation target, and neither side has yet backed off or followed through with a second strike — a stable-but-armed standoff, not a de-escalation.
