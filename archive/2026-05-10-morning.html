Iran's Response: Headline Constructive, Substance TBD

IRNA confirmed Iran transmitted its response to the US peace proposal through mediator Pakistan on May 10. The response focuses on: (a) ending the war on all fronts, especially Lebanon; (b) safety of shipping through Hormuz. The nuclear program was not mentioned in reporting — consistent with Iran's demand for Hormuz-first, nuclear-later sequencing that the US has previously rejected.

Why this matters structurally: Breaking silence after five days signals the diplomatic channel is alive. Iran chose to engage before Trump-Xi (4 days away) rather than letting China negotiate on its behalf. But deferring nuclear is the tell — if this is another "solve Hormuz first" counter-proposal, the US will likely reject or counter, and the Polymarket bounce reverses.

US officials are cautious. Energy Sec Wright: awaiting "clear resolution." UN Ambassador Waltz: talks proceeding "longer and slower than anyone would like." NBC intelligence assessment: Iran can withstand the blockade for months without economic collapse — complicating the pressure timeline.

First LNG Through Hormuz: The PGSA in Practice

The Al Kharaitiyat (Nakilat Shipping Qatar) departed Ras Laffan and successfully transited Hormuz to Port Qasim, Pakistan. It used a "northern route" hugging the Iranian coastline — a corridor designated and regulated by the IRGC. Iran granted specific approval as a trust-building gesture with Pakistan, which is mediating the peace talks.

Pre-war: 3 LNG shipments/day through Hormuz. Today: 1 authorized transit. This is the Goldman "sloppy peace" made physical — Iran demonstrating it can selectively open the Strait as sovereign toll-keeper. One tanker is not normalization, but it proves the mechanism works.

Qatar's PM warned Araghchi that using Hormuz as a "pressure card" only deepens the crisis. Regional pushback on Iran's transit leverage is growing.

IRGC Escalation Warning

The IRGC stated any attack on Iranian oil tankers and commercial vessels would trigger "assaults on US bases and enemy ships" throughout the region. This is the sharpest IRGC deterrence language since the May 7 port strikes. It is conditional — designed specifically to deter the pattern of US tanker disabling (Sea Star III, Sevda) that has intensified over the past week.

Lebanon: Ceasefire Dying, Expiry in 7 Days

Israeli strikes killed 39+ across Lebanon on May 9-10 despite the ceasefire, including an attack on al-Saksakieh (Sidon district) that killed 7 including a child. The ceasefire expires May 17. Iran's response explicitly mentioned Lebanon as a front requiring resolution — but Israel's escalation is destroying the foundation for any Lebanon guarantee in the MOU.
