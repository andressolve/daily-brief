Hormuz: from fee-jawboning bluff to actual kinetic attack

A Qatari state-owned LNG carrier ("Al Rekayyat") was struck by a projectile while exiting the Strait near the Omani coast and caught fire; crew safe. A second vessel sustained structural damage, no injuries. UKMTO confirmed both incidents. CNN and NPR report Iran's IRGC "reportedly fired at least two missiles" at commercial ships (NPR; CNN).

Iran's posture is deflection, not claim. State TV said the tanker "came under attack after ignoring warnings" — implying the vessel was at fault, not admitting the strike. Foreign Minister Araghchi invoked paragraph 13 of the US-Iran MoU (the safe-passage obligation) and warned that final-deal negotiations "will not commence if threats continue" — recasting Iran as the aggrieved party responding to US "threats," not the actor (Al Jazeera). President Pezeshkian posted that Iran remains committed to the MoU "provided Washington upholds its obligations — mutual understanding is a two-way street." Both statements preserve deal-space rhetoric even as an actual attack occurred — a two-track signal consistent with the framework's ongoing read of Iranian factional messaging: hardline action, deal-preserving language.

Qatar — a Gulf partner, not Israel — is the direct accuser, with its Foreign Ministry calling it an "unacceptable attack" and a "serious and explicit violation of international law," holding Iran "fully legally responsible." That broadens this beyond the Iran-Israel dyad for the first time this cycle. Netanyahu reportedly said Israel will ensure "freedom of maritime trade" — the first explicit Israeli signal of readiness to intervene on shipping security.

Framework read: this upgrades the Hormuz thread from "fee-formalization bluff, no bid emerged, confirmed through Monday close" to an actual kinetic incident against commercial shipping, with responsibility deflected but not denied.
