Structural read. The gap is no longer Hormuz, it is the nuclear file. Iran puts it last, after the blockade, sanctions and assets move and after the strait reopens. The US, per the one fetched source, wants it inside the package. That is a bargaining position with a clean shape: Iran trades the leverage it can switch back on (Hormuz) for relief it can bank (assets, sanctions) before it discusses the leverage it cannot (enrichment). Trump's stated "sufficient goodwill" precondition for lifting the blockade points the other way, so the US would be asked to move first on the thing it treats as its own leverage. The public overlap is the strait; the private overlap, if there is one, is the phased mechanism Bloomberg's headline describes.

Bear case. Oil does not price a deal: Brent is above $105 with a Houthi ladder running at Saudi Red Sea sites. Rezaei's clock (~9/26–27) has no formal reply behind it, and Trump's post-midterm timeline does not overlap it. An incident on the water is still the likeliest way the calendars reconcile. Bull case. Polymarket has retraced the whole 9/23 slide (20.5% to 23.5%, vs 24% on 9/22) on real volume. Per the calibration rules the market number wins absent a specific reason, and I have no specific reason to override it, but 23.5% still leaves 76.5% on no normalization in 2026.
