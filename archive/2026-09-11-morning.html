The highest-value item in this file today is a coverage gap, not a new event — and it should reframe how much weight Andres puts on Trump's own public timeline claims going forward. WSJ (exclusive, dated 9/9, still circulating) reports VP Vance and SecState Rubio privately warned Trump in the Oval Office and Situation Room that the war could drag on through the end of his term — January 2029. That is a direct, sourced contradiction of Trump's own public line, logged in this file as recently as 9/10 morning, that he expects the war to end "immediately after" the November midterms because Iran "can't hold out any longer." A president publicly promising a near-term end while his own VP and Secretary of State privately warn it could run three more years is not a minor gap — the WSJ piece itself frames the public messaging against a backdrop of rising domestic anger over living costs tied to the war ahead of midterms. This directly validates, rather than just extends, this file's standing practice of treating Trump's war-timeline comments as political messaging, not intelligence.

Second, a genuine widening of the war this file had not previously logged: Houthi forces struck multiple Saudi Aramco-linked facilities on 9/8 — Abha, Najran, the Jizan Economic City complex, and Khamis Mushait airbase — with "dozens" of missiles and drones, wounding 73 and halting some Saudi energy operations. This is a Saudi-Houthi front distinct from both the Hormuz shipping campaign and the earlier Kuwait/Bahrain strikes, and it is a real contributor to this week's oil risk premium that this file missed for three days. Flagging plainly, consistent with this file's practice of naming its own coverage gaps rather than smoothing them over (the private-credit.md Loparex miss is the closest recent precedent).

Separately, US Energy Secretary Chris Wright delivered the hardest implicit regime-change language logged yet from a cabinet official, telling CNN the US will keep pressuring Iran "until it changes its position or its government changes." This still isn't a formal response to Rezaei's restricted-zone declaration itself — Washington's silence on that specific question now runs six-plus days. No Pickaxe Mountain strike has occurred; Trump's hardened rhetoric on that front remains unconverted to action.

Bear case: nothing today points toward near-term resolution. The WSJ story, if accurate, means the administration's own internal timeline is measured in years, not months — a structurally worse read than Trump's public messaging implies, and one more reason to discount his midterm-timeline claim specifically rather than take it as a forecast.
