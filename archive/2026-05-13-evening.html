Warsh Confirmed as Fed Chair

The Senate confirmed Warsh 54-45 — the closest margin for a Fed chair in the modern era. Only Democrat Fetterman crossed over. Warsh takes the chair Friday when Powell's term expires. His first FOMC meeting is June 16-17. (CNBC, NPR)

Structural read: Warsh inherits the worst inflation backdrop since Volcker (CPI 3.8% + PPI 6.0%) and a president demanding rate cuts. The 54-45 margin means minimal political capital. His credibility test comes at the June FOMC: hold (safe), cut (political), or hike (economically correct but politically suicidal). Market rallying today suggests traders expect the Warsh put — they think he'll be dovish. That may be wrong.

Iran / Geopolitics Trump Downplays

Delta morning → evening: Trump explicitly downplayed Iran at summit ("don't need help"). Iran VP Aref: "right to Hormuz established, closed." Pentagon: war cost $29B. Hapag-Lloyd: $50-60M/week Hormuz costs.

Trump: "We Don't Need Help With Iran"

In pre-summit remarks, Trump stated: "I don't think we need any help with Iran. We have Iran very much under control. We'll win it one way or the other." This directly contradicts the morning thesis that Trump would press Xi to use China's economic leverage over Tehran. (CNBC, Al Jazeera)

Three reads on the downplay:

  1. Negotiation posture — don't show weakness by asking for help. Classic Trump.
  2. Genuine belief — military solution is working, deal not needed.
  3. Lowering expectations — if a deal surfaces Thu-Fri, it looks like a surprise win.

The simplest read: he's not playing the China card on Iran. If Thu-Fri meetings produce nothing on Iran, the deal mechanism becomes unclear and escalation risk increases.

Iran Hardening Further

First Vice President Aref: "Our right to the Strait of Hormuz is established, and the matter is closed." Combined with Gharibabadi's morning rhetoric, both the pragmatic and hardline camps are rejecting concessions into the summit. (Al Jazeera)

War Economics

Pentagon's acting Comptroller told Congress the war has cost $29 billion so far (up from $25B last month). Doesn't include infrastructure damage repair. Separately, Hapag-Lloyd disclosed $50-60M/week in extra costs from Hormuz disruption, with CEO expecting freight rates to rise further. This is the physical supply-chain pipeline feeding PPI/CPI. (Euronews, Defense News)
