Iran / Middle East

Delta since 7/12 morning Both weekend claims (Hormuz closed, Al Udeid destroyed) are now contested. Corrections on Mojtaba Khamenei's timeline and Trump's own WSJ quote. Major structural correction: the diplomatic channel is active, not dead.

Hormuz — a framing fight, not a walk-back. CENTCOM is now explicitly disputing Iran's "closed until further notice" declaration, asserting the strait remains legally open and navigable. This doesn't contradict the underlying data: Bloomberg/Windward AIS tracking shows vessels running dark (transponders off) now outnumber tracked transiting vessels for a third straight day — meaning the practical shipping collapse documented Sunday (35→17→11 daily transits) is real and likely understated, even as CENTCOM's "open" claim is a legal/freedom-of-navigation assertion rather than a factual rebuttal. Read this as "IRGC-declared-closed vs. US-declared-open, both technically defensible, actual traffic near-zero" — not as Iran backing down.

Al Udeid remains disputed. Iran claims a new, separate July 12 strike destroyed a command-and-control center and maintenance facility at the base; CENTCOM's press release claims a successful defense with no damage. No independent imagery has surfaced from either side.

Two corrections: Mojtaba Khamenei's July 11 statement, logged Sunday as his "first" public statement, was not in fact his debut — he has documented statements from March and late May 2026. It should be understood as his first vengeance-framed statement, not his first public words. Separately, a new WSJ quote has Trump saying "Israel came up with nothing" on the assassination-plot intelligence cited to help justify this round of strikes — a real, on-record crack in the US's own stated rationale.

Structural read — a genuine correction to Sunday's framing Iran's FM Araghchi met Oman's FM in Muscat on Saturday, July 11, specifically on Hormuz "safe passage mechanisms," under a mid-June 14-point US-Iran MOU that established a 60-day negotiating window — this was happening in parallel with, not superseded by, the weekend's strikes and retaliation. Sunday's "diplomatic and military tracks are now fully decoupled" read overstated the case. The better model: three tracks inside Iran — diplomatic (Araghchi-Oman, still working the MOU's safe-passage mechanism), military (IRGC acting unilaterally on Hormuz enforcement and Gulf-state retaliation), and throne (Mojtaba, using vengeance rhetoric as political cover rather than operational command). The US side shows a mirrored duality — Trump's public rhetoric running alongside CENTCOM and negotiators' continued technical engagement. The right question is no longer "has diplomacy failed" but "which of Iran's three tracks is actually driving events on a given day."

Bottom Line

Today is a lesson in not letting one dominant headline crowd out a second, unrelated one: the oil gap-up everyone was watching for arrived on schedule, but it wasn't actually what moved US equities most — a Korean brokerage's estimate cut on SK Hynix shipment timing did more damage to the tape than the war did. Meanwhile, the two loudest Iran claims from the weekend (Hormuz closed, Al Udeid destroyed) both turned out to be contested rather than confirmed, and the "diplomatic channel is dead" read from Sunday was simply wrong — Iran's foreign ministry was in Muscat working a safe-passage mechanism the same weekend its military was retaliating across five countries. The calibration lesson twice over: verify claims before they harden into narrative, and don't assume the biggest story of the weekend is the one that actually moves markets on Monday.
