Iran / Middle East

Delta since this morning Toll reversed under Gulf pressure; blockade holds; Rubio's opposition surfaces as a new factional detail; Al Udeid unresolved for a third day.

The toll proved highly reversible; the blockade did not. Trump reversed the 20% Hormuz cargo toll roughly a day after announcing it, replacing it with unspecified "Trade and Investment Deals" from Gulf states, after UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, and Bahrain pushed back. Secretary of State Rubio had been vocally opposed to the toll on international-law grounds — the first daylight this cycle between State and the White House on a Hormuz-policy specific, not just tone. The naval blockade itself remains in force; only the toll was withdrawn.

This qualifies, rather than reverses, this morning's "first confirmed hard-policy escalation" read: the military track (blockade + UAE tanker strikes + ~90 US strike targets) held all day with no walk-back, while the newer, more novel economic-policy prong turned out to be a 24-hour policy — consistent with this cycle's standing pattern of rhetorical/policy whiplash outrunning the more stable military track.

Structural read Al Udeid damage confirmation remains unresolved for a third day — no update found either direction. No further Araghchi-Muscat follow-through found this evening. Worth watching tomorrow whether Iran reads the toll reversal as a concession or treats it as irrelevant given the blockade holds.
