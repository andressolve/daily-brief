Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night: Supersedes the "quiet, no Kharg strike" framing entirely. A full US strike wave landed overnight with a stated preemption rationale (mine-laying, undersea cables), Iran alleges civilian deaths at a wedding and calls it a war crime, Iran retaliated on US bases, and the rial hit a fresh record. Full detail in state/iran.md.

CENTCOM completed a full "wave of strikes" (~6:50pm EDT, 9/1) against Iranian air-defense sites, radar systems, naval assets/mine-laying capabilities, and communications sites — explicitly framed as follow-on to "attempted attacks by the IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and American service members deployed to the region," directly tying the strikes to the Khasab-area tanker attacks this file logged 9/1. CENTCOM footage reportedly showed strikes on 16 Iranian minelayers; a US source (via Al Arabiya) adds that the operation also targeted an active plot against undersea cables in the strait. Read: this is a materially harder threat characterization than anything CENTCOM has offered since the 8/30 Larak strike — if accurate, Iran was moving from harassment tactics toward actually contesting physical control of the strait, a real escalation in Iranian intent, not just US posture. (Tribune India, Gulf News)

Iran claims a US strike killed civilians at a wedding — a war-crime allegation with named victims, a first for this cycle. Iranian officials say a strike hit a house during a wedding ceremony in Kuhestak, Sirik County, Hormozgan province; the Iranian Red Crescent initially reported 4 killed (including a child) and 50+ injured, while Hormozgan's deputy governor later put the toll at 5 killed, 68 injured. The governor's office named four victims, including a 4-year-old (Amir Ali Karimi) and two women in their 40s. Iran's Foreign Ministry called it "barbaric war crimes." CENTCOM's response — "we're aware of the reports... the US military never targets civilians" — denies intent, not that a strike occurred at that location or that deaths resulted. Read: every prior civilian-facing casualty claim this cycle has been contested/unclaimed-projectile or infrastructure-focused; a named-victims wedding-strike allegation with a child among the dead is the kind of incident that tends to harden domestic Iranian opinion behind the regime even as it deepens the war's human cost, and gives Tehran a concrete, sympathetic international-messaging asset independent of its own repression record. Worth watching for independent (non-Iranian-state, non-US) verification. (Al Jazeera, Washington Examiner)

Iran retaliated with rocket/drone attacks on US bases, including reported explosions over Aqaba, Jordan; the IRGC threatens "heavier, more widespread, and devastating responses." Iran is reusing the same retaliatory playbook (strike US-linked bases in partner states) from the 8/30 Larak-retaliation cycle rather than escalating to a new target class — a mild note of continuity inside an otherwise sharper cycle. The IRGC's rhetoric is the most explicitly escalatory of the cycle so far, though this file's standing pattern (rhetoric outrunning follow-through) has held more often than not. (NCRI)

Rial hit a fresh record, breaking above 2.1M/USD on the free market — Iran's central bank governor called the latest leg "psychological" rather than fundamentals-driven. The rial has now lost roughly 63% of its value since March 2026. Read: a governor calling a currency collapse "psychological" reads as an attempt to decouple the currency move from the government's own war/sanctions policy choices, not a substantive explanation. (Euronews)

The most analytically important fact this morning is what didn't happen: markets are not pricing this escalation with anything like yesterday's intensity. A strike wave this specific — named target set, undersea-cable plot, civilian-casualty allegation with named victims — would, on a naive prior, be expected to move markets at least as much as yesterday's tanker-strike-driven risk-off close. It hasn't, at least not yet. See Markets/Polymarket below for the full cross-asset read.

Read: strategic logic unchanged — NVIDIA reaching for the open-model/open-dataset distribution layer as a hedge against custom-silicon substitution risk. Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

Nothing here changes the zero-position stance — 97th consecutive session with no new positions entered. The genuinely interesting fact this morning isn't the escalation itself (this cycle has produced several), it's the market's non-reaction to it: a strike wave with a harder threat characterization (mine-laying, undersea cables) and a first-of-cycle named-victims civilian-casualty allegation produced a smaller market move than yesterday's tanker-strike session. Either markets are genuinely desensitizing to incremental Hormuz headlines — plausible given the Larak→tanker→strike-wave sequence has run for a week without closing the strait to global flows — or this morning's read is simply premature and the reaction catches up by the close. That question, not the Fed-odds gap or Broadcom's print, is the one worth resolving first. On the Fed: today's soft ADP/ISM pair is the first real dovish counterweight to a five-session hawkish drift, and the 11-point gap between Polymarket's direct pull (~57%) and the cited CME FedWatch figure (~68%) is unusual enough to flag rather than paper over — trust the direct pull per this file's own rule, but don't treat the question as closed. Broadcom's print tonight is a genuine dual catalyst: the operating numbers test the AI-semis demand thesis directly, and any comment on the reported $60-100B Apollo/Blackstone financing talks would be the first hyperscaler-adjacent confirmation of a private-credit-to-AI-capex linkage of real size. Private credit itself remains the one beat that should be showing lagged stress from a week like this one and still isn't.
