Iran Widens the Map, Markets Shrug the Oil Leg

Jordan strike opens a new front while WTI fades a third straight session — the market is pricing supply-return, not risk-off
July 10, 2026 — Morning Brief · Run: ~08:00 PT · Day 133 of Iran war

TL;DR — What changed and why it matters

  • Iran struck Jordan for the first time this war — 10 missiles at the Azraq military base (8 intercepted), a materially different target than the Gulf-state bases hit so far: Jordan is a moderate Arab state with its own peace treaty with Israel, so this widens the war's political aperture, not just its map.
  • Times of Israel: Israel is willing to join US strikes on Iran, awaiting Trump's go-ahead — single-outlet sourcing, but paired with the Jordan strike it's the most escalatory data point pairing of the week.
  • Oil keeps fading despite the escalation — WTI $71.33 (-1.1%), Brent ~$75.56 (-1%), third straight declining session even as the war's map expands. The market is treating this as an eventual-supply-return signal, not a risk-on trigger.
  • The Meta "Iris" chip story that moved markets Thursday is 87 days old — it's the MTIA generation Broadcom already disclosed 4/14/26 getting a production-start date, not new NVIDIA-displacement evidence. Old fact, new market reaction.
  • Polymarket's new Hormuz-traffic markets confirm "gradual reopening" is priced as almost entirely rhetorical — "normal by July 15" prices 0.65% Yes, "by July 31" prices 4.5% Yes. Israel-Iran permanent peace by July 31 collapsed to 1.55% Yes (from ~12% in late June).

ABC News, Al Jazeera.

The Israel-may-join-strikes signal and the Jordan strike are the two most escalatory data points of the week, landing the same day the diplomatic channel is trying to prove it still works. Bear case: the "sloppy peace" pattern (simultaneous fighting and dealing) this file has tracked since June may finally be tipping toward fighting — Polymarket's Israel-Iran permanent-peace collapse (below) is independent confirmation.

Bottom Line

The war just got geographically and politically wider — a strike on Jordan, a possible Israeli re-entry into direct strikes — and the market's answer was to sell oil for a third straight session. That is not complacency; it is a specific, calibrated bet that this escalation resolves toward an eventual Iran-supply-return outcome rather than a sustained Hormuz interdiction, a bet Polymarket's new near-zero Hormuz-normalization markets and its collapsing Israel-Iran peace odds both corroborate from different directions — one says traffic won't normalize, the other says the war won't end, and oil is pricing the former as the dominant near-term variable.
