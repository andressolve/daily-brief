Two more tankers disabled — blockade tightening

An F/A-18 Super Hornet from USS George H.W. Bush fired precision munitions at the smokestacks of M/T Sea Star III and M/T Sevda, disabling both before they could reach an Iranian port. Earlier in the week, a US jet shot out the rudder of another tanker. Blockade enforcement is escalating in real-time — the US is now physically disabling Iranian vessels while nominally maintaining a ceasefire.

Iran counter-seized a tanker in the Gulf of Oman, citing "attempts to disrupt its oil exports." The tit-for-tat dynamic is now routine: US disables, Iran seizes.

Araghchi's condemnation — the diplomatic anti-signal

"Every time a diplomatic solution is on the table, the U.S. opts for a reckless military adventure." This is not the language of a counterparty about to accept an MOU. Combined with the missed Rubio deadline and continued kinetics, the diplomatic track weakened materially during the session.

Structural read: Iran is positioning itself as the aggrieved party whose diplomatic overtures keep being interrupted by US military action. Whether genuine frustration or strategic framing for rejection, the practical effect is identical: no momentum toward a deal.

Trump-Xi: the new forcing function

CNBC reports Iran is expected to dominate the May 14-15 summit agenda, potentially at the expense of tariff and rare earth discussions. Trump wants a deal before landing in Beijing. China hosted Araghchi on May 6. The question is whether Trump's summit deadline creates enough pressure on Iran through China, or whether Iran plays the clock.
