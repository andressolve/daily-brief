Iran / Middle East

Delta since Saturday morning: Netanyahu formally rejected the Gaza roadmap and co-opted the hawks rather than facing a coalition revolt; a Pentagon munitions-depletion story surfaced; IRGC media narrated (without denying) the ADNOC attack and issued a formal blockade/withdrawal/end-of-war demand list; Polymarket's Hormuz contract finally moved.

The Gaza roadmap fight resolved Sunday — in the hawks' direction, but not via the mechanism flagged Friday. Netanyahu told Sunday's cabinet: "Israel rejects the 15-point document." No IDF withdrawal until "genuine, not fictitious" Hamas disarmament; no Palestinian state "as long as I am prime minister." Smotrich, who was demanding a cabinet vote to formally block the roadmap as of Friday, instead praised Netanyahu's "unequivocal clarification" Sunday — a reversal from confrontation to endorsement. Read: the PM absorbed the hawks' position directly rather than facing a formal revolt. That removes the coalition-fracture threat to Netanyahu's own authority while simultaneously hardening Israel's break from the US-brokered framework itself. No confirmed Trump administration reaction has surfaced yet — the open question for the week is whether Washington treats this as a rupture or lets it pass.

A new structural thread worth tracking independently of the diplomatic fight: US military capacity is straining, not just Iranian capacity. The Pentagon has given defense contractors 21 days to submit accelerated production plans. US munitions stockpiles are reported down an estimated 65% since the war began; THAAD interceptors remain below pre-war levels despite a partial rebuild; Army long-range precision munitions are described as "virtually" exhausted. This is a plausible non-diplomatic reason for Washington to want de-escalation that has nothing to do with Iran's internal coherence or Israel's negotiating posture — a supply-side constraint on the war's continuation that the faction-lens analysis doesn't capture on its own.

Iran's IRGC media didn't deny the ADNOC attack — it narrated it (Sepah News: the tanker "currently burning in the US-supported shipping route") — a de facto ownership without a formal claim of responsibility, consistent with the pattern of deniable-but-visible escalation this file has tracked. Separately, national security chief Zolghadr issued a formal list of Hormuz-reopening conditions: lift the US naval blockade, full US troop withdrawal from the region, and a permanent end of the war. This is harder and more concrete than Araghchi's Saturday "other conditions and compensation" framing — a maximalist opening position, or a genuine hardening of Iran's ask, not yet distinguishable from this run.

ADNOC's own tally is now the standing quantitative anchor for the shipping-attack thread: 15 vessels attacked since the war began (3 this week alone), 1 crew member killed, 20 injured; strait traffic running at ~10% of pre-war levels (33 transits Monday–Thursday last week vs. 50 the prior week). This is a cleaner, more independently verifiable data point than Iran's own traffic claims, which Saturday's file already flagged as inconsistent with independent tracking.
