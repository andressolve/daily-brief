Iran / Middle East

Delta since this morning: Hormuz's substance moved against normalization while Polymarket moved toward it; Gaza's flagged cabinet vote didn't happen but the coalition fracture widened into a more durable form.

Hormuz: the draft deal's terms got harder, not softer. Iran's proposal with Oman, now under review in Iran's parliament, would bar US and Israeli ships from the Strait outright, ban cargo linked to Israel, and impose fees of up to 7% of cargo value plus 20% fines for violations of Iran's conditions. This is a harder, more specific version of the "no tolls" dispute flagged this morning — not a walk-back of "agreed in principle," but confirmation that the principle Iran agreed to is more restrictive than Washington's framing implied. The US rejected the terms on contact: "any temporary routes will be without any impediment." Trump nonetheless said Friday the talks are "moving along" — a genuine gap between his public optimism and the actual negotiating text.

The Polymarket divergence deepened, not reversed. Despite terms objectively harder to reconcile with US demands, the Hormuz contract rose +1.0pp to 59.5% — the opposite of what the headline would suggest, extending (not correcting) this morning's -3.0pp divergence. The most coherent read: the market is treating this as Iran's opening parliamentary position, still subject to negotiation, rather than a take-it-or-leave-it final term. Worth flagging as a live tension: if Iran's parliament ratifies this language unchanged, the market's calm read will look wrong in hindsight.

Gaza: the formal cabinet vote didn't happen, but the coalition fracture is sharper and more concrete. Thursday's cabinet meeting produced no vote to block the roadmap. Instead, Shin Bet chief David Zini told the Security Cabinet that Hamas is using the roadmap to buy time for a political victory "comparable to the Oct. 7, 2023, attack" — a direct intelligence-chief assessment, not just a minister's framing. More consequential: Smotrich and Strock are now claiming they voted for the original roadmap only because they received inaccurate information in the earlier cabinet discussion, and are demanding it be reconsidered. This is a different, harder form of the break than "resuming strikes to signal rejection" — an attempt to retroactively delegitimize the cabinet's own prior approval, which if it holds would be a more durable obstacle to implementation than nightly strikes. Watch whether this produces an actual re-vote over the weekend, or stays at the level of public complaint while the approved roadmap remains technically live.

No update on: Minoan Pioneer claim, IRGC-delegation gap, Mojtaba Khamenei thread, Kuwait/CENTCOM dispute — all still stale, carried forward.
