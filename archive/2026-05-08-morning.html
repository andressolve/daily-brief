The overnight kinetic exchange

USS Truxtun, USS Mason, and USS Rafael Peralta were attacked by Iranian missiles, drones, and small boats while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on May 7. No US assets were hit. CENTCOM retaliated by striking Iranian military facilities at three ports — Bandar Abbas, Qeshm, and Bandar Kargan — targeting missile/drone launch sites, C2 nodes, and ISR facilities. These are strikes inside Iranian territory.

Trump's framing: "Just a love tap." Ceasefire still in effect. He also vowed to hit Iran "more violently" if no deal struck.

Structural read: The US is conducting strikes on Iranian territory while expecting a peace deal response the same day. This is either masterful dual-track pressure (the Nixon madman theory applied to 2026) or a framework that will snap when the kinetic threshold exceeds Iran's tolerance for cognitive dissonance. The market is betting on the former.

UAE: now a persistent target

UAE reported another Iranian missile barrage Friday — 2 ballistic missiles and 3 drones. Three people wounded. This is the second attack on UAE after the May 4 Fujairah oil terminal strike. The UAE has not yet invoked Article 51 self-defense, but the political pressure to respond is building. Watch Abu Dhabi's diplomatic statement today.

MOU response: Rubio's "today" deadline

Rubio told reporters in Rome: "We should know something today." He also called the PGSA "unacceptable" — "Iran now claims they own an international waterway... That's an unacceptable thing they're trying to normalize." And acknowledged the structural problem: Iran's system is "fractured and dysfunctional."

Iran's position: Still "reviewing" messages from US via Pakistani mediators. No formal response delivered. This is now Day 3 of what was billed as a 48-hour window. Pakistan Today reports the world is awaiting Tehran's response.

1,500 commercial vessels trapped in the Persian Gulf, unable to transit. PGSA compliance creates a catch-22: complying with Iran's transit authority could trigger US sanctions; refusing means no passage.

Jobs report: the goldilocks print

April nonfarm payrolls +115K vs 62K consensus. Unemployment held 4.3%. Average hourly earnings +0.2% MoM (below 0.3% est), +3.6% YoY (below 3.8% est). Gains in healthcare (+37K), transport/warehousing (+30K), retail (+22K). Losses in information (-13K), federal government (-9K), manufacturing (-2K).

Read: Resilient labor market with cooling wage growth. This is the exact combination that keeps the Fed on hold — no urgency to cut (economy not collapsing), no urgency to hike (wages not accelerating). The market loves this, which is why equity futures are up despite overnight bombing runs.

Oil-gold divergence: gold was right all along

This morning the divergence is BACK and widened. Gold at $4,720 (weekly high) while oil merely recovered to $100. Gold has been the more honest asset throughout this crisis — stubbornly pricing the tail risk that equity and oil markets dismiss.

Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

The US bombed three Iranian ports and both sides called it a ceasefire. This is the defining absurdity — and the defining opportunity. If Iran responds to the MOU constructively today (as Rubio expects), the peace trade accelerates: oil drops, AI and equities run, energy hedge becomes a trim candidate. If Iran uses the strikes as justification to reject, oil goes to $110+ and the whole thesis resets. Gold at $4,720 is pricing the second outcome; equity futures are pricing the first. The goldilocks jobs report (115K, wages cooling) provides a macro floor for equities regardless, but it doesn't resolve the geopolitical binary. The asymmetry still favors patience. Wait for Iran's response today before acting on any position.
