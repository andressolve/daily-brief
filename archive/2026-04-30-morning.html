CENTCOM Briefing: The Decision Tree Expands

Admiral Brad Cooper will brief Trump today on new military options, per Axios. Three plans are expected:

  1. "Short and powerful" wave of strikes on Iranian infrastructure targets, designed to break the negotiating deadlock and force Iran back to the table with more flexibility on nuclear.
  2. Physical seizure of part of the Strait of Hormuz to reopen it to commercial shipping. Could include ground forces. This is the most operationally complex option.
  3. Special forces operation to secure Iran's enriched uranium stockpile. The highest-risk, highest-reward option.

Structural read: Yesterday, Trump's posture was "blockade is more effective than bombing" — a single-path strategy. Today, he gets briefed on three kinetic alternatives. Even if he doesn't authorize any, their existence changes the calculus. The briefing itself is a signal to Tehran: the siege has an expiry if Iran doesn't yield. The question is what triggers the switch — $140 oil? $5 gas? An IRGC provocation?

IRGC Responds: Mousavi Threatens Warships

"We have already seen what happened to your bases in the region; your warships will face the same fate." — IRGC Aerospace Commander Majid Mousavi, Iran International

This is from the aerospace arm — the division that controls ballistic missiles and drone capability. Not fast-boat posturing. Mousavi's statement is a pre-emptive deterrent response to CENTCOM strike planning reports. The bear case: IRGC is building justification for retaliatory capability display if strikes materialize. Iran demonstrated this capability in the Apr 20 drone attack on US warships.

The Oil Reversal: Demand Destruction Ceiling Found

Brent spiked to $126 per barrel — the highest since Russia's 2022 Ukraine invasion — on reports of the CENTCOM military briefing, then pulled back to ~$114 as the IEA's demand destruction framing took hold. (CNBC, CNN)

Structural read: The intraday reversal maps the market's ceiling. Somewhere between $120-$130, demand destruction becomes the natural brake — shipping companies reroute, manufacturers substitute, consumers cut back. But this ceiling is conditional: if strikes materialize, analysts warn oil could blow through to $140-$150 before demand can adjust. (Euronews)

The political dimension: Gas at $4.30/gal, up 27 cents in a single week. This is approaching the pain threshold for Trump's base. The tension: Trump explicitly chose blockade over bombing ("more effective"), but the blockade's economic cost to American consumers is now visible at every gas station. Does $4.50 or $5.00 gas change his calculus?

UAE Exits OPEC — Effective Today

The UAE's OPEC/OPEC+ exit is effective May 1. The UAE was OPEC's third-largest producer with capacity of 4.8M bpd vs. its old quota of 3.2M bpd. (Al Jazeera)

Near-term: Muted. Hormuz blocks UAE exports too. Long-term: HSBC estimates ADNOC could ramp to 4.5M+ bpd over 12-18 months. If Hormuz reopens, that's 1.3M+ bpd of new supply — structurally bearish for oil. More importantly, the exit undermines OPEC+ cohesion and makes coordinated supply management harder. (CNBC)
