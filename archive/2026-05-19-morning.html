Oil Sanctions Waiver: Dead on Arrival

Yesterday's evening brief flagged the Tasnim/Bloomberg report of a US oil sanctions waiver as "potentially the single most important concession of the entire war." It wasn't real. A US official flatly rejected the claim overnight, stating any sanctions easing would require "reciprocal actions from Tehran."

The structural read: Iran's semi-official media either fabricated the waiver to test US reaction, or a trial balloon was floated and immediately shot down by hawks in the administration. Either way, the single biggest economic incentive the US could offer — temporary oil sanctions relief, Iran's #1 demand — is off the table. This narrows the negotiating space significantly.

Market impact: Brent crashed from >$111 to ~$102 on the original report. It's now at $110.81 — recovered but not back to $111+. The market has partially repriced the denial but hasn't fully unwound the move. Upside risk to $113+ if no diplomatic progress today.

Situation Room: What to Expect
