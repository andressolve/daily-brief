"Open" means open-on-Iran's-terms

Overnight, Iranian state media said troops fired warning missiles at four vessels attempting to cross Hormuz without authorization — and framed it as part of the effort to open the Strait. This is the PGSA toll-keeper model operating live during deal-drafting. It reinforces the Goldman "sloppy peace" read we've carried for weeks: even a signed MOU institutionalizes Iranian gatekeeping, not a return to pre-war free transit. A signature changes the legal frame; it does not hand the Strait back.

The Lebanon contradiction

The sharpest new geopolitical fact is not Hormuz — it's Lebanon. On May 28, Israel declared the entirety of south Lebanon (everything south of the Zahrani River — ~14% of the country, ~300 towns and villages including Tyre, hundreds of thousands of residents) a combat zone, issuing a mass forced-displacement order. Netanyahu and Defense Minister Katz ordered a "deepening" of operations. Roughly 1,000 people have been killed in Lebanon since the April 16 ceasefire; over a million displaced since March.

This collides head-on with the MOU. The reported 60-day framework includes a provision to end the Israel-Hezbollah war — yet Israel is escalating to the largest forced-displacement order of the conflict the same week Trump decides whether to sign. Either the Lebanon clause is aspirational, or Israel is locking in facts on the ground before a deal freezes them. Either way it is a live way the clean-deal path breaks, and the top thread for the evening run.
