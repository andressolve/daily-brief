Ghalibaf's "Operation Fauxios" — The Most Important Overnight Signal

Mohammad-Baqer Ghalibaf, Iran's parliament speaker, posted on X: "Operation Trust Me Bro failed, now it's Operation Fauxios" — a portmanteau of "faux" (fake) and "Axios," mocking both Trump's diplomatic overtures and the Axios MOU report. In an audio message to Iranians, he declared the nation will "give their lives but will not surrender."

This matters because Ghalibaf is not a reformist. He is the pragmatic hawk faction leader who led the Islamabad delegation and is Trump's designated Iranian contact. If the faction most capable of delivering a deal is publicly mocking it, the 48-hour response window (expiring ~May 8 morning) is more likely to produce a rejection or impossible counter-demands than acceptance.

PGSA: Iran's "New Procedures" Are a Sovereignty Assertion

The Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA), founded May 5, is now operational. All vessels seeking Hormuz transit must email pgsa@hormuz.ir, submit registration forms (destination, origin, flag, cargo value, crew nationalities), and pay tolls before receiving transit permits. (Maritime Executive)

Structural read: This is NOT what you build if you plan to relinquish Hormuz control. The PGSA is an institutional bureaucracy with toll revenue — it creates a constituency for maintaining the regime. The IRGC's "safe, stable transit" statement (May 6) now reads as: Iranian-controlled, toll-based transit. Open passage was never offered.

Shipowner reaction: Five industry executives told Bloomberg/Insurance Journal they won't send ships yet. Fearnleys: "The shipowners I've spoken to have said they'll believe it when they see it." Bimco awaiting official confirmation. No significant traffic increase observed. (Insurance Journal)

Israel Bombed Beirut — Complicating Factor

IDF killed a Radwan Force commander in Beirut's southern suburbs on May 6 — the first Beirut strike since the Israel-Hezbollah ceasefire on April 17. Strikes continued May 7 in southern Lebanon. This gives Iran cover to slow-walk the MOU response ("how can we negotiate while our ally is being bombed?") and complicates the broader peace architecture. (Al Jazeera, WaPo)

NVIDIA-Corning: Building the Physical AI Infrastructure Layer

NVIDIA announced a $3.2B partnership with Corning for three new advanced optical fiber manufacturing plants in North Carolina and Texas. Corning will expand US fiber production capacity 50%+ and optical connectivity manufacturing 10x. NVIDIA receives warrants for up to 15M Corning shares at $180 exercise plus a $500M pre-funded warrant. 3,000 new jobs. (CNBC, NVIDIA Newsroom)

Structural read: NVIDIA is investing in co-packaged optics — replacing copper interconnects with glass fiber in AI rack-scale systems. This is supply-side infrastructure investment, not a demand-side GPU sale. It signals NVIDIA sees multi-year AI datacenter buildout as locked in and is now building the physical layer beneath compute. Bullish for AI capex sustainability.

Anthropic $30B ARR — Passed OpenAI

Anthropic hit $30B annualized revenue in April 2026, surpassing OpenAI at ~$25B. Growth trajectory: $1B (Jan 2025) → $9B (Dec 2025) → $14B (Feb 2026) → $19B (Mar 2026) → $30B (Apr 2026). 80% enterprise revenue. 1,000+ customers at $1M+/yr. (SaaStr)
