Khamenei funeral formally begins 7/4 — Mojtaba non-attendance officially explained

Per WaPo 7/3, CNN 7/3, and Time 7/3: the public funeral rites for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei (killed Feb 28 in the US/Israeli strikes that opened the 39-day war) formally begin Saturday July 4 at 6 a.m. Tehran time when Tehran's Imam Khomeini Mosalla opens for a continuous 24-hour farewell ceremony. The main procession follows on July 6, a 10-kilometer route from Imam Hossein Square to Azadi Square. Burial is at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad on July 9. Officials from China, India, and Pakistan are attending; millions of Shi'ite Muslims expected including travelers from Iraq, Lebanon, and Pakistan.

Iran Hormuz warning + Gharibabadi CENTCOM pushback

Per Al Jazeera 7/3, The Hill 7/3, and PBS 7/3: Iran's joint military command warned Thursday that all oil tankers moving through the Strait of Hormuz must use Iran's approved routes or face “an immediate and forceful response from the armed forces, endangering the security of the violating vessels.”

The warning was prompted by two things: (i) CENTCOM's Wednesday Bahrain security dialogue at which regional leaders reaffirmed the “free flow of commerce” through the strait, and (ii) a growing share of tankers using an Omani-coast route that undercuts Tehran's leverage over the strait.

Iran's deputy FM Kazem Gharibabadi — the same principal-Iran-actor who announced the MOU-violation communication channel on Wednesday — pushed back on CENTCOM Thursday-Friday: the Bahrain forum “cannot establish legal order and security for the Persian Gulf.” Iran's government defines the strait routes, not U.S. Central Command.

Structural read. This is the first same-week friction print from a principal-Iran-actor who architected the framework's substance-track channel. Gharibabadi Wednesday = channel architect. Gharibabadi Friday = CENTCOM rejection. Same principal, opposite direction. The framework has to prove it can survive Iranian leverage-assertions. The Hormuz warning is a route-control assertion (leverage), not a blockade threat (flow denial) — importantly, Brent's modest +0.6% response is the price signal that the market read it as leverage assertion, not blockade risk. If markets had priced blockade, Brent would move percentage points, not 0.6%.
