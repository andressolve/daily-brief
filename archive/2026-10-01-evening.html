The three-session "10Y/30Y back-up is a structural, not one-day, story" thesis this file carried all week broke today — the 10Y closed around 5.24%, down 5-6bp from Wednesday's close and well off this morning's 5.34% intraday high, after ISM Manufacturing PMI came in soft (54.5 vs. 55 consensus, down from August's 54.6) (CNBC). That matters because it's the first scheduled data print in four sessions to move yields the textbook-expected direction (soft data → lower yields) rather than being overridden by debt-supply/term-premium pressure. Equities staged a genuine comeback from a weak, yield-pressured open: index closes conflict slightly across sources (S&P +0.19% to +0.23%, Nasdaq +0.04% to +0.45%, Dow +0.04% to +0.38%) but agree on direction and on the story — chip stocks, led by Micron's beat-and-raise, rallied broadly, and unlike Tuesday and Wednesday's "pop at the open, fade by the close" pattern, today's gains held (Yahoo Finance, TheStreet). VIX, notably, ticked up slightly to 16.34 (+1.9%) even as yields eased — a small, worth-flagging divergence from the pattern this file logged all week. Oil reversed hard: Brent fell 1.3% to $96.76, giving back essentially all of this morning's China-refiner-driven spike to $99.81 — that catalyst didn't survive the session. WTI prints still conflict across sources ($90.06 vs. $91.50), an unresolved data-quality gap carried from prior sessions. A heavy Fed speaker slate (Barkin, Collins, Schmid, Waller, Jefferson, Bowman, Cook, plus Williams and Logan) produced no single market-moving line found tonight — the ISM miss, not the speakers, reads as today's actual driver.

Read: Worth being honest that this is a genuine, if single-session, crack in a thesis this file treated as structural through Wednesday night — the first time in four sessions a data print pulled yields the expected direction instead of the term-premium story overriding it. Equally notable: today is the first session this week where chip-led gains didn't compress into the close, which is arguably the more tradeable fact than the yield move itself. Two explanations are live and this file can't yet adjudicate between them: either the long-end back-up is genuinely pausing, or today is a one-off data-driven dip inside a still-intact structural uptrend. Bear case: a single soft ISM print against weeks of debt-supply pressure and a still-undecided Oct FOMC is thin evidence the structural story is over — Monday's open is the next real test. Bull case: this is exactly the kind of session (cooler data, yields ease, risk assets rally and hold) the "rates are the dominant cross-asset driver" framing predicted would eventually happen once a data print actually cooperated.
