Iran / Middle East Geopolitics Priority

Delta since last run
Rubio's "slight progress" at NATO is the first positive US signal since the Khamenei uranium directive two days ago. Pakistan Army Chief in Tehran. But Rubio simultaneously rejected Iran's Hormuz tolling system and raised "Plan B" with NATO allies. The tone shifted; the structure hasn't.

What Rubio actually said

At the NATO foreign ministers meeting in Sweden, Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters: "I don't want to exaggerate it, but there's been a little bit of movement, and that's good." The US is waiting for Iran's formal response to its latest terms, conveyed this week through Pakistani mediators.

Critically, Rubio also rejected Iran's proposed Hormuz tolling system: "That's just not acceptable. It can't happen. If that were to happen in the Straits of Hormuz, it will happen in five other places around the world." He raised a "Plan B" with NATO allies — the first time the Hormuz contingency has been multilateralized beyond the US-Iran bilateral frame.

The structural read

Rubio's comments carry three simultaneous signals: (1) genuine forward movement on some axis of the negotiation, (2) continued hardline on Hormuz and nuclear, and (3) coalition-building for pressure if talks fail. The "slight progress" likely refers to peripheral terms (sanctions relief framework, economic provisions) rather than the core uranium or Hormuz issues. The fact that he felt comfortable saying it publicly — at NATO, no less — suggests the administration wants to signal willingness to deal, perhaps to manage oil prices downward.

Pakistan Army Chief Munir in Tehran is the active mediation channel. No direct US-Iran meetings since April 12. All communication runs through Islamabad. The military channel (Army Chief vs. FM) suggests security guarantees are part of the discussion.

The oil-to-yield transmission

The causal chain is clean: Rubio "progress" → Iran deal odds rise → oil drops $5-6/bbl on the week → inflation expectations ease → 10Y yield falls 7bp → equities rally → Dow record. If Brent sustains below $105, the rate-cut thesis could re-emerge for H2 2026. If talks stall and oil bounces back above $108, the entire chain reverses.

Bear case on this rally: It's built entirely on diplomatic optimism. Rubio himself said "I don't want to exaggerate it." The structural obstacles haven't changed. Oil could snap back to $108-110 on a single negative headline. The rally is headline-driven, not fundamentally-driven.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last run
Three material developments: AMD Venice on TSMC 2nm (AMD +5.89%), NVDA H100 rentals up 20% YTD (neoclouds +4%+), and the $45B SpaceX-Anthropic COLOSSUS deal.

AMD Venice EPYC on TSMC 2nm

AMD announced its next-generation EPYC "Venice" processor is ramping on TSMC's 2nm process, with future production planned for TSMC's Arizona fab. CEO Lisa Su highlighted "substantial increase in demand for CPUs, particularly fueled by AI inferencing and agentic AI workloads." Stock +5.89% on May 22.

Structural read: This is the clearest validation of the CPU-as-AI-foundation thesis. Venice on 2nm puts AMD at process parity with the leading edge. The inference shift from pure-GPU to CPU+GPU architectures is real — and AMD is positioning to capture it. For the portfolio: AMD remains far above the $190-210 Feb target zone, but the thesis is strengthening, not fading.

NVDA H100 pricing power

NVDA CFO Colette Kress disclosed H100 rental prices are up 20% YTD and A100 up ~15%. Neocloud stocks surged: NBIS, CRWV, and IREN all gained 4%+ on the news.

Why this matters for the portfolio: Rising GPU rental rates directly counter the D.A. Davidson thin-margin bear case from May 19. If the underlying GPU asset is appreciating in rental value, neocloud revenue per GPU-hour is expanding, not compressing. This doesn't resolve the leverage concerns, but it undermines the primary margin objection.

SpaceX-Anthropic: $45B COLOSSUS deal

Anthropic is paying xAI $1.25B/month ($15B/year, $45B over 3 years) for access to COLOSSUS 1 and COLOSSUS 2 compute infrastructure: 220,000+ NVIDIA GPUs, expanding to GB200 capacity. Either side can terminate with 90 days' notice. (TechCrunch)

Three structural reads: (1) Anthropic's $30B ARR can fund $15B/yr compute spend — the AI revenue cycle is self-sustaining at the top. (2) xAI's COLOSSUS infrastructure is commercially competitive, not just an internal asset — Musk is monetizing excess capacity. (3) The "compute is the bottleneck" thesis is validated by willingness to pay at this scale. For portfolio: NVDA benefits (GPU demand), SpaceX IPO gets revenue anchor, and Anthropic gets capacity it couldn't build fast enough organically.

SpaceX IPO retail access

SpaceX announced direct retail access through Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, and E*TRADE at the same IPO price as institutions. Target $1.75T valuation. Jun 12 listing. For Blue Owl (OWL): SpaceX carry on OWL's books is at ~$1.25T; a $1.75T IPO = significant markup potential.

Bottom Line

Rubio's "slight progress" changed the tone. Oil dropping 5-7% on the week is the most portfolio-relevant signal — it eases inflation fears, brings yields down, and supports the equity risk-on trade. But the structural negotiation gaps (uranium enrichment, Hormuz control) haven't closed. Rubio himself rejected Iran's tolling system in the same breath he acknowledged progress. The market is pricing a deal; the diplomats are still arguing about first principles. For the portfolio: the macro environment is cautiously improving (oil down, yields down, Dow record), but individual entry zones haven't reopened. The right move remains patience — if the deal track continues and oil sustains below $105, the macro case for entry strengthens even at current valuations. If talks stall, oil bounces back to $108+ and we're back to yesterday.
