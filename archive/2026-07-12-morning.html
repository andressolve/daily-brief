Iran / Middle East

Delta since 7/11 morning Trump ultimatum rejected, third strike wave (~140 targets, 300+ cumulative), formal Hormuz closure declaration, five-country retaliation wave including a strike on Qatar's Al Udeid Air Base.

Iran rejected Trump's 24-hour ultimatum; the US ran a third straight night of strikes. CENTCOM said Saturday night's wave hit ~140 military targets — missile and drone sites, naval capabilities, ammunition storage facilities, communication networks, and coastal surveillance locations — bringing the three-night cumulative total past 300 targets struck. Washington Post, Fox News.

Iran's IRGC navy formally declared the Strait of Hormuz "closed until further notice." The announcement came early Sunday after Iran said it fired a warning shot at a vessel attempting to transit an unauthorized route. This is a real hardening from the "gradual reopening" framing IRGC itself used as recently as Thursday — a rhetorical shift that lines up with the hard data: daily Hormuz transit counts fell from 35 ships (7/7) to 17 (7/9) to 11 (7/10), per IMF PortWatch tracking. Investing.com/Reuters.

Within 24 hours, Iran retaliated across five Gulf states. Coordinated missile and drone strikes hit US military facilities in Jordan, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, and the UAE — the most geographically dispersed single retaliation wave of the war. In Qatar, the IRGC specifically targeted Al Udeid Air Base, the largest US airbase in the region, claiming it destroyed a command-and-control center and an aircraft-maintenance facility; Qatar says it intercepted the incoming missiles, and neither side's claims are independently verified. This is a second, distinct Iranian strike on Qatar this week — Thursday's strike hit an early-warning radar system; this one specifically targeted the airbase. Euronews, Ynet.

Structural read Every axis of this weekend's news points toward escalation, not the "positive progress" mediator claim Friday's Qatar talks generated. A mediator state absorbing a direct strike on its largest US airbase is about as hard a signal as exists that the diplomatic channel and the military campaign are now operating on fully disconnected tracks — bear case: there is no obvious off-ramp visible from either side right now, and the formal Hormuz closure declaration (as opposed to informal near-standstill) is itself an escalation in language, not just fact.

Bottom Line

This is the most kinetically active weekend of the war since the initial strikes — a formal Hormuz closure, 300+ cumulative targets struck, and a five-country retaliation wave that hit a mediator's own airbase. What makes it genuinely strange is that none of the markets that should be pricing geopolitical risk are moving: Fed odds drifted toward complacency, recession odds are flat, and the Israel-Iran peace contract barely twitched. Only the narrowest, most mechanical market — the Hormuz ship-count contract — repriced hard, because it's tracking a fact (vessel transits), not a narrative. That's the calibration lesson of this weekend: when rhetoric escalates faster than a market can verify it, watch the contracts tied to observable facts, not sentiment. The open question is whether Monday's cash session — starting with oil — closes that gap or extends it. Zero positions remain deployed on Day 135; nothing here changes that, but Monday's open is now the cleanest, highest-stakes catalyst window of the week.
