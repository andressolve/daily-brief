Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night: a second, distinct maritime chokepoint (Bab el-Mandeb) turned fatal for the first time in a year; Iran's Vela Nova response stayed rhetorical; Trump's own posture hardened toward attrition; Polymarket's Hormuz contract fell to a fresh cycle low.

Second front: the Houthis re-enter the war with the first fatal Red Sea attack in over a year. Yemen's Houthis fired ballistic missiles at the small cargo ship Tihamah in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, killing 3 Pakistani and 1 Indonesian crew members; a follow-up "double-tap" strike on the rescue operation killed 2 more. A Houthi-aligned outlet claimed the target was carrying Saudi military equipment — unverified, sourced only to Houthi-aligned media (CNBC, UPI, Al Jazeera, 8/11-8/12). This opens a second maritime chokepoint front alongside Hormuz — worth tracking as a distinct escalation vector rather than folding it into the Hormuz/US-blockade thread, even though both bear on Gulf shipping risk broadly. Whether this is Houthi opportunism exploiting a distracted US Navy, or coordinated with Tehran, is not yet established either way.

Iran's response to the Vela Nova strike is rhetorical escalation, not kinetic retaliation — so far. FM spokesman Baghaei stated Hormuz stays closed until the US "ends" the naval blockade and "compensates for the damage" — explicitly conditioning reopening on a US climbdown, not just the Oman technical track. No missile, drone, or proxy response targeting US assets has been reported as of this pull. Tehran is using the strike rhetorically (hardening its public ask) rather than escalating kinetically — consistent with the standing pattern of Iran preferring leverage-via-blockade over direct confrontation with US forces.

Trump's own posture hardened too — he's now framing this as a war of attrition, not an imminent deal. Trump told Axios he's willing to let economic pressure grind rather than force a settlement, and is holding to the reparations demand as a precondition — a harder line than the "today or tomorrow" and "soon" framing he used as recently as 8/4-8/7. Both sides have now separately signaled they're settling in for a longer standoff rather than a near-term resolution — Iran conditioning reopening on a US climbdown, the US conditioning any deal on reparations neither side expects the other to grant. This strengthens, not just confirms, the 8/11 read that both reparations demands function as cover for stalemate.

Hormuz bill: outline approved, not a final law — correction to prior framing. Iran's parliamentary National Security Committee approved only the outline of the Hormuz bill on 8/9 (unopposed); full floor vote, Guardian Council review, and presidential signature are all still pending. "Advancing" was and is accurate; a final, binding bill is not imminent. Iran and Oman reportedly agreed on geographic coordinates for the proposed dual-lane route, but the arrangement remains stalled per Baghaei's comments above — the 8/11 "US objected and stalled it" read carries forward, unresolved either direction.

Gaza: no new movement. Netanyahu's rejection of the Board of Peace's 15-point roadmap stands; the Board's Mladenov pushed back publicly ("nobody is required to do anything... last of all, Israel, before we have verified steps"). Trump still not engaging the rift directly — unchanged from 8/11's read.

The genuine cross-market puzzle this morning: kinetic severity is rising while price action is flat-to-risk-on. Oil is roughly flat, equities are green on an in-line CPI print, and even Polymarket's Hormuz-normalization odds falling reads more like "reopening is less likely by Dec 31" than "the war is escalating toward broader conflict." The market appears to be reading the US Vela Nova strike as blockade-enforcement (a step toward eventual reopening on US terms) rather than as war escalation — the opposite framing from how equivalent kinetic news moved oil +2.3-2.5% on 8/11. This gap is worth watching for whether the Houthi fatalities (once fully priced) close it, or whether markets continue treating Gulf-adjacent kinetic incidents as noise unless they hit the Strait of Hormuz itself.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last night: Nebius's Q2 confirms CoreWeave's beat was sector-wide, but the same capex-outrunning-revenue shape shows up again; KKR confirmed alongside Apollo in NVDA's $500B platform; ERCOT/FERC jurisdiction correction.

Nebius (NBIS) Q2, reported this morning: revenue $582.3M (+454% YoY, beat ~$574M consensus), AI cloud revenue +514% to $575M, adjusted EBITDA turned sharply positive at $236.2M (vs. -$21M loss in Q2'25) — first real profitability inflection; GAAP net loss -$190.4M, adjusted EPS -$0.12 (beat -$0.67 est.) (TradingView, BusinessWire). Capacity guidance raised to 5GW for 2026 (from 4GW), capex guidance raised to ~$20-25B (from ~$16B) — but full-year revenue guidance held flat at $3.0-3.4B despite the beat. Opex cash flow $2.25B vs. capex $5.66B in the quarter — spending running well ahead of operating cash generation, financed externally, the same pattern CRWV showed last night. Stock volatile: premarket +5-18% swings before settling, choppier than CRWV's clean move.

This reframes the standing question, not just confirms it. Raising capacity/capex guidance while holding revenue guidance flat suggests neoclouds aren't demand- or capital-constrained right now — they're power- and buildout-speed-constrained. That's a different bear case than the vendor-financing-circularity thread this file has tracked: circularity is about who is financing the buildout and whether that web is systemically fragile; a power/speed constraint is about whether the buildout can physically happen fast enough regardless of financing. Both risks are live simultaneously — worth tracking as separate axes, not conflating "the capital is there" with "the capacity will get built on schedule."

KKR confirmed as a second named BDC-complex partner in NVDA's $500B platform, alongside Apollo. NVIDIA's original 8/10 announcement names six partners — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — not Apollo alone, correcting yesterday's Apollo-only framing in this file. KKR's role builds on its existing tie to NVIDIA as a founding investor in Helix Digital Infrastructure (NVIDIA). This doubles, not just confirms, the direct crossover between the tracked private-credit complex and AI-capex circularity — both Apollo and KKR are now formally embedded as platform financiers, not just adjacent BDC sponsors.

Correction: ERCOT's pause is a state/PUCT process, not a FERC one. ERCOT confirmed via market notice it missed the Aug 7 deadline to classify "Batch Zero" large loads under Gov. Abbott's verification directive, and will file a "good cause exception" ahead of PUCT's Aug 20 meeting. ERCOT is explicitly outside FERC's jurisdiction on interconnection rules — the 8/13 FERC show-cause deadline applies to six other RTOs (PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO), not ERCOT. This corrects prior framing in this file that implicitly conflated the two tracks — going forward, ERCOT/PUCT (next date: 8/20) and the FERC six-RTO show-cause (8/13) are two separate, parallel regulatory threads.

Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication
