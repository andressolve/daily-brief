Print 1 — Beijing Hormuz fee formalization: substance-track binary carved out

At the World Peace Forum in Beijing on Saturday, Iran's ambassador to China Abdolreza Rahmani Fazli stated that Iran will “definitely” charge new fees for ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz, framed as service fees (safe-passage monitoring, environmental) rather than tolls, and announced that “friendly nations that stood by us during the hard times” will receive “special treatment” (Japan Times 7/5; Times of Israel 7/4; Arab News 7/5). China and Russia are the implicit tier-1 beneficiaries. Iran disclosed “collaboration and cooperation with Oman on new arrangements” for the waterway. No implementation date, fee schedule, or eligibility criteria disclosed.

Direct defiance of Rubio's public position (Al Jazeera 6/24; Times of Israel):

“It's an international waterway. No country is allowed to charge tolls or fees on an international waterway.” — Sec. of State Rubio

Structural read. This is a qualitatively different print from Friday's route-warning. Friday was a coercive posture (route menu control). Sat's Beijing announcement is a revenue-model claim on a chokepoint framed with a carve-out for aligned states — turning the operational leverage into permanent policy AND handing tier-1 partners a discriminatory benefit. This carves out a specific substance-track binary that Rubio and Fazli have staked publicly on opposite sides of. It is not yet a mechanism break (channel + coordination cell remain live; talks-pause is scheduled), but it is the sharpest publicly-articulated substance-track gap of cycle.

Bull-side counter: the venue (Beijing) and speaker tier (ambassador-not-FM) leave room for it to be a China-audience trial balloon rather than final policy; the AM $3B walk-back precedent (7/1) shows Iranian diplomatic prints can be re-scoped after Trump/Rubio pushback. Framework-material trigger: a principal-tier voice (Pezeshkian, Araghchi, Larijani, Mojtaba, Qaani) echoing the fee-policy commitment before 7/10.
