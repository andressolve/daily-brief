Summit Day 2: One New Verb, No New Mechanism

Trump said Xi offered to help negotiate an end to the war with Iran and keep Hormuz open. Xi pledged China would not provide military equipment to Iran and said there should "never be a charge" for transiting Hormuz. But per Yahoo Finance/AP and CSIS, the summit produced "little movement on the war in Iran." Analysts expect stabilization in US-China relations but no breakthrough on Iran.

Structural read: Xi's mediation offer costs China nothing. The question remains: does China actually cut Iranian oil purchases? Exert economic leverage on Tehran? Or just position itself as helpful without doing anything? Until Beijing takes a costly action (cutting ~1.5M bpd of Iranian crude imports), this is diplomacy as theater.

Hormuz: Getting Worse, Not Better

Despite summit rhetoric about "open Hormuz," open transits have fallen to near zero per day since May 6. The PGSA is operational, requiring all vessels to register and pay tolls. 1,500+ vessels remain stranded in the Persian Gulf. Iran has laid mines, deployed GPS spoofing, and is conducting drone/missile attacks on shipping. The physical reality of Hormuz is diverging sharply from the diplomatic rhetoric.

Lebanon: Clock at T-3 Days

A third round of talks with higher-level envoys is underway in DC. Lebanon demanding ceasefire extension, Israeli withdrawal timetable, and detainee release. Ceasefire expires Sunday, May 18. Since March 2: 2,896 killed, 1.6M displaced.
