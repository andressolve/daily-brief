Delta since this morning: oil re-coupled with the news even as Polymarket didn't; the parliamentary Hormuz bill advanced; a new, non-kinetic thread (USS Abraham Lincoln crew-welfare crisis) surfaced as a complication to the blockade's "indefinite" framing.

Correction to this morning: oil was not "little changed" by the close. Iran's parliamentary Hormuz bill moved from "stuck" to "advancing." The Committee on Councils and Internal Affairs approved the general principles of the "Strategic Action Plan for Ensuring Security and Development of the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf" — 7 of 14 articles approved, including a formal ban on transit of US, Israeli, and "hostile state" equipment through the strait (HNGN). This corrects this morning's "remains stuck in committee, no floor vote scheduled" line — there's real committee-level movement now, though full parliamentary passage is still pending.

New, non-kinetic thread: a genuine crew-welfare crisis aboard USS Abraham Lincoln, now a political liability for the "indefinite blockade" posture. The carrier has been deployed 260+ days — one of the longest in modern history — with sailors' families reporting food shortages, contaminated water, broken toilets, and at least two attempted suicides; Senate Democrats are demanding a formal investigation from Hegseth, who called the reports "completely misrepresented." USS George Washington is reportedly steaming to relieve the Lincoln (Washington Post, CNBC, Bloomberg).

Read: this is the first concrete evidence that Hegseth's "sustainable indefinitely" claim — made just yesterday — has an actual human/operational cost generating its own political story, independent of the Iran side of the ledger. Doesn't change the market-facing thesis, but it's a genuine new axis — force-sustainment strain and domestic political pressure — that could eventually constrain how "indefinite" the blockade really is. Worth tracking as its own thread going forward.

Bessent: no new substance today, previewed measures still "next week." His Newsmax quote ("measures like have never been seen in the history of economic isolation") is the same preview flagged this morning — nothing new landed today (Washington Times, The Hill). Next week's announcement remains the single most consequential pending data point.
