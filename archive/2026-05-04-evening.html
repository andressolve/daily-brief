Why Fujairah Matters

Fujairah is the terminus of the Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (ADCOP) — the route UAE uses to bypass Hormuz entirely. It carries crude from inland fields directly to the Gulf of Oman. By attacking Fujairah, Iran is communicating: "There is no safe alternative. You cannot export Gulf oil without our permission." This is the strategic equivalent of Iran blockading two straits simultaneously.

The Divergence That Matters

Oil +6%, VIX flat, equities barely down. This combination is historically unstable. It implies the market believes Hormuz combat stays contained and doesn't feed through to the broader economy. If Fujairah is hit again, or if oil sustains above $115, equities correct 3-5% minimum. Gas at $4.39/gal will reach $4.50-4.70 within a week — political and consumer pain escalates.

Gold down on a war day = the market reads this as inflationary, not deflationary. Dollar strengthens on rate expectations (oil up → inflation sticky → Fed can't cut → USD up → gold down). This reframes the war-hedge: energy long > gold long in this regime.
