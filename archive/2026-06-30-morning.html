DRAM price-fixing class action

Filed 6/25 in N.D. Cal., 17 plaintiffs (individuals + small businesses) allege Samsung + SK Hynix + Micron coordinated to keep commodity DRAM (DDR3/DDR4) artificially scarce — framing the marketed “HBM pivot” as cover to curtail commodity supply — driving ~700% price inflation over four years. The complaint leans on the 2005 DOJ price-fixing matter (Samsung guilty plea $300M, SK Hynix $185M, executives jailed; Micron avoided via amnesty/cooperator status) as a recidivism pattern. Judge Noel Wise. As of 6/30: no admissions, no settlements, no maker has disclosed a reserve (Tom’s Hardware; Yahoo; AppleInsider).

The structural read: this does NOT disconfirm AI compute demand or the demand-driven memory super-cycle. Civil class actions run in years; no DOJ involvement; no disclosed reserves. What it IS: the political-economy / value-capture bear case (sub-thesis #5 affordability) made concrete. The makers’ defense is precisely that the shortage is structural — fab space + EUV lead times, with the whole complex (including non-defendant capacity) tight; this update’s own SK Hynix/Micron data corroborate genuine AI-led undersupply, not manufactured scarcity. Timing note: lands ~10 days before SK Hynix Nasdaq ADR debut (~7/10), which inherits US class-action exposure and broader US discovery more directly than a Korea-only listing.

Escalation trigger: DOJ/FTC parallel probe or any maker disclosing a litigation reserve → escalate to a Moderate margin-capture risk on the multiple. None of this is operative yet.
