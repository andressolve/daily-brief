Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Friday morning: Iraq's Hormuz request resolved. Sanctions package gets a Monday date. Iran's rhetoric hardened. Polymarket resumed its downtrend to a fresh cycle low.

Iraq's special-status request, open since 8/19, is resolved. Iran granted Iraqi oil tankers permission to transit Hormuz — one of Baghdad's main asks during Ghalibaf's recent Iraq visit. This reads as a narrow, negotiated exception rather than a general loosening of the blockade: Iraq has no tanker fleet of its own, so the concession is specific and limited, not a signal that Tehran is easing pressure on Gulf shipping broadly.

"Economic D-Day" now has a specific date: Monday 8/24. Bessent will hold a press conference detailing what he's calling "the greatest co-ordinated economic isolation in the history of the world." This is sharper than the prior vague "next week" framing this file has carried for over a week — but this file has already logged one broken 8/14 promise on the same package, so Monday should be treated as a test of follow-through, not a given.

Iran's diplomatic track hardened, not softened, ahead of Monday. Iran's Foreign Ministry called the sanctions push a "declaration of war on all nations." Trump reiterated the (not new) "American territory" Hormuz claim, and Iran's Defense Ministry said the country is "better prepared than adversaries for a prolonged war." None of this is new positioning in kind, but the timing — hardening rhetoric in the run-up to a dated, high-profile US announcement — is consistent with Tehran trying to raise the diplomatic cost of Monday's package before it lands.

Polymarket: Hormuz resumed its downtrend, -2.0pp to 29.5% — a fresh cycle low below Thursday's 30.5%, confirming Friday's +1.0pp bounce was a pause rather than a reversal. The underlying picture (blockade, near-zero strait traffic) hasn't changed; the market's brief optimism didn't hold.
