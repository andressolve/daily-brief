Delta since Saturday morning

Bloomberg and CNN confirm a second consecutive quiet night on the direct US-Iran kinetic track: Axios reports (two unidentified sources) that Trump directed the military not to conduct new strikes as of Friday 7/24, and Iran reciprocally halted its own retaliatory attacks; CNN separately describes operations as "on a hold." This is exactly the confirmatory test flagged Saturday as the top watch item — a second quiet night meaningfully firms up, without fully resolving, the de-escalation read.

On a genuinely different front, the war escalated: Houthi rebels struck Aramco's Jizan refinery and the Yanbu export terminal on July 25 — the group's first direct attack on Saudi oil infrastructure since 2022, independently corroborated via satellite imagery and NASA fire-detection data (thermal anomalies at Jizan ~01:17 UTC), a higher-confidence read than the claims-only reporting that's characterized parts of this cycle. Jizan processes ~400,000 bpd (~4% of Aramco's output); at Yanbu, two ballistic missiles were intercepted by a Greek-operated Patriot battery — a notable new detail suggesting international/coalition air-defense involvement in Saudi Arabia not previously logged. Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said there were "no injuries or fatalities and no impact to the company's supply to its customers"; an official damage assessment is still pending.

Structural read: this is not a simple "war getting better or worse" story — it's two distinct tracks moving in opposite directions simultaneously. The direct US-Iran kinetic track is genuinely cooling; the Houthi proxy front (tracked since 7/20's blockade declaration) is escalating into a new target class it hadn't hit before — direct Saudi export infrastructure, not just tankers. Yanbu specifically has become central to Saudi crude exports since the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed — Saudi Arabia has spent five months rerouting exports through the East-West Pipeline to Yanbu, but every barrel still has to exit through Bab al-Mandeb, which the Houthis blockaded 7/20.

Bear case on Saturday's Momeni/Mojtaba pause-offer read: an Israeli security-official claim alleges Mojtaba Khamenei "is not in Iran" and that messages issued in his name are fabricated by IRGC commander Ahmad Vahidi and other senior figures; a separate claim disputes the specific "10-day pause" framing as unrealistic. This doesn't resolve either direction — no written letter, no on-record government confirmation exists either way — but it raises the bar for treating the pause offer as authentic without further corroboration.

Read against Polymarket: Hormuz-normalization continued rising to 57.5-59% (see Polymarket section) on the same day as the Aramco strikes — not necessarily contradictory, since the contract prices US-Iran/Hormuz-transit risk specifically, a different axis than the Red Sea/Bab-al-Mandeb proxy front, but it's a reminder the market isn't yet treating the Aramco strikes as Hormuz-relevant, which may or may not be right. Oil's own reaction is the cleanest read on severity: Brent spiked back above $100 immediately after the strikes, then pulled back to stabilize in the $89-90 range — consistent with the market taking Aramco's "no supply impact" statement at face value.

Watch items: a third consecutive quiet US-Iran night, any further Houthi-Aramco follow-through, and whether the Momeni/Mojtaba channel gets any on-record Iranian government confirmation — the three highest-value tests for Monday.
