The Overnight Escalation

At 10:17 PM ET on May 27, Iran launched a ballistic missile toward a US military base in Kuwait. Kuwaiti forces intercepted it. CENTCOM called it an "egregious ceasefire violation." The strike was IRGC retaliation for US "self-defense" strikes on missile launch sites and minelaying boats in southern Iran near Bandar Abbas earlier in the week.

Washington and Tehran have repeatedly accused each other of violating the ceasefire and have traded strikes throughout the week. But they have not returned to full-scale hostilities and have kept negotiating.

The Deal on Trump's Desk

Despite the military exchange, Axios reports that US and Iranian negotiators have finalized a 60-day MOU framework awaiting Trump's final approval. Reported terms:

  • Strait of Hormuz: "unrestricted" shipping — no tolls, no harassment. Iran must remove all mines within 30 days.
  • Sanctions: US issues waivers allowing Iran to sell oil freely during the 60-day window.
  • Nuclear: Iranian commitment not to pursue nuclear weapons. First negotiation topics: enriched uranium disposal, enrichment limits.
  • Status: Trump has NOT signed. Iran has NOT formally confirmed. Both reserving leverage.

Structural Read: "Sloppy Peace" Confirmed

Day 90 crystallizes the pattern. A ballistic missile at a US base + a deal framework on the President's desk, simultaneously. The IRGC missile was a faction signal: "we exist and must be accommodated in any deal." It is not a deal-killer — it is a bid for terms. The market agrees: Polymarket Dec 31 deal recovered 5pp to 79% overnight, reading the Axios report as the dominant signal over the skirmish.

But near-term deal timelines shattered. Jun 7 deal odds went from 54% to 23%. The Kuwait missile forces another round of posturing before anyone signs. The endpoint hasn't changed; the path just got messier and longer.
