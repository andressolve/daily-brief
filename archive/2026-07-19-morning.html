Iran / Middle East

Delta since Saturday morning First US combat deaths of the war confirmed; Iran declares the MoU "suspended"; Saudi Arabia threatens direct military force for the first time.

CENTCOM confirmed two US service members were killed and one is missing after Iranian ballistic missiles and drones struck Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan on Friday, 7/17 — four additional service members were medically evacuated but have since been discharged. This brings cumulative US KIA to 16 for the war and marks the first named, dated combat-death incident in this file's tracking. Trump ordered a new wave of retaliatory strikes, per CENTCOM "to punish IRGC forces" for the Jordan attack — an 8th consecutive night of US strikes, up from Saturday's 7th, hitting coastal surveillance, air-defense, and missile/drone storage sites near Sirik and Qeshm Island. Iran's Health Ministry now puts cumulative casualties from US strikes since 7/6 at 50 killed and more than 500 injured; civilian infrastructure damage has escalated on both sides — Iran's Bunji desalination plant in Jask was "completely destroyed" (over 10,000 residents lost water access, 20 villages affected), a bridge connecting Roudan and Bandar Abbas was hit, and Iranian retaliation struck Kuwaiti civilian power and desalination infrastructure directly.

Separately, and just as consequentially, senior Iranian officials explicitly declared the mid-June Islamabad MoU dead. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the US "has violated and suspended all its commitments," Foreign Ministry spokesman Baghaei accused Washington of violating every article of the agreement, and First Vice President Aref said the strikes came "before the diplomatic ink had dried." Asked directly whether Iran was abandoning the interim agreement, Trump's on-record response was: "I couldn't care less."

"The US has violated and suspended all its commitments." — Kazem Gharibabadi, Iran Deputy Foreign Minister, 7/18
Structural read — calibration divergence This is the first time either side has used explicit "suspended"/dead-agreement language on record about the MoU — a real rhetorical escalation. But Polymarket's own MoU-specific contracts don't yet treat it as a formal trigger event: "Iran announces withdrawal from MOU negotiations by July 24" reads just 9.5% Yes (28% by Aug 15), while the mirror "US announces withdrawal... by Aug 31" contract sits meaningfully higher at 35.5% Yes on a steeper rising curve. The market is reading Gharibabadi's statement as continued brinkmanship rhetoric, not a resolvable withdrawal — and currently prices US disengagement from the framework as more likely than Iranian disengagement, consistent with Trump's dismissive quote above. Worth watching whether this gap between "what officials are saying" and "what the market is pricing" closes or widens through the week.

New this run: Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince vowed, with explicit US backing, to use military force against Iranian incursions — the first direct Saudi military threat of the war, and a genuine widening-of-the-conflict risk that wasn't previously on this file's radar. The Saudi foreign ministry separately condemned strikes on Riyadh and the Eastern Province despite Saudi Arabia's declared airspace closure. Saturday's flag on Gulf-state diplomatic-response dating is now resolved: Qatar's Prime Minister/Foreign Minister held dated, current-wave calls with both Iran's Araghchi and Saudi FM Prince Faisal bin Farhan urging a recommitment to diplomacy, alongside fresh (not recycled) UAE/Qatar/Saudi condemnations — confirming these are genuine 7/17–7/19 reactions rather than stale coverage of the earlier 7/12–7/15 wave.
