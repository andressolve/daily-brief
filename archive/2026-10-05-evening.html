Iran / Yemen Biggest confirmed strike claim of the stretch; new US-side credibility split

Delta since this morning Houthi claim escalates from "near Riyadh" to named targets (airport, refinery) plus a global-aviation threat. Yemeni claim upgrades from Dhubab to Mocha. Trump breaks with his own VP and Israeli intel on the Flydubai incident.

The Houthis claimed drone, ballistic-missile, and cruise-missile strikes on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Aramco's refinery at Rabigh, plus unspecified military sites and smaller airports — and warned "all global airlines using Saudi airspace" against continuing flights, with an exception carved out for Mecca and Medina (CBS News). This is a harder, more specific claim than this morning's contested "strike near Riyadh" framing. Separately, Yemeni government forces upgraded their territorial claim from this morning's Dhubab district to the full Red Sea port city of Mocha (Al Jazeera). Neither claim has independent corroboration — both remain combatant self-reports.

A new, non-Hormuz thread: asked whether he believes Iran was behind the Sept 30 Flydubai co-pilot attack-attempt, Trump said "I do. I personally do" — without elaborating on evidence. This directly contradicts VP Vance, who said he hadn't seen conclusive evidence tying Iran to the incident, and Israeli security officials, who assessed the Omani co-pilot acted alone (Times of Israel, TASS). Trump separately posted that gasoline prices are "no longer" being driven by the Strait of Hormuz, attributing them instead to Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries and California refinery closures — a rhetorical decoupling of Iran/Hormuz from oil prices that lands the same day a Houthi claim named an actual Aramco refinery.

Read: Today is the sharpest version yet of the shrug pattern this file has tracked, precisely because the claim was bigger and better-specified than anything digested this morning, and markets treated it identically to three vaguer prior days. That strengthens the "calibrated indifference, not systematic lateness" read, but it raises the stakes if that read is eventually wrong — a bigger miss compounding on three smaller ones is worse, not better, for the bear case. The Trump/Flydubai divergence is worth carrying on its own terms: a president making an unsupported personal claim that his own VP and an allied government's security services won't back is the mirror image of the faction-coherence question this file has long applied to Iran's own statements — US-side claims deserve the same evidentiary discipline. Bear case: a named-refinery strike claim plus an explicit threat to global aviation is a worse aggregate picture than this morning's framing, regardless of whether markets price it. Bull case: Hormuz's IMF PortWatch transit data still hasn't moved, and the fact that this bigger claim still didn't move the Polymarket contract (see below) is the cleanest evidence yet that its resolution criteria is narrowly and correctly scoped to the strait itself.
