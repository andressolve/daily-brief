Iran's Silence is the Signal

Four days have passed since the US expected a 48-hour MOU response. Iran has not formally responded through Pakistan. The Araghchi condemnation from Friday remains the last substantive Iranian diplomatic signal: "Every time a diplomatic solution is on the table, the U.S. opts for a reckless military adventure."

Read: Iran is not agonizing over the MOU terms — it is positioning for the Trump-Xi summit (May 14-15, T-5 days). Araghchi already met Wang Yi in Beijing on May 6. If China can extract better terms from Trump at the summit, Iran benefits by waiting. The silence is a bet on Chinese leverage, not factional deadlock.

The sporadic clashes in Hormuz confirm the "managed stalemate" is the functional reality: low-level naval exchanges within a "ceasefire" framework that neither side will formally declare dead. Iran seized the tanker Ocean Koi, accusing it of disrupting oil exports. The tit-for-tat seizure dynamic is now routine.

Lebanon Ceasefire Collapse — The Underreported Variable

Israel killed 19 people in Lebanon on May 9 — the worst single-day toll since the April 17 ceasefire. Three Israeli strikes hit the Saadiyat highway, ~20km south of Beirut, outside Hezbollah's traditional strongholds. Hezbollah responded with a drone targeting northern Israel.

Structural implication: Iran's counter-proposal for the MOU requires "binding no-attack guarantees for Iran and Lebanon." But Israel is actively escalating in Lebanon while the US pushes Iran to sign. This gives Iran a legitimate reason to delay: why sign a deal with a Lebanon component when the Lebanon ceasefire is being violated daily? Israel's escalation is not just a bilateral issue — it directly undermines the Iran deal architecture.

Intel-Apple: What It Means for the Stack

Apple and Intel reached a preliminary deal for Intel to manufacture Apple chips — likely M-class processors for Macs/iPads initially, potentially iPhone later (9to5Mac/WSJ). Intel stock hit all-time high $130.57, surpassing the August 2000 dot-com peak by 74%. The US government (Intel's largest shareholder under the Lip-Bu Tan deal) played a role in bringing Apple to the table.

Structural reads:

  • For TSMC: Loses Apple exclusivity. Apple diversifying foundry risk. TSMC's advanced node pricing power may weaken.
  • For NVDA/AMD: Intel foundry with Apple as anchor customer gains credible advanced-node path — longer-term competitive threat on silicon manufacturing.
  • For the AI trade: Validates the "infrastructure TAM broadening" thesis. The training-centric GPU phase is giving way to inference, edge, and enterprise — which require different architectures and benefit a wider range of companies.
