Iran / Middle East

Delta since Saturday morning: no new confirmed tanker attack; an Iran-Oman routing deal; a stale-but-real Vahidi backchannel scoop; a firmer USS Lincoln overboard confirmation plus a CENTCOM ship visit; Lebanon's death toll revised up with a provocation counter-claim; Bessent's bear case sharpened on the record.

The tanker-attack pattern paused — the cumulative ADNOC-vessel count holds at 15, with no new confirmed strike since Friday night. One data point, and this file isn't treating it as a resolved de-escalation; it's the first quiet 24-48 hours in the run of attacks this file has been tracking at increasing precision.

Separately, Iran and Oman reached a technical Hormuz routing deal — coordinated separate inbound/outbound shipping lanes — though Tehran still conditions full normalization on an end to the US blockade. Mechanics, not a breakthrough (Iran International).

The weekend's most analytically interesting item: an Axios scoop that IRGC commander Gen. Ahmad Vahidi privately told KRG President Nechirvan Barzani, on a May 14 call, that he "fully support[s]" Iran's negotiators and "prefer[s] to solve this crisis through negotiations." The call was arranged via a secret Trump-administration backchannel run through then-DNI Tulsi Gabbard (Axios).

Read carefully, not credulously: this cuts directly against the standing faction-map label for Vahidi ("war until objectives, non-negotiable") — but three separate flaws keep it from being a rewrite. It's three months stale (a May call, reported now). It's sourced to the Trump administration's own account of its own diplomacy, which has every incentive to frame itself as having quiet hardliner buy-in. And a follow-on secret meeting attempt in Erbil later collapsed over Iranian security concerns — suggesting whatever opening existed in May didn't hold. Logged as an open question in the faction map, not a resolved revision.

USS Abraham Lincoln: the story gets both its first real policy response and a firmer evidentiary floor. CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper made an in-person visit to the Lincoln on Saturday — his only ship stop on a 10-day regional tour. Separately, CNN independently confirmed via three US officials that a sailor did go overboard on Aug 3, a firmer status than Saturday's "unconfirmed" framing — though the broader "multiple suicide attempts" narrative in the original reporting remains unconfirmed (Task & Purpose).

Lebanon: the death toll was revised up to 11 (from 9), and Israel is now claiming the strikes were retaliation for a Hezbollah drone attack that wounded three IDF troops — a provocation counter-claim that complicates Saturday's "unprovoked escalation" framing. Hezbollah is threatening retaliation; an 8th round of Rome talks is set for September (France24).

Bessent's "unprecedented economic measures" got no new substance this weekend, but the bear case on them sharpened. Bloomberg Economics' Chris Kennedy went on record that the measures likely won't "materially change Iran's calculus" absent a broader Trump prioritization shift (Fortune).
