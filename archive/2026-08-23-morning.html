Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Saturday morning: A genuine domestic-stability data point surfaced (Kerman fuel crisis), Bessent's Monday package sharpened to target China, Iran finalized a new Oman trade deal, and Polymarket's Hormuz contract reversed again.

The Kerman fuel episode is this file's clearest domestic-stability data point in weeks, and it deserves more weight than a single-city pricing story would normally get. Authorities piloted a refinery-cost gasoline price (87,200 tomans/liter) in Kerman province on 8/13, then canceled it within hours after immediate public backlash; long lines, restricted smart-fuel-card allocations, and black-market gasoline reaching 110,000 tomans/liter have since been reported across multiple cities. Three regime MPs — Mirmohammadi Meybodi, Yousefi, and Haji Deligani — have since escalated their public warnings, with Mirmohammadi Meybodi explicitly citing the November 2019 gasoline protests (one of the regime's most violent domestic crackdowns) as the precedent to avoid; regime-affiliated outlet Jamaran separately warned a further price increase could turn "an economic decision into a national-security crisis."

President Pezeshkian has acknowledged the US naval blockade has cut off fuel-import routes and left the government without the forex reserves needed to buy gasoline abroad — a direct, on-the-record link between the Hormuz/blockade story and a domestic-stability risk this file has under-weighted. The external squeeze (Hormuz closure, near-zero oil exports, the incoming Bessent sanctions) and the internal fuel crisis are the same story from two ends, and it's Tehran's own MPs naming the 2019 precedent, not Western commentary projecting it onto them. This sharpens the standing faction-lens question: the diplomatic/IRGC split matters less if the more immediate threat to regime coherence is domestic economic pain from the blockade itself.

Bessent's Monday press conference now has a China-specific edge this file hadn't captured. Coverage previewing the 2pm ET Monday announcement frames it as pressuring China directly, not just isolating Iran bilaterally — China purchases over 80% of Iran's seaborne oil exports and "faces direct economic consequences" from the planned measures. Iran has "condemned looming US economic sanctions on the eve of Washington's planned announcement," threatening retaliation against countries that join the pressure campaign. If Monday's package genuinely targets Chinese buyers of Iranian crude, that is a materially larger, more consequential action than the Hezbollah-financing designation already logged as a partial, narrower delivery on the original "Economic D-Day" promise — still a test, not a given, per this file's standing base-rate skepticism on Bessent's dated promises.

Iran finalized a preferential trade agreement with Oman on 8/21, distinct from the already-tracked Hormuz shipping-route deal. The Trade Promotion Organization of Iran confirmed the agreement cleared administrative procedures and was sent to parliament for final review; it traces back to 18 MOUs signed during Pezeshkian's May 2025 Muscat visit, not a new negotiation. Worth naming the timing explicitly — Iran is finalizing a new preferential-trade channel with a neighbor in the same week Washington previews what it's calling its most sweeping sanctions push yet. This doesn't offset the scale of US pressure, but it's a genuine counter-data-point to a narrative of total, uniform economic isolation, consistent with the Iraq tanker exception logged 8/22. Two such threads in four days is worth tracking as a pattern.

Markets / Macro

Delta since Saturday morning: The real delta is underneath Saturday's headline number, not a new one — a genuine flash crash this file missed in real time.

BTC's Saturday rally and Saturday's flash crash are the same day, not competing stories — and the crash is the part this file's own Saturday-morning entry missed. Bitcoin cracked an intraday high near $79,500 before a sharp flash crash sent it briefly below $77,000 shortly after 04:00 UTC on 8/22; the broader crypto market cap fell from $2.68T to $2.55T in just six minutes — about $108B erased, the largest flash crash since October 2025 — with $1.8B in 24-hour liquidations affecting 286,130 traders, $448M of it long positions. XRP fell as much as -37% intraday, Solana -11.5%, Ethereum -5%, a far more violent move across the complex than BTC's own headline number implies. BTC recovered to close Saturday at ~$78,335 (+7.26%, the figure already logged).

This doesn't overturn the bullish ETF-inflow framing this file carried Saturday — the close held, and the $606M/83%-BlackRock inflow story is a real, separate catalyst — but it's a genuine complication that should have been caught in real time.
