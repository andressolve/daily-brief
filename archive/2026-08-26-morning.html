Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since last night: The Pakistan channel now has specific terms and Tehran hasn't accepted them. A new Iran-Oman corridor proposal emerged. The faction lens (Ghalibaf-hardline vs. Pezeshkian-conciliatory) needs revising — both are now converging on an economic-exhaustion argument.

The Pakistan-mediation channel now has real, specific terms on the table — and Tehran has explicitly not accepted them. Munir delivered a formal US offer to Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaee: end the naval blockade and lift sanctions, in exchange for reopening Hormuz and halting proxy attacks, built on the June MOU framework. Rezaee's response was "internal consultations," will "respond soon" — not an acceptance. Iran's negotiating team publicly reframed it as "the US must fulfill its commitments first," and Pezeshkian separately demanded the US "change its tone and approach." Read: this resolves last night's "promise vs. deliverable" open question — the promise now has actual terms, but Tehran's response is a stall/counter-demand, not the optimism last night's entry logged. This is a genuine downgrade, not a continuation.

A separate, more concrete de-escalation thread emerged overnight: Iran and Oman are negotiating directly on a temporary Hormuz corridor. Araghchi (Iran FM) and Busaidi (Oman FM) met in Tehran 8/25 and proposed a temporary joint navigation corridor through the strait plus a joint mine-clearance project; technical talks continue toward a permanent corridor. Oil dipped on the news. Read: worth keeping this analytically separate from the Pakistan/US channel above — this is a bilateral Iran-Oman technical/logistics track, not the US-Iran sanctions-for-Hormuz bargain. Two live channels working the same underlying problem from different angles is itself a data point that pressure is producing diplomatic activity, even though neither has produced a signed outcome.

Faction lens needs revising: Ghalibaf's recent rhetoric reads as converging with Pezeshkian's, not contrasting. Ghalibaf's line — "no matter how much military power we have, if people are hungry... we will not endure" — is now being amplified by Bessent himself as evidence US pressure is working, and it sits alongside Pezeshkian's own "end the war from a position of power and dignity" framing. Read: this file has run a clean "IRGC/hardline vs. diplomatic/conciliatory" split for weeks. That split looks less clean this morning — economic exhaustion may be becoming a cross-faction talking point, not a diplomatic-track-only one. Worth testing over the next few sessions whether this holds.
