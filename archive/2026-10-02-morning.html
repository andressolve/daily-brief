Iran / Geopolitics Correction: a real escalation this file missed, now flagged directly

Retroactive correction — not a delta, a miss Rubio ordered Iran's UN delegation to leave the US on the evening of Mon 9/28, after that day's talks stalled. This file's own 9/28-evening and 9/29-morning entries covered those exact dates and reported only "talks continued without a breakthrough" — materially softer than what actually happened. The story surfaced via Axios reporting dated 9/30-10/1, days after the event.

Sequence, now corrected: the White House believed Monday morning that progress was still possible; by late Monday afternoon, US officials concluded talks were at a stalemate; that evening, the US Mission to the UN told Iran's mission the delegation "had to leave New York right away" (Axios, The Hill). A US official told Axios Rubio's rationale was that the delegation had "overstayed their welcome." Araghchi's delegation departed for the airport hours later, boarding a flight to Doha at 1:20am Tuesday 9/29 (Washington Times). Iran disputes the framing, not the fact: Iran's UN mission posted on X that the delegation left "in accordance with the schedule... communicated to the US Department of State in advance on September 17" — a pre-arranged departure, not an expulsion, in their telling. Current status: Qatari mediators are continuing to work both sides' compromise proposal; no breakthrough and no new session has been reported since. Separately, Pezeshkian said (10/1) Iran "will continue to aim for dialogue" despite being "targeted... several times" during negotiations, while reporting describes both him and Araghchi under heavy pressure from hardliners not to negotiate at all — the ultraconservative Kayhan newspaper urged him to use the UN stage as "a courtroom for American crimes," and IRGC's Vahidi has signaled readiness to keep confronting the US (Iran International).

Read: This is a genuine process miss worth owning directly, per house style, rather than smoothing over — the file had the right dates in view and the wrong altitude: it tracked the diplomatic *content* dispute (uranium-transfer mechanism, delivery terms) and missed the harder *procedural* fact that the US ordered the other side's delegation out of the country mid-negotiation. That changes the read on everything logged since 9/28: "quiet, continuing, sequencing-not-substance" was too charitable a frame for a process the US side apparently regarded as broken down enough to warrant a diplomatic rebuke. Iran's "pre-scheduled, not expelled" counter-framing is itself informative — a government trying hard not to look expelled is a government trying to preserve the option of returning to the table without losing face, a mild bullish tell that the channel survived the incident rather than proof it didn't happen. The Pezeshkian/hardliner story is the sharper version of this file's standing faction question: an elected government arguing for dialogue against an IRGC/clerical bloc that would rather use the UN stage to prosecute the US than negotiate. Bear case: a delegation told to leave mid-talks, the same week hardliners are openly pressuring the president against talking at all, is a genuinely worse diplomatic picture than this file conveyed as of last night. Bull case: Qatari mediation continuing after the walkout, and Iran's own care to frame its departure as non-adversarial, both argue the channel survived rather than closed.
