Delta since this morning Iran confirmed receipt of the 4-nation proposal (no details); US "exploring" it and urged Israeli restraint; Trump confirmed he "hasn't finished retaliating"; tanker strike confirmed with steering-gear damage; weekly Hormuz traffic collapse quantified at -66%.

Iran's spokesman Baghaei confirmed the government received the four-nation ceasefire proposal but declined to give details on its contents or Iran's position. The US said it is "exploring" the proposal and separately urged Israeli restraint — a step beyond passive awareness, but still short of endorsement or a concrete counteroffer. At the same time, a senior US official reaffirmed that Trump "hasn't finished retaliating," and the tanker hit by an "unknown projectile" this morning is now confirmed to have sustained steering-gear damage. Lloyd's List Intelligence data quantifies the practical cost of all this: weekly Hormuz vessel traffic has collapsed roughly 66% (53 vessels vs. a normal 157).

Structural read Diplomatic-widening and hawkish-hardening are advancing on parallel tracks, not converging — consistent with the standing three-track (diplomatic/pragmatic-hawk/IRGC) framing. Iran confirming receipt of a proposal it won't detail is a data point about willingness to engage, not willingness to concede. The -66% traffic collapse is the clearest hard number yet that the practical cost of the standoff is large regardless of how the diplomacy resolves.
