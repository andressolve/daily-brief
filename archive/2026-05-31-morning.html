The hardening: tighter text, not a signature

Saturday closed with Trump's "blockade will now be lifted" Truth Social post still framing the narrative. Sunday flipped it. Per CNN's "Trump sent back Iran deal text with changes" report, the revised US text reportedly requires the enriched material at the struck Iranian sites to be "unearthed" by US personnel "in coordination with" Iran + IAEA and "DESTROYED" — operationally invasive language Iran has resisted since Round 1 in April. The new text also includes "No money will be exchanged, until further notice" — explicitly closing the frozen-funds release that the WH was internally debating Friday night. US officials told reporters Tehran "may take days" to respond. Friday's "blockade lifted" Truth Social was narrative; Sunday's revised text is contractual. The two are pointing in opposite directions, and the contractual one is what counts. The deal is now further from signature, not closer — which is the opposite of what Friday's Polymarket 81% print priced.

Ghalibaf rejects in advance

Sunday afternoon Tehran time, Parliament Speaker Mohammad-Baqer Ghalibaf — Trump's designated contact, the pragmatic-hawk faction leader who led the Islamabad delegation, the same Ghalibaf who mocked the April framework as "Operation Fauxios" the morning before its collapse — drew a public hard line:

"Tehran will not agree to any deal unless it fully secures Iranian rights. Our only criterion is to achieve tangible results before we fulfill our commitments." Parliament Speaker Mohammad-Baqer Ghalibaf, Sunday May 31 (via CNN)

The structural read: the most deal-friendly Iranian camp with formal authority — the pragmatic hawks — is publicly rejecting the new US text in advance of Tehran's formal response. Vahidi (hard-core IRGC) doesn't have to weigh in if Ghalibaf is doing the rejecting. This is the cleanest faction signal since Round 2 collapsed Apr 21.

Lebanon: Beaufort Castle, first time since 2000

The IDF captured Beaufort Castle on Sunday — the first time Israel has held it since the May 2000 withdrawal, a 26-year reset of the strategic geography of southern Lebanon. The IDF also secured Beaufort Ridge and Wadi al-Saluki. 1 IDF soldier was killed, 4 wounded — the first Israeli combat death since the south-Lebanon advance began. Tyre (Lebanon's fourth-largest city) was hit in a wave of strikes: 14 killed despite the legally-still-in-effect ceasefire. Netanyahu Sunday: "deepen and expand our hold."

The MOU's reported "ends the Israel-Hezbollah war" clause is now decisively contradicted by an Israeli advance deeper than at any point since the 2000 withdrawal. Lebanon will not sign a deal that ratifies Israel at Beaufort. Combined with the tightened MOU text, the deal architecture is collapsing on two axes simultaneously: nuclear/money on the US-Iran track, and Lebanon territory on the Israel-Hezbollah track.

Hormuz: blockade intact, Friday's "lifted" was theater

Despite Trump's Friday "Ships caught in the Strait due to our amazing and unprecedented Naval Blockade, which will now be lifted, may start the process of 'heading home!'" post, the blockade on vessels to/from Iranian ports remains operational. CENTCOM cumulative tally: ~84 vessels redirected, 4 disabled (some sources still cite 115+/6 — counts contested across CENTCOM and state-media — but the direction-of-travel is consistent: no ship has moved in either direction). Traffic still ~4 vessels/day vs ~95 normal. The WSJ reported the US is now "helping guide vessels going dark" through Hormuz — an admission the official blockade is being routed around by clandestine traffic, not lifted. Goldman's "sloppy peace" upgrades again to "sloppy peace with clandestine bypass." Friday's Truth Social post is the closest empirical confirmation we've gotten that the announcement was rhetoric, not policy.
