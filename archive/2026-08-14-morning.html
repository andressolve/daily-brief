Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night: a third chokepoint attack overnight, this time with a clean IRGC attribution; Bessent previewing "unprecedented economic measures"; broad Gulf-state condemnation; Polymarket and oil both unmoved.

A third chokepoint-adjacent attack, and for the first time a clean attribution. Two ADNOC-linked commercial tankers were struck overnight; UAE reporting attributes the attack directly to the IRGC — a harder, more specific attribution than the 8/8 ADNOC tanker incident (flagged but not conclusively sourced) or the Bab el-Mandeb Houthi strike (Iran-linked only by proxy). Gulf states and Western framing treat this explicitly as piracy/unprovoked aggression, citing UN Security Council Resolution 2817 (Fox News).

Read: the attribution chain is tightening with each incident. This is no longer "attacks in the region with ambiguous responsibility" — it's a UAE government naming the IRGC directly for a third distinct chokepoint incident since early July.

Gulf-state condemnation is broad and explicit, not just US/UAE framing. Qatar called it "a severe violation of international law"; Bahrain urged the UN Security Council toward "firm and deterrent measures" against what it termed "economic blackmail"; Kuwait and Egypt also condemned the attack. This is the widest simultaneous Gulf-state condemnation logged this cycle — previously individual states reacted to specific incidents; this is a bloc-level response, raising the diplomatic cost to Iran of continuing this pattern.

Bessent is preparing "unprecedented economic measures" against Iran, with announcements expected next week — the most consequential single data point of the morning. The standing threads have been military/naval (the blockade, CENTCOM enforcement) and diplomatic (Trump/Araghchi rhetoric); this is the first concrete signal of a forthcoming Treasury-led economic escalation, additive to both (Fox News). If it lands as previewed, it would be a genuine escalation-of-kind on the sanctions axis, parallel to — not a substitute for — the naval blockade.

Vance and Trump both restated hardline framing: Vance said the US will emerge "in a stronger position, particularly with respect to Tehran's nuclear program and stable fuel prices"; Trump reiterated his "total control" claim. Iran's position: the strait "will not be reopened until Iran's conditions are accepted." UKMTO's running tally: since July 6, twenty ships have experienced projectile strikes, sixteen along the Southern Omani route specifically, with operators now favoring alternate northern routes (Fox News).

Cross-market: the decoupling thesis just passed its hardest test yet. A genuinely new, cleanly-attributed kinetic attack produced zero movement in Polymarket's Hormuz contract (still 45.5%) and oil is reportedly "little changed" despite the attack and Bessent's economic-isolation threat. Either markets have priced maximum expected severity already, or there's real risk of underpricing tail escalation — both readings are live; this file leans toward the former given the pattern's consistency across a dozen-plus sessions, but flags the latter as the bear case.

No update this morning on Gaza, CENTCOM's blockade tally, or the parliamentary Hormuz bill — carried forward unchanged.
