Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night: Hormuz deal terms now public for the first time; new unclaimed strike near Al Khasab; new THAAD/Patriot depletion story reframes Iran's vessel-targeting threat.

Hormuz deal terms are public for the first time: a 60-day interim mechanism — inbound traffic via a northern lane in Iranian waters, outbound via a southern lane in Omani waters "in coordination with Iran," no tolls for the 60-day window, mine clearance of the median lane within 30 days, then a permanent two-way arrangement. Not yet signed; reporting frames it as "close," attributed to envoy Witkoff's negotiating track. Axios, Al Jazeera, Jerusalem Post, Israel Hayom, The National.

The read here matters: this resolves rather than vindicates the standing bear case. Bessent's no-toll claim holds up against the actual published terms. But the lane structure — Iran administering, and effectively holding veto power over, the inbound corridor — confirms the bear case's core objection: this is a negotiated, Iran-gated arrangement, not restored "freedom of movement" through international waters. Now that structure is on the record for the first time, the debate can be conducted on terms instead of rhetoric — a genuine change in the shape of the argument, not a resolution of who's right.

Separately, and on a parallel track: a cargo ship was hit by an unknown projectile overnight, ~37km NE of Al Khasab, Oman, and lost power — the second unclaimed strike at this exact chokepoint in under a week, distinct from the already-logged 7/31 Khasab tanker strike. Diplomatic optimism and ambiguous strikes are running in parallel, not in sequence — the deal track is advancing at the same time unexplained attacks continue at the same location, which argues against reading either track as dispositive of the other.

New structural fact: CNN reports the US has expended ~80% of its THAAD interceptor stock and ~50% of its Patriot stock since Feb 28; CSIS estimates 3+ years to rebuild THAAD inventory. This reframes Rezaei's vessel-targeting threat (logged last night) — and plausibly explains why the US is pushing hard for a deal this specific week, rather than holding out for better terms from a position of comfortable strength.

Polymarket's Hormuz contract: 62.0% Yes, +1.5pp overnight — now pricing the newly-public deal structure, not just rhetoric, a different kind of signal than prior sessions' rhetoric-driven jumps. No update on the Kuwait/CENTCOM damage dispute, Bahrain, Egypt, or the Mojtaba Khamenei incapacity thread — all carry forward unresolved.
