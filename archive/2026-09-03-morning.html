Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night: US struck Iranian government tankers directly for the first time this cycle. Iran's retaliation widened to four countries (Jordan, Kuwait, Bahrain, Iraq). Death toll now 11 per Al Jazeera. Rial broke 2.2M/USD. Full detail in state/iran.md.

The tanker strikes are a genuine escalation in target selection — the first time this cycle the US has directly targeted Iranian state assets rather than military hardware. US drones fired missiles into the engine rooms of two Iranian government tankers anchored off Iran's coast, north of the blockade line. Axios and Iran International both confirm this is the first time the US has struck Iranian tankers specifically in retaliation for attacks on shipping, distinct from prior blockade-enforcement actions — one US official described the rationale as reducing the risk of further Iranian attacks on tankers, Navy ships, and aircraft, "to mow the lawn." This sits alongside the broader ~100-target CENTCOM strike wave (IRGC air-defense, radar, mine-laying, comms, anti-ship missile, and attack-drone systems) already underway. Read: every prior US strike this cycle has targeted Iranian military or proxy hardware; directly hitting two government-owned tankers is a step toward pressuring Iran's own state economic assets. Worth watching whether this becomes a repeated tactic — which would materially raise the stakes for Iran's remaining tanker fleet and its already-crippled oil-export capacity — or was a one-off action. (Axios, Iran International, TWZ)

Iran's retaliation spread to four countries, not two — Kuwait and Bahrain are new named targets. Iran fired roughly 25 ballistic missiles at bases in Jordan and Kuwait, plus about two dozen drones at Bahrain (targeting radar installations and Sheikh Isa airbase, where Iran says US forces are stationed, plus a separate ballistic-missile attack on a US Navy site in Bahrain), alongside continued strikes on Iraq. Bahrain says its air defenses intercepted the drones; roughly half the Jordan-bound missiles reportedly reached Jordanian airspace, with most intercepted. No US casualties reported. Al Jazeera calls this "the heaviest exchange of fire between the US and Iran in more than a month," with an aggregate US-strike death toll of 11 (including the previously-named Sirik wedding victims). Read: this is a real widening of the theater, not just a bigger strike on the same two countries — Tehran has now demonstrated willingness and capability to hit US-linked targets in four separate Gulf/Levant states in a single retaliatory wave. Consistent with, and sharper than, the standing read that Iran is reusing and scaling up its base-strike playbook rather than escalating to a qualitatively new target class (e.g., Kharg Island) on its own initiative. (Al Jazeera)

The rial hit a fresh record above 2.2M/USD, with the renewed strikes named as the explicit driver. An acceleration from last night's 2.15-2.22M range and from 8/31's ~2.111M — a third consecutive session of record lows tracking directly with the escalation. (Al Arabiya)

The bear case on the desensitization thesis: a genuinely wider war — new target classes on the US side, four countries hit on Iran's side — with markets shrugging is not obviously bullish. It could mean the market has correctly identified this as a contained, bounded exchange with a durable ceiling (Gulf shadow-fleet oil flows, no Kharg strike, no full strait closure). Or it could mean risk is being systematically mispriced and the eventual correction, when it comes, will be sharp and sudden rather than gradual. Nothing this morning resolves which read is correct — see the Polymarket section below for the sharpest version of this tension.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last night: NVIDIA confirmed the $12.93B Hugging Face acquisition. Broadcom's AI-specific print keeps beating even as the stock falls on the narrower consolidated guide. Korea/Taiwan chips partially rebounded from yesterday's CXMT selloff.

NVIDIA confirmed the $12.93B Hugging Face acquisition overnight — from three weeks of rumor to a signed deal, and the deal structure is itself a signal. NVIDIA's official blog confirmed the acquisition, with Jensen Huang framing NVIDIA's role as strengthening infrastructure, reliability, safety, and deployment while Hugging Face retains its brand and independence. Hugging Face hosts 3M+ models and 500K+ datasets for 18M+ developers across 200K+ companies against ~$150M of disclosed annual revenue — an ~86x revenue multiple justified only by strategic platform value. Notably, this is structured as a conventional acquisition rather than the license+acqui-hire pattern NVIDIA used for Groq, Poolside, and Rebellions specifically to reduce antitrust/HSR exposure. Read: this is NVIDIA reaching for the open-model/open-dataset distribution layer as a hedge against custom-silicon substitution risk — the same strategic logic flagged when talks first surfaced. But choosing a conventional structure here, after avoiding it three times before, suggests NVIDIA judged this deal either too clean to need the workaround or too strategically important to structure around review. Worth watching for regulatory commentary given NVIDIA increasingly owns both compute capacity and the open-model distribution layer sitting on top of it — a fresh data point for the same platform-concentration thread as the NVIDIA revenue-share-pause story.

Broadcom's AI numbers keep beating even as the stock prices the narrower consolidated guide miss — the same "world vs. market" pattern this file has tracked all cycle (AMAT, Marvell, and AVGO's own June print). FY27 AI target of >$100B reaffirmed, not raised, for the second consecutive quarter. Read: this confirms only the original, already-known $35B vehicle — not the newly-reported $60-100B expansion (still unconfirmed by all four parties, see Private Credit below).
