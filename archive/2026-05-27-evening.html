What Happened After Morning

Trump at cabinet meeting (PBS News): Declared Iran would not receive sanctions relief in exchange for surrendering enriched uranium. "They're going to give up their highly enriched uranium, not for sanctions relief. No, no, not at all." Also called Iran "negotiating on fumes" and said he's "in no rush" for a deal. This is a HARDER LINE than the morning's "not yet satisfied" — it explicitly rejects a core MOU provision (sanctions waivers during negotiations).

Netanyahu rejected the Lebanon clause: Said the ceasefire "does not include Lebanon" and Israel will intensify strikes against Hezbollah. The MOU's language about ending military operations "on every front, including Lebanon" is now contested by a party that wasn't at the table but has veto power over implementation.

Structural read: both sides are using the leak to demonstrate leverage, not walk away. Iran leaked to create momentum. Trump hardened to show he won't be boxed in. Netanyahu rejected to protect Israeli freedom of action in Lebanon. None of these moves are deal-killers — they're standard negotiation behavior after a premature public disclosure. But they mean the "clean deal" path the morning priced is messier than it looked.
