Iran / Middle East

Delta since Friday evening: Araghchi broadened Iran's reopening conditions to Lebanon and sanctions relief; the IRGC publicly decoupled itself from the Oman track; the UAE accused the IRGC of missile-striking an ADNOC tanker; Gaza saw no further movement.

Saturday produced the clearest faction-lens evidence of the week — and, for once, a live kinetic example rather than just contradictory rhetoric. FM Araghchi told reporters the Oman arrangement is "very close to the final result," but stressed full reopening depends on "other conditions and compensation for the violation of the memorandum of understanding by the United States" — and for the first time named the specific grievances: the US backing a southern route without Tehran's coordination, the US failing to halt Israeli strikes on Lebanon, and Washington's refusal to unfreeze Iranian sanctioned assets. This is a materially broader ask than Friday's ship-ban/cargo-fee dispute — Iran is now bundling Hormuz normalization with the Lebanon front and sanctions relief, using its one remaining chokepoint-leverage asset well outside shipping.

Separately, IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said reopening depends on Washington accepting Iran's conditions and is "not related to" the Iran-Oman negotiations — a direct, public statement that the faction which actually enforces the strait does not consider itself bound by whatever Araghchi and Oman agree. This confirms, from the IRGC's own mouth rather than inferred from its absence, the IRGC-delegation-gap thread flagged repeatedly this week. Iran's parliamentary national security spokesman Qashqavi separately confirmed 8/7 that the Oman framework is agreed and pending approval from "higher levels" — incremental progress, not ratification.

Hours after Araghchi's "very close" framing, the UAE said Iran fired a missile at an ADNOC tanker transiting the Strait. ADNOC confirmed a vessel was targeted in the early hours of Saturday; no injuries, situation "brought under control." The UAE Foreign Ministry called it a violation of UN Security Council Resolution 2817 and explicitly accused the IRGC — not "Iran" generically — of an act of piracy, using the strait "as a tool for economic pressure or blackmail." Qatar's foreign ministry said it "rejects using the Strait of Hormuz as a bargaining chip," and Bahrain called on the UN Security Council to compel Iran to reopen the strait and halt attacks on commercial shipping. No Iranian confirmation, denial, or IRGC claim of responsibility has surfaced as of this run. Read: naming the IRGC specifically — not "Iran" or "unknown projectile," as with the 8/3 Minoan Pioneer incident — sharpens the same-day contradiction. A Gulf state is now publicly attributing an attack to the exact faction that, hours earlier, was independently declaring itself outside the negotiating track. This is a stronger, more concrete version of the "which Iran is speaking, and does it have authority to speak" problem than anything logged this week.

Iran's own traffic claims don't match independent tracking — a credibility gap worth naming, not just a negotiating position. Araghchi said traffic under the Islamabad MOU's Clause 5 reached "60% of normal conditions within two weeks" before the US allegedly tried to establish new routes. Independent trackers (UKMTO, Windward, NBC's tracker) instead show the strait still running at a small fraction of the ~70/day pre-war baseline — single-to-low-double-digit transits as of early August. If Iran's public data claims don't match tracked reality on the one metric that's independently verifiable, that should discount the credibility of its other claims by a similar margin.
