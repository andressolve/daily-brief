Iran / Yemen Territorial claims, Pentagon on record, Hormuz data unchanged

Delta since Sunday morning Yemen offensive claims territory (Dhubab, Bab al-Mandeb). Pentagon confirms carrier deployment on record for the first time. JCS Chairman quantifies the missile-free streak at -86% from Day 1. Hormuz transit count unchanged.

Yemen's government (PLC chief Rashad al-Alimi) says it has retaken Dhubab district and positions near the Bab al-Mandeb strait from the Houthis, and opened a new "strategic offensive" toward Houthi-held Sanaa; the Saudi-led coalition claims roughly 100 fighter jets in support and "air protection" secured over the strait. Al Jazeera's own reporting is explicit that it could not independently verify the battlefield claims (Al Jazeera). The Houthis countered with their own unverified claim of seizing a highway linking Taiz to Aden (CNN). Neither side's casualty figures from the weekend (700 claimed vs. ~30 independent) have gained any new corroboration.

The Pentagon crossed from leak to on-record confirmation. A Pentagon spokesperson confirmed, for the first time officially rather than via anonymous sourcing, that the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group and USS Makin Island amphibious readiness group are deploying — roughly 9,000 personnel (7,000 Navy, 2,000 Marines), bringing total US regional presence to ~20,000, with arrival possible by the end of October, earlier than the "late November" figure this file had been carrying (CBS News). Defense Secretary Hegseth separately told reporters Saturday the Hormuz blockade is "ironclad" and that Trump has "all the options he needs on the table."

Iran's own restraint got its first official quantification: Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine stated Iranian missile launches are down 86% from Day 1 levels (GlobalSecurity.org) — a harder data point than any single-sourced "Iran preparing for major fighting" claim this file has had to weigh it against. Hormuz itself remains unchanged by the hardest available metric: IMF PortWatch's latest count is still just 1 transit (Sep 27) against an 85/day pre-war baseline, with 247 AIS-visible ships now holding position nearby and jammed-vessel signals spiking (IMF PortWatch). The rial sits at ~2.693M/$, roughly flat to slightly weaker versus Sunday's 2.686M (Pashizi). No new faction-level signal from Khamenei's office, the IRGC, or Pezeshkian since Sunday.

Read: Today's most important fact may be what didn't move: markets. Oil falling, not spiking, on a day with a live Yemen offensive and a claimed strike toward the Saudi capital is the sharpest version yet of this war's eight-month non-reaction pattern — for the first time, the market moved the wrong direction relative to the headline. Three threads worth holding separately: Yemen is now a territorial war (Dhubab and the approaches to Bab al-Mandeb are a different order of stakes than contested casualty counts, since that strait — not Hormuz — is the Red Sea chokepoint); US force posture crossed from leak to confirmation; and Iran's own restraint remains the most falsifiable fact in the file. Bear case: a government claiming it retook ground near Bab al-Mandeb, a Pentagon confirming nine thousand troops inbound, and a Houthi claim of reaching Riyadh's suburbs is a worse aggregate picture than this file had Friday — and markets shrugging it off for an eighth straight month doesn't mean the risk isn't there. Bull case: Hormuz's 1-transit-vs-85 reading is unchanged, not worse, meaning the Yemen front isn't yet migrating into the actual chokepoint this war has closed — and an 86% missile reduction from Iran's own forces is hard to square with the Camp David "considering resuming major combat operations" framing unless Yemen is substituting for, not building toward, direct Iran-US escalation.

Bottom line / Portfolio implication

Monday was the clearest test this file has had in weeks, and the market passed it in a way that cuts against the intuitive read: a government claiming new Yemeni territory near a strategic strait, a Pentagon confirming nine thousand troops inbound, and a Houthi claim of reaching toward Riyadh all landed on a session where oil fell and equities rose. That is not complacency by itself — IMF PortWatch's Hormuz-transit data is genuinely unchanged, and the contract most plausibly tied to actual news (the Fed's) moved exactly as it should have on real data. The structural read worth carrying forward: this market can and does distinguish between news that matters to a specific, narrow resolution question and news that merely sounds dramatic — which argues for taking Polymarket's calibration more seriously, not less, even as the underlying war keeps adding real facts (an 86%-down missile streak, a newly confirmed carrier deployment, a sponsor-equity name sliding toward a 30% drawdown with no catalyst). Nothing here changes the zero-position thesis. Portfolio: zero positions, 144 sessions, unchanged.
