Iran / Middle East

Delta since this morning: CENTCOM denied damage on the Kuwait claim (not the strike itself); Iran explicitly denied the Egypt/Damietta strike this morning's brief attributed to it — a real correction, not just an update.

Kuwait, partially addressed: CENTCOM stated on X that "no US aircraft were destroyed or damaged in recent attempted Iranian attacks... all missiles and drones were intercepted or failed to reach targeted areas" — a direct rebuttal of the IRGC's claim to have destroyed hangars, satcom equipment, and fuel tanks at Ali Al Salem and Ahmad al-Jaber. That's not a clean confirm/deny of whether a strike was attempted at all — CENTCOM's own phrase "attempted Iranian attacks" implies something was launched — but it resolves the damage question in the US's favor. Context filled in tonight: this is stated Iranian retaliation for a US "heavy wave" of strikes late Wednesday night that hit dozens of IRGC targets, killing an Iranian family of three (including a 2-year-old) on Qeshm Island.

The correction that actually matters: Egypt. This morning's brief described Egypt as a new front Iran opened. That's wrong on attribution. The Damietta drone strike on the tanker Energos Winter and a second vessel happened Wednesday, Egypt's cabinet called it an unclaimed, unidentified drone, and Iran has explicitly denied involvement. The strike itself is real and a genuine new Suez-adjacent shipping-risk data point — it just isn't Iran's, on the record. Folding it into "Iran's widening offensive" without that denial attached overstated the morning's read.

Revised structural read: the "breadth over depth" thesis from this morning survives, but on a thinner evidentiary base than presented — one contested/undamaged Kuwait claim, no confirmed Bahrain detail, and an Egypt strike Iran itself denies. The single highest-value unresolved fact remains a clean CENTCOM/Kuwait confirm-or-deny of what, if anything, actually landed.

Markets / Macro

S&P 500 +0.7% to 7,489.72, Nasdaq +1.0% to 25,373.85, Dow +0.53% to 52,485.03 (its fourth straight winning month) — a close that treated the day's Iran news as a non-event. MU reversed hard, from +3.24% premarket to -3.16% at the close, with no distinct new catalyst found — worth a clean look tomorrow morning.

Oil gave back essentially its entire premarket spike: Brent closed back near $86 (from $92.27 premarket, roughly a -6.7% intraday round-trip); WTI closes conflict across sources (~$85-86.80). July as a whole was still oil's strongest month since March, +20%+. The 30Y closed at 5.28%, up from this morning's already-19-year-high 5.23% — long rates kept climbing even as equities rallied hard and oil's risk premium faded. No single dated catalyst explains that combination; flagging honestly rather than forcing a story. VIX, gold, and BTC were not independently re-verified tonight — an evening-scope gap, first task tomorrow morning.

Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

The tape won today, not the war. Equities closed near records, AMZN's beat only got bigger, and oil gave back its entire escalation premium — despite more confirmed US strikes on Iran and a still-unresolved Kuwait claim. But two things keep this from reading as a clean "escalation is priced out" story: the 30Y kept climbing to a fresh high even as everything else rallied (a genuine, unexplained divergence worth tracking, not smoothing over), and this morning's own read on Iran's offensive breadth needed correcting — the Egypt front wasn't Iran's, on the record. Zero positions, 55th session.
