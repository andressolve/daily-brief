Trump's Abraham Accords Expansion

This is the most analytically significant development today. Trump posted on Truth Social: "I am mandatorily requesting that all Countries immediately sign the Abraham Accords." He named Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Pakistan, Egypt, Turkey, Jordan — and extended the offer to Iran itself.

Two reads on this:

  • Bull (masterplan): Trump is packaging Iran peace inside a broader regional normalization. Saudi Arabia joining Abraham Accords = the ultimate diplomatic prize. Makes the deal harder to oppose domestically (bigger than just Iran). Increases political payoff exponentially.
  • Bear (scope creep): Adding 6+ countries and demanding Abraham Accords participation introduces massive new complexity. Saudi Arabia has never signed. If this becomes an actual precondition (not aspiration), the timeline stretches significantly and Polymarket is too hot.

Assessment: The language is aspirational, not conditional. "One or two may have a reason for not doing so, and that will be accepted." The bilateral Iran MOU can proceed independently. But watch Saudi Arabia's response — if MBS signals willingness, the regional architecture becomes real.

Rubio vs. Iran: Confidence Gap

Rubio from India: deal could be finalized "today." Describes a "pretty solid thing on the table" re: Hormuz opening + "very real, significant, time-limited negotiation on nuclear matters."

Iran FM spokesman Baghaei: "No one" can say finalization is "imminent." Classic negotiating posture — maintain leverage, don't look eager. Not a walk-away signal.

Cross-Beat Read: The Macro Regime Shift

If Brent stays below $100, this is the most significant macro development since the ceasefire began. The causal chain: deal → Hormuz reopening → oil supply normalization → lower energy costs → lower inflation expectations → rate-cut hopes revive → growth/tech bid strengthens. This is the opposite of the Apr 8–May 15 regime (war → high oil → inflation fear → rate hikes priced → tech drag).

Bear case on the oil move: The physical Hormuz situation hasn't changed. Iran FM says deal "not imminent." IRGC still claims sovereignty via PGSA. Oil is trading the announcement, not the implementation. If deal stalls, Brent snaps back above $105 violently. The gap between market pricing (deal done) and diplomatic reality (still negotiating) is the widest since Apr 17.
