Iran Legislating Hormuz Control

Ebrahim Azizi, head of Iran's parliamentary national security commission, told state television the armed forces would be the authority for the Strait under proposed legislation. The law would prohibit passage of "hostile vessels" and mandate all transit fees be paid in Iranian rials (Times of Israel, BSSNEWS).

Structural read: This is the Goldman "sloppy peace" made concrete. Iran is building permanent legal infrastructure around Hormuz control. Combined with the existing toll revenue (including crypto payments), there is now a bureaucratic, legal, and financial constituency for maintaining the closure. This is not the behavior of a country preparing to negotiate Hormuz away — it is the behavior of a country institutionalizing its control claim.

UAE Leaves OPEC — The Cartel Fractures

The UAE announced it will leave OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1 (NPR, CNBC, Bloomberg). Third-largest OPEC producer after Saudi Arabia and Iraq. Member for nearly 60 years. Official statement: "reflects the UAE's long-term strategic and economic vision." Real drivers: production quota frustration (UAE wants 5M bpd by 2027) and Iran's missile/drone attacks on UAE territory.

Why this matters beyond oil: The Iran war is not just disrupting energy supply — it is dismantling the institutional framework that manages global oil. If OPEC's third-largest member walks because of Hormuz, the cartel's ability to coordinate production is structurally diminished. Long-term: more potential supply (bearish oil). Short-term: structural uncertainty and loss of coordination (bullish oil). Watch for Saudi Arabia's reaction.

International Fracture at the UN

Bahrain convened a high-level UN meeting demanding Iran reopen Hormuz. Most countries supported the resolution. Russia and China blamed the US and Israel, not Iran — attributing the blockade to "American and Israeli actions." The Bahrain-sponsored statement notably omitted mention of the US blockade (NPR). UN Secretary-General Guterres warned of the "worst supply chain disruption since COVID-19" (Al Jazeera).

Gold's Plunge Is the Signal

When gold falls 2%+ on a day of maximum geopolitical uncertainty, the market is telling you its primary fear has shifted from conflict to inflation. Oil at $111 means Hormuz is no longer just a geopolitical story — it is an economic emergency. The market is pricing energy-driven inflation as the dominant risk, not escalation. This changes the political calculus: domestic energy costs may force the US to prioritize Hormuz resolution over nuclear.

EIA noted Brent spot surging past futures price — backwardation signals physical tightness in the crude market (EIA). This is structural, not speculative. Consistent with IEA's 13M bpd lost assessment and Pentagon's 6-month mine-clearing estimate.
