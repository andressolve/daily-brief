Iran / Middle East

Delta since Saturday morning: the strikes that dominated Saturday's brief were called off overnight — a genuine, if provisional, de-escalation, layered with a sharpened Kuwait dispute and a widening CENTCOM-vs-shipping-data gap on Hormuz.

Strikes called off, not confirmed by Iran. Trump posted Saturday night that the US remains "locked and loaded and ready to go" but is standing down because the "perimeters of a deal" have been agreed, contingent on Iran "rapidly" finalizing terms — full reopening of Hormuz plus an end to Iran's nuclear program. Saudi Crown Prince MBS reportedly phoned Trump to push for the pause. Read skeptically: Iranian state media has given no confirmation that Tehran requested the halt or agreed to these specific terms — WSJ reported Trump had authorized the strike plan, CBS reported no final approval had been given; even "how close it came" isn't cleanly resolved.

Hormuz: CENTCOM's "open" claim needs a caveat this brief didn't have Saturday morning. CENTCOM posted Sunday that the strait is "open to all vessels," "Iran does not control the strait," and "traffic is flowing" — a direct rebuttal of Friday's Strait Authority "not possible" declaration. But independent shipping-data trackers report actual commercial traffic down ~90% even as CENTCOM insists it's open — a material gap, not a rounding difference. Iran's Strait Authority is also still requiring ships to use Iran-designated routes/insurance, which Secretary Rubio called "unacceptable." Treat CENTCOM's "open" as technically true but materially misleading — the operative fact is the ~90% volume collapse, not the binary open/closed framing either side is arguing.

Kuwait dispute sharpened, not resolved. Kuwait's own authorities now confirm injuries (3, minor) and damage from Aug 1 drone activity — directly contradicting CENTCOM's still-standing position that "no US aircraft were destroyed or damaged." Kuwait's own account of its own soil is the stronger source, but this brief won't declare CENTCOM wrong outright.

Talks happened, but the structural obstacle to closing "rapidly" is now visible. Iranian FM Araghchi called Turkey, Pakistan, and Saudi officials; Interior Minister Momeni traveled to Islamabad to meet PM Shehbaz Sharif, with Pakistan acting as intermediary. A Pakistani official said there is "apparently no one decision-making command structure" on the Iranian side and responses take "2 to 3 days" — a real structural problem for Trump's demand of a rapid deal.

A correction to this brief's own standing framing: the "Momeni/Mojtaba succession thread," carried as an open item for weeks, was never actually a succession contest. Mojtaba Khamenei has been Supreme Leader since March 2026; the live issue is that he was reportedly badly injured in the war's opening strikes, has not appeared publicly since, and communicates only via IRGC intermediaries, with actual authority concentrating in the Supreme National Security Council/IRGC. This is a more plausible explanation for the "no one decision-making command structure" problem Pakistan's mediator flagged than bureaucratic friction — a genuine leadership-incapacity vacuum at the top of the Iranian state. Flagged as a correction per house calibration rules — sourced but not independently verified beyond the cited reporting, treat as best-available read, not confirmed fact.

Other data points: NCRI reports point-to-point inflation at 83.9% (food over 100%), and a state-TV broadcast cut on Speaker Ghalibaf after hardline-MP criticism of the diplomatic track — visible factional strain consistent with, not proof of, the command-vacuum read. Bahrain and Egypt threads unchanged from Saturday. US embassy/travel warnings remain in effect region-wide. The single highest-value fact to chase before Monday's open: does Iran's side confirm or deny the deal-perimeter Trump described, and does shipping volume recover toward CENTCOM's characterization or stay near the reported ~90% collapse.
