Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night FM Araghchi formally rejected the US ceasefire proposal on Hormuz-sovereignty grounds; Trump's "massive attack" rhetoric stayed rhetorical; CENTCOM's 13th strike night; Iran retaliated on Bahrain/Kuwait/Jordan; new tanker incidents near Oman and Larak Island.

The single most important delta of the morning: FM Abbas Araghchi rejected the US ceasefire proposal, delivered via a channel that ran through Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi — who visited Trump at the White House, then flew to Tehran to meet President Pezeshkian. Araghchi called the US approach "irrational, excessive, and hegemonic" and said Iran isn't interested in a "temporary deal" that leaves control of the Strait of Hormuz unresolved (JPost, Haaretz). This is structurally more significant than an IRGC rejection would have been: it's the pragmatist-track foreign minister, not a hardliner, declining the deal on a substantive sovereignty issue.

Trump's Thursday-afternoon "massive attack" rhetoric (Axios) has not converted to any operational signal overnight — no new carrier movements or Israeli mobilization found; the existing posture (Abraham Lincoln + George H.W. Bush carrier strike groups, a third carrier reportedly moving toward theater) predates the Axios remarks. Israel raised its alert status and IDF Chief Zamir reaffirmed readiness to "immediately return to war," but conducted no strikes; no Iranian projectile reached Israeli territory. CENTCOM confirmed a 13th consecutive night of strikes — command centers, drone storage, comms, coastal surveillance, maritime capability — incremental, not a new tier. Iran retaliated Friday AM with IRGC drone strikes on US assets in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan — a repeat of the established pattern.

The Red Sea/Hormuz front widened further: IRGC claims a tanker was set ablaze near Oman on the southern Hormuz transit route, plus two tankers reported leaking near Larak Island. The Houthi blockade (declared 7/20) is visibly biting — Hormuz crossings fell to just 1 tanker on July 23, the lowest since May 7. Domestically, the regime executed Mehdi Khanaki (protest-related), bringing uprising-linked executions to 24.

Structural read Today's Araghchi rejection is the clearest evidence yet that the standing "three-track, not converging" model undersells how unified the regime is on the Hormuz-sovereignty question specifically. Track divergence is real on tone and tactics, but not on the substance of what Iran will concede. The next real test is whether Trump's rhetoric converts to an operational decision, not just a repeated threat.
