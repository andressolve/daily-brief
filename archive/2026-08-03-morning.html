Bottom Line / Portfolio Implication

Sunday's brief flagged two questions as the highest-value facts to chase before Monday's open: does Iran confirm or deny Trump's deal framing, and does Hormuz shipping recover. Both resolved today, and both cut the same direction — Iran denied the talks/deal claim outright, and shipping traffic logged a third straight session near-total collapse. Yet oil sold off hard anyway, and equities rallied intraday, because markets had already front-run the de-escalation on Saturday's stand-down announcement; today's Iranian pushback is a data point the tape hasn't fully absorbed yet, not a reversal it has priced. The more structurally important story today may not be Iran at all — it's CoreWeave's $2.6B term loan pricing wide of talk, the first time this cycle's vendor-financing-fragility watch has gotten a primary-market credit signal instead of an equity or CDS proxy. That's a genuine escalation (Watch to Moderate-Strong), worth tracking independent of how the Iran story resolves. Two threads to hold in tension this week: whether Iran's denial eventually forces a repricing of Monday's oil move, and whether CoreWeave's term-loan aftermarket trading confirms or fades the credit-stress signal.
