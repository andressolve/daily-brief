Iran / Middle East

Delta since this morning: The physical shutdown at Hormuz — not the MOU expiry or Trump's Oman threat — is the day's real story.

MarineTraffic and Kpler data confirm Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic nearly stopped over the weekend: just 13 vessel transits total (3 on Sunday), zero recorded commodity-vessel transits on Aug 16, down from 31 transits the prior weekend and against a historical baseline of ~130/day. Shipping through the strait is now down roughly 90% since the war began Feb 28. This didn't appear in this morning's brief, which led instead with the MOU's formal expiration and Trump's threat to "bomb" Oman if its mediation "gets in the way."

Correction to this morning's framing: the MOU expiry and Oman threat were the rhetorical layer sitting on top of a physical collapse that was already underway independent of anything said Monday. The hard vessel-count data is the more important fact — worth carrying forward as the dominant Iran data point, not the political theater around it.

Bessent's "unprecedented" sanctions, previewed 8/14 as landing "next week," did not land today — the window named for delivery has now technically closed without action, sharpening rather than just repeating the standing skepticism. No update tonight on the Vahidi backchannel story or Lebanon.

Markets / Macro

Delta since this morning: The AI-led green open didn't survive contact with the bond market. The 30-year yield, not Iran, drove the close.

S&P 500 closed 7,745.06 (-0.52%); Nasdaq Composite closed 26,644.91 (-0.32%) — a full reversal of the morning's +0.1%/+0.5% futures lift on the Anthropic IPO-revenue story. The dominant force by the close was the bond market: the 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.31%, its highest level since 2007, closing in on that year's 5.44% GFC-era peak. Drivers cited include deficit concerns, heavy long-dated bond issuance, and inflation stuck above target — plus, notably, AI-related debt issuance (AMD's $4.75B notes today, the broader vendor-financing/SPV wave) explicitly named as a contributing factor competing with Treasurys for demand.

Calibration note: this morning's "real, AI-driven Monday" framing needs correcting, not extending. A $190-200B 2028 revenue forecast for one AI lab wasn't enough to hold the tape up against a 19-year-high long bond yield.

Oil faded off its morning spike even as the Hormuz shutdown evidence hardened — Brent closed ~$88.31, roughly flat day-over-day, well off the $91.53 early-session read. A genuine, unresolved divergence: either oil hadn't fully priced the vessel-count data by the close, or it's already treating sustained disruption as the base case. US SPR below 300M barrels remains standing structural context.

BTC diverged from the risk-off tape, extending gains to ~$64,242 by evening despite the equity selloff and yield spike — worth watching whether that's a genuine decoupling or a weekend-oversold bounce continuing on its own schedule.

Gold's standing discrepancy resolves: confirmed Monday reads cluster $4,395-4,440, implying Friday's close was closer to ~$4,375 — the $4,487 figure this file had carried forward as "last confirmed clean" appears to have been the error.
