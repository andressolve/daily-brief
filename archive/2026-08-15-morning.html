Iran / Middle East

Delta since Friday evening: the tanker-attack pattern continued overnight (count now 15); Bessent's economic-measures target list leaked for the first time (Chinese banks/yuan channels); USS Lincoln relief confirmed but the welfare story is now contested; a new adjacent front opened in Lebanon (9 killed).

The Hormuz attack pattern did not stop Thursday night — it continued into Friday. UAE's Foreign Ministry confirmed a separate ADNOC-affiliated vessel was struck late Friday, calling it "piracy"; the cumulative count of ADNOC vessels attacked since the war began now stands at 15, a running tally this file hasn't carried before at this precision (Al Jazeera, CNN).

Read: this bears directly on the standing oil-vs-Polymarket divergence. Polymarket's Hormuz contract ticked up modestly overnight (47% Yes, +1.5pp) even as attacks kept happening — which cuts against reading the contract's earlier flatness as "markets have priced max severity already." The attacks aren't stopping; if anything the pace is a genuine, ongoing input the market is still digesting, not a one-off shock that's already fully priced.

Bessent's "unprecedented economic measures" got their first real content leak, and the scope looks bigger than "more Iran sanctions." Bloomberg reports the measures under consideration include targeting Chinese banks and the yuan-denominated payment channels Iran uses to sell oil, plus the shipping/insurance/refining intermediary networks that let Iran keep exporting despite existing sanctions (Bloomberg).

Read: if this lands as reported next week, it's a materially different animal than prior Iran-specific sanctions rounds. Directly targeting Chinese financial institutions risks a US-China spillover that goes beyond the Iran beat entirely, into trade/financial-system risk this file doesn't otherwise track. Worth flagging explicitly as a tail risk to watch, not just an Iran-pressure escalation.

USS Abraham Lincoln: the relief is now confirmed, but the welfare story has curdled into a partisan fight rather than resolving. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao confirmed the Lincoln (266 days deployed, 200 in combat) will return home, with USS George Washington's carrier strike group — which just finished a Vietnam port call 8/5 — en route as relief on what a US official called a "previously planned" rotation, no arrival date given. Separately, the Navy pushed back hard on the "sailors jumping overboard" reporting that drove Thursday's Senate Democrat pressure, disputing any observed rise in suicidal ideation and citing onboard mental-health staffing; Snopes' own investigation (8/14) found the overboard claims unconfirmed (Gulf News, CBS News, Snopes).

Read: the turnover itself softens the "indefinite blockade is straining the force" read from Thursday/Friday — a relief carrier is genuinely inbound. But whether the underlying welfare claims were accurate is now contested and politicized rather than settled, and "previously planned" rotation timing conveniently undercuts the read that this was a reactive scramble. Treat as a live messaging fight, not a resolved thread — don't carry either side's framing as fact.

New, adjacent front: Israeli strikes killed 9 in southern Lebanon Saturday (including 3 children), targeting Hezbollah near Ansar/Deir Zahrani — the deadliest strikes since the June 20 truce (France24). Not yet part of this file's core Gaza/Hormuz tracking, but worth a standing watch-line: Israel now has two live fronts (Gaza stalemate, Lebanon strikes) running in parallel with the separate US-Iran Hormuz standoff.

Iran's parliamentary Hormuz bill: no material progress since Friday — still only general principles/outline approved (7 of 14 articles), full floor vote/Guardian Council review/presidential signature all still pending.
