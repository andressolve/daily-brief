Iran / Middle East

Delta since Sunday morning New Houthi blockade threat against Saudi Arabia; a third US KIA in Iraq; first concrete ceasefire proposal since the MoU collapse.

The Houthis — Iran's Yemeni proxy — declared a maritime blockade/embargo on Saudi Arabia and threatened to close the Bab el-Mandeb strait (roughly 12% of world trade), triggered by a Saudi strike on Sanaa airport aimed at Houthi leaders returning from Khamenei's funeral. This is the war's first genuinely new, non-Iranian-state front, and layers a proxy-pressure axis on top of Riyadh's own direct military threat against Iran logged Sunday. Separately, a third US service member is now confirmed dead — a distinct incident from Friday's Jordan strike: a service member died 7/18 during controlled detonation of an Iranian drone in Iraqi Kurdistan. Cumulative US KIA is now likely 17, though CENTCOM has not directly restated a new round cumulative figure this run — treat as inferred, not confirmed. US strikes extended to a 9th consecutive night.

Structural read — faction lens The first concrete diplomatic development since the MoU-suspension declarations arrived today: mediators, via Qatar, passed Iran a proposed 10-day ceasefire, and Foreign Ministry spokesman Baghaei confirmed receipt, saying the channel is active. But this is happening in parallel with, not instead of, the hawkish track: Ghalibaf (the pragmatic-hawk faction leader) set a hard MoU-implementation precondition, and the IRGC said the Strait "will not be opened." This is exactly the pattern this file's standing thesis predicts — Baghaei (civilian-pragmatist) can open a channel, but he does not have the authority to bind Ghalibaf or the IRGC to it. Whether Tehran formally responds to the ceasefire proposal within 48-72 hours, and which faction's voice dominates that response, is the single most decision-relevant open question heading into tomorrow.

Brent's move above $90 intraday was reportedly "little changed" specifically on the Houthi blockade announcement — read as evidence that Hormuz-closure risk was already priced in before today's news cycle (see Markets section for the full spike-and-round-trip).

Markets / Macro

Delta since Sunday morning Oil spiked to a 5-week high then fully reversed. Equities dispersed. Everything else roughly flat.

Brent gapped from Friday's $88.10 settle to an intraday high of $91.42 (+3.8%, a five-week high), confirming the weekend's mines/downed-MQ-9/first-KIA escalation — then fully reversed after Iran's foreign ministry signaled openness to talks, last trading ~$87.72-88.22, net-flat to slightly down despite the war's most severe weekend to date. WTI mirrored the pattern: touched ~$84-85, last ~$82.61.

Structural read This is the cleanest "spike-then-fade" of the entire war: hard-power escalation (mines, a downed aircraft, a third KIA, a new Houthi front) moved price sharply intraday, but a soft-power signal (Iran's openness-to-talks statement, the same Qatar channel logged in the Iran section) erased the move within the session. At the margin, Iranian diplomatic signaling is now moving oil more than the kinetic events themselves — worth testing again tomorrow against whatever Tehran says about the 10-day ceasefire proposal.

Equities were dispersed, not clean risk-on: S&P +0.18-0.42%, Nasdaq +0.42-0.68% (a chip-sector rebound ahead of this week's Tesla/Alphabet earnings), Dow -0.32% (-164.62 pts). VIX eased to 18.24 (-2.82%) — no fear bid despite the weekend's severity. BTC ~$64,199, essentially flat; gold flat at $4,014.30. Fed no-change reads ~95% (flat vs. Sunday's 94.45%, noise-level — sourced from Polymarket's public page rather than a fresh direct gamma-api curl this run). 10Y remains unresolved via primary source — a standing multi-week gap, not re-flagged further.

Bottom Line

Today is the clearest evidence yet that this war has two speeds — a kinetic one (mines, a new Houthi front, a third US KIA) that keeps escalating on paper, and a signaling one (Qatar's ceasefire channel, Iran's "national interests" talk-openness statement) that markets are pricing far more heavily. Oil's full round-trip from $91 to flat, and Polymarket's simultaneous compression of both MoU-withdrawal contracts, are the same signal from two different instruments: right now, words are moving price more than weapons. That's a genuinely different regime than the "markets shrug off Iran" pattern tracked for most of July, and it's worth testing again tomorrow against Tehran's actual response to the ceasefire proposal. AMD and MU's good single-name days are real, but they happened inside that same volatile session — not a reason to abandon the zero-position stance, now 38 sessions running. If the Kimi K3 technical pushback holds up, it's a live, not yet priced, upside catalyst for MU/NVDA/TSM whenever a deploy signal does fire.
