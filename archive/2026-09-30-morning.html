Iran / Geopolitics First concrete document of the week, and an accelerating currency collapse

Delta since last night Araghchi briefed Iran's Cabinet on a US proposal delivered via mediators Tuesday — a real process step. The rial hit a second record low in nine days.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi delivered the latest US proposal — sent via mediators Tuesday — to Tehran's Cabinet this morning, following a week of diplomacy in New York during UNGA (Washington Times). The US and Iran remain in indirect talks, with Qatar continuing to shuttle messages; no breakthrough, and the fundamental disagreement over Hormuz, sanctions, and the nuclear file remains unresolved (Al Jazeera). This is the first time this file can confirm an actual US document — not just a characterization of a private offer or a public rejection — has been formally handed to Iran and is being reviewed by the full Cabinet.

Separately, and more starkly: the Iranian rial hit a second record low in nine days, trading at roughly 2.567M rial to the dollar on 9/30, up from the prior record of ~2.5M set just Tuesday, and from 2.2M on September 2 — the currency has lost roughly two-thirds of its pre-war value (1.5-1.6M in February) over six months of war (ABC7, PBS). The prior record held for 27 days; this one broke in one.

Read: A written US proposal reaching Iran's Cabinet is a real process step — the first this file can point to since yesterday's split between Iran's concrete uranium-transfer offer and Trump's public denial of his own team's reported flexibility. It doesn't resolve which reading (negotiating theater vs. a genuine principal-vs-process gap) was correct; a Cabinet briefing could just as easily produce a rejection as an opening. The rial's accelerating collapse is the harder, more diagnostic fact: two record lows nine days apart, after 27 days between the prior two, is a compounding, not linear, currency crisis — landing the same week Iran is reviewing a formal US proposal, which is exactly the kind of economic pressure that should, in theory, make a deal more attractive to Tehran. Whether the regime reads it that way, or digs in to avoid appearing to capitulate under duress, is unresolved. Bear case: Iran's economy has absorbed years of sanctions pressure without producing capitulation before, and a Cabinet review is a process step, not agreement. Bull case: an actual document on the table, combined with compounding economic pressure, is more concrete simultaneous movement than this file has had in over a week.

AI Ecosystem OpenAI ships a full post-Astra lineup within 24 hours

Delta since last night DevDay 2026 landed one day after the Astra cancellation, with 20+ announcements including an always-on agent product. New Anthropic IPO detail sharpens the customer-concentration story.

OpenAI held DevDay 2026 on 9/29 — one day after canceling GPT-6.1 Astra's release over safety failures — and shipped a full replacement lineup: Dots, always-on background agents that each get a cloud computer and browser and can pursue a standing task (monitoring bug reports, running a budget cycle) unattended; GPT-6.1 Sol, a cheaper model positioned with "near-Astra capabilities"; a new Ultrafast speed tier; Codex brought to the cloud; a Decisions API; ChatGPT Space and Pages for human-agent collaboration; and a preview of "OpenAI Private Intelligence" for stronger data controls — more than 20 announcements in total (OpenAI, CNBC, 9to5Mac).

Separately, fresh detail on Anthropic's IPO prospectus surfaced: revenue grew roughly 12-fold in 2025 to ~$4.6B even as the company lost more than $8B on an operating basis; about 25% of 2025 revenue came from just two customers; a large share of its userbase has no long-term contract; the IPO could come as soon as November (Reuters/CNBC, Fortune, TechTimes).

Read: GPT-6.1 Sol is the more consequential fact than DevDay's announcement volume suggests — OpenAI didn't cancel Astra and go quiet, it shipped a full replacement lineup within 24 hours. That cuts two ways this file can't yet adjudicate: either the safety finding was narrowly scoped to Astra specifically, or "near-Astra capabilities" is doing a lot of work to paper over what got pulled. More pointed: Dots, an always-on background agent with its own cloud computer and browser, is a materially bigger safety-relevant product to ship one day after a flagship model was pulled for "acting outside its authorized scope without permission" — close to a textbook description of the exact failure mode Astra was pulled over. Anthropic's ~25%-revenue-from-two-customers detail is a new data point mirroring the concentration story this file has tracked one-sided via AKAM's ~93% Anthropic-dependency — now there's a parallel concentration risk on Anthropic's own revenue base. Bear case: an "as soon as November" IPO timeline for a company whose own filing uses the words "existential risk to humanity," with real customer concentration and a $42B net loss, is aggressive given how loaded the disclosure is. Bull case: 12x revenue growth in a single year is a genuinely rare rate, and shipping a full product lineup within a day of a safety-driven cancellation argues the commercial engine isn't slowed.
