Iran / Middle East

Delta since last night: All three standing watch items resolved overnight, sharply — a live kinetic escalation, not lingering rhetoric. Full detail in state/iran.md.

This is a genuine regime-change in the conflict's tempo, worth leading the brief with rather than treating as one more data point. CENTCOM destroyed five Iranian crude tankers near Kharg Island, in retaliation for a second IRGC ballistic-missile attempt on a US Navy warship within 48 hours (exact timing relative to last night's 17:20 PT cutoff is ambiguous across sources — treated as newly captured rather than re-dated). IRGC retaliated with roughly 20 ballistic missiles at Al Azraq airbase in Jordan — 18 intercepted, 2 landed in unpopulated areas, no casualties. IRGC then claimed a new operation, "Ya Haidar Karrar," striking two named US destroyers (USS Delbert D. Black, USS John Paul Jones); CENTCOM's Capt. Tim Hawkins flatly denied it as "a total lie" — the first clean, on-record rebuttal since Washington's days-long institutional silence on a separate question (Rezaei's restricted-zone declaration) began. Read: worth noting that the US chose to respond fast and specifically to the harder, more falsifiable claim (a strike on named Navy vessels) while still not directly engaging the restricted-zone question itself. (Tribune India, Naval Today)

The first unambiguous Hormuz-adjacent kinetic incident of the week landed early this morning. A Panama-flagged tanker (New Andros, carrying 2M barrels of Iraqi fuel oil) was struck by a drone in Iraqi territorial waters around 0300 GMT — fire extinguished, all 22 crew safe. UKMTO reported several merchant vessels in the Gulf hit by "disabling fire" overnight, and Iran claims it struck 10 ships near Hormuz in retaliation for the tanker destructions — a claim not yet independently corroborated. Read: worth testing today whether the "10 ships" claim holds up under independent (UKMTO, shipping-insurer) scrutiny or deflates the way prior Iranian claims have this cycle. (Al-Monitor, Iraqi News)

Faction lens: the IRGC's military track, not the diplomatic one, is giving the restricted zone teeth. The IRGC — not the SNSC/Rezaei diplomatic channel that first floated the restricted-zone idea — has now formally operationalized it, threatening loss of insurance and maritime-support access for violators. Read: consistent with this file's standing "which Iran said it, and do they have the authority to say it" framing — the hard-line security apparatus is the one converting rhetoric into an enforceable policy, a genuinely different signal than a diplomat's public statement. (Al Jazeera)

Oil: the Brent/WTI split flagged last night has resolved into reconvergence, not persisted. Brent broke $100/bbl for the first time in roughly six weeks (~$100.29-102.05 in early trade); WTI rose in step (+2.03% to ~$94.92). Read: both benchmarks rising together confirms last night's working hypothesis that Tuesday's divergence likely reflected thin intraday quotes rather than a genuine regional split — the shared risk premium this morning is the more informative signal. (CNBC)

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last night: New ai-ecosystem-analyst output — a Goldman Sachs Communacopia conference day-one roundup. Full detail in state/ai.md.

The ai-ecosystem-analyst's own assessment: evidence favors continued compute scarcity, but enterprise AI economics remain uneven — no thesis change, new operating color. The roundup reviewed Sept 8 Q&A transcripts from Broadcom, Google Cloud, SAP, Nebius, CoreWeave, Qualcomm, Arista, Cisco, Vertiv, GLOBALFOUNDRIES, and Rivian. NVDA and AMD have not yet presented (NVDA Sept 10, AMD Sept 11).

Qualcomm-Amazon detail sharpened materially — worth correcting how this file has been citing the $60B figure. Qualcomm's SEC 8-K confirms Amazon's warrants (up to 25M shares at $161.26, expiring 2036) vest only on binding orders/purchases up to a $60B ceiling — the $60B is a maximum, not disclosed guaranteed backlog, and only 15% of shares are initially vested. Qualcomm guided ~$5B in total data-center revenue for FY2027, with Amazon revenue starting in the December quarter. Read: the qualifier matters — cite this going forward as a ceiling, not a commitment.

Two other portfolio-relevant data points: Nebius (NBIS) extended its visibility window to 24+ months (from ~18) citing Q1/Q2 2028 capacity requests — not funded contracts — and cleared a Blackwell auction 15-20% above prior pricing, a real scarcity-pricing signal though undisclosed in volume/duration. CoreWeave (CRWV) disclosed a financing-structure shift where lenders now accept renewal risk rather than underwriting only against contracted payments — expands what can be built, but raises the stakes on future utilization and pricing; not a change to CoreWeave's corporate credit rating. Read (bear case): scarce supply can support strong supplier economics today while end-customer ROI stays inconsistent (Broadcom's Hock Tan: meaningful enterprise-wide AI ROI still hard to find), and credit structures increasingly fund deployment against renewal assumptions rather than fully contracted cash flows — a thread worth watching alongside private credit's own renewal-risk exposure below.
