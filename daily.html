Markets / Macro

Delta since this morning: The rally extended through the close, and Meta's after-hours print gave it a second, independent leg.

Meta's Q3 print is tonight's central fact: revenue $64.6B (+26% YoY, beat the $63.38B consensus), a $9.89B legal charge (almost exactly the ~$10B this file flagged as the risk this morning) pulling GAAP EPS to $3.83 against $7.40 ex-charge, a raised 2026 AI capex guide, a 2-for-1 stock split, and a new non-voting Class C share class — the stock rose more than 3% after hours. This is the first hyperscaler AI-capex confidence test since the broader AI-infra selloff, and it passed clean: absorbing a surprise charge and raising spending while still getting rewarded is a stronger signal than a beat alone. SMCI itself closed up roughly 9-12% (reports vary), confirming the premarket pop held the full session. Oil was essentially unchanged: Brent ~$110.13-110.32, WTI ~$98-99 depending on source, still holding the ~$110 Brent band on "Middle East worries" per CNBC, not extending this morning's modest uptick. CRWV had a wide, volatile session — down as much as -6.4% intraday to a fresh 52-week low before recovering to close +2.5% on a fresh Goldman note.

The structural read

Tonight is the cleanest two-data-point confirmation this file has logged in a week: SMCI's hardware beat this morning and Meta's capex-confidence beat tonight point the same bullish direction, on two independent, large, dateable prints rather than one. Bear case: one bullish week doesn't resolve the structural CUDA-moat question or the private-credit NAV questions this file has carried for weeks — Meta's capex confidence says nothing about whether the lenders financing the buildout are themselves sound. Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since this morning: The optimistic "accepted in principle" framing got messier, not cleaner — but Polymarket didn't react.

Araghchi said tonight that "the Americans informed us that they agreed to these conditions, but they wanted to add a few more clauses" to Iran's seven-day Hormuz reopening plan — a real complication on top of this morning's "accepted in principle, wants guarantees" read, since reopening an already-agreed plan with new clauses is a different degree of friction than simply asking for guarantees (bne IntelliNews). He separately warned that "any further escalation around the Strait of Hormuz or elsewhere in the Middle East could seriously endanger civilian lives, regional security and...the global economy," while saying Iran "will never bow to threats" (The National). Important caveat: this is Iran's own characterization, carried by regional outlets tonight — no independent US confirmation of the "added clauses" claim has surfaced. Hormuz-normal-by-Dec-31 reads 11.5% Yes tonight (direct Gamma-API fetch) — identical to this morning, zero movement despite the harder rhetoric.

The structural read

This morning's "accepted in principle" framing didn't survive one full news cycle intact — the US reportedly reopening the negotiation with new clauses is meaningfully different from just wanting guarantees, and this file should downgrade this morning's optimism accordingly, with the caveat that it's a one-sided account. The more interesting fact may be Polymarket's total non-reaction: 11.5% both readings, through a day with real rhetorical movement and zero new physical facts. Bear case: a contract this flat through genuinely harder language could be complacent, not calibrated — worth checking tomorrow for confirmation or correction. Bull case: this is exactly the kind of claims-and-warnings-without-new-facts noise this file's framework says the contract should correctly ignore, and it did.
