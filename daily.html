Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since last night: the US struck Iran overnight — the tail event this file's own calibration instrument priced at ~8.9% happened — and Netanyahu's own party is separately reported weighing non-recognition of Israel's election results.

US forces struck Iranian targets on Qeshm Island overnight (reported starting ~9:48pm ET / ~6:48pm PT), after officials said the IRGC fired a surface-to-air missile at a US aircraft over the Strait of Hormuz; the US hit the missile launcher in response, and a senior official explicitly characterized the strike as defensive and "not the start of new expanded strikes." Iran's IRNA reported five explosions on Qeshm in the early hours of Nov 3; no independent confirmation of damage or casualties was found. The Wall Street Journal separately disclosed this was just one of several limited, previously-covert US military operations against Iran in recent weeks — new information, not previously known. The timing is awkward for the diplomatic-track read: Trump, asked by Reuters on Nov 2 whether he'd resume strikes after the midterms, said "no" — hours before the Qeshm strikes were reported.

Separately, and just as structurally significant: Netanyahu's Likud is reportedly weighing refusing to recognize Israel's own election results outright. No stated legal or factual basis has surfaced yet; the Eisenkot-aligned bloc responded with a joint statement affirming "uncompromising commitment to protecting the election outcomes," accusing Likud of a "dangerous and unhinged delegitimization campaign." This shifts the Israel story from "can Eisenkot assemble 61" to "will Netanyahu's camp contest the count itself," hours before Herzog's Wednesday coalition consultations. Procedurally, opposition parties (Eisenkot's Yashar, Lieberman's Yisrael Beiteinu, Bennett's B'Yachad, others) formally agreed Nov 3 to jointly recommend Eisenkot to Herzog and begin coalition talks immediately once the mandate is granted — firms up the recommendation math but doesn't touch the Lieberman/Arab-abstention governing-majority problem, and now sits under the legitimacy fight's shadow.

On diplomacy: still no formal US response to Araghchi's two-day Hormuz-reopening plan; Araghchi said Nov 3 that Doha consultations had become "highly intensive" and the outcome now depends on "Washington's political rationality," while Ghalibaf said Iran "delivered a strong and rational response" and "the ball is now in America's court." A new factional wrinkle: Iran International reports rumors Ghalibaf has been sidelined as chief negotiator (triggered by Vance's letter being addressed to Rezaei instead), which Araghchi denies, saying Ghalibaf remains chief negotiator with Rezaei's backing. No direct Mojtaba Khamenei statement has addressed Pezeshkian's "certain authorities" claim. The IRGC's Nov 2 claim of two more mined tankers remains neither confirmed by CENTCOM nor explicitly denied; UK Maritime Trade Operations logged 30 vessel attacks in October alone, a record high.

The structural read

The single fact that reorders this file's framing overnight is that the strike this file's own best calibration instrument priced at ~8.9% happened anyway — a useful corrective against reading a low-but-nonzero probability as low-risk. Bear case: officials calling this strike "defensive" and bounded is exactly the framing used after every prior exchange of fire this war, with no track record yet of holding; Netanyahu's own party openly weighing non-recognition of an election result, landing the same 24 hours as the first actual US strike since the ceasefire, is a second, independent instability vector that compounds rather than offsets the military one. Bull case: the strike is reported as narrowly responsive — IRGC fired first, the US hit only the launcher — and both Araghchi's and Ghalibaf's Nov 3 statements keep describing the broader diplomatic track as alive, consistent with Trump's own "no" on resuming strikes hours earlier. Standing watch: Washington's still-unanswered response to Araghchi's plan, and Herzog's Wednesday consultations now carrying the added weight of Likud's legitimacy challenge.

The structural read

Bear case: Ritter's historical base-rate framing — 14 of 4,110 IPOs priced this richly, all underperforming afterward — is a genuinely new and uncomfortable data point for the bull case's best example, arriving the same week Anthropic's own numbers show margin compression accelerating. Bull case: $1.4-1.6T is still an enormous valuation for a three-year-old company with $17B in quarterly revenue growing >1,000% YoY, and a markdown during roadshow preparation is normal price discovery, not evidence the business is broken. AMD's print tonight is the next hard test.
