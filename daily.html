Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since this morning: neither track resolved. Israel's count firmed in the direction already flagged, but produced a hung parliament with a new kingmaker. Iran's formal response to Qatar is still pending.

With ~93% of Israel's vote counted, Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 seats to Eisenkot's 48 — up from the 85%-counted reading this morning, confirming the reversal of last night's exit-poll "Eisenkot leads" narrative. But neither bloc clears the 61-seat threshold: the two Arab parties nearly doubled their projected seats to 19 and are now the likely kingmakers in any coalition negotiation (Bloomberg, NPR). That's a structural fact worth tracking on its own, not folding back into the "who won" framing — a Netanyahu-led coalition that needs Arab-party cooperation is a materially different government than one that doesn't.

On the war: no Pentagon or White House corroboration or denial of this morning's WSJ report (Gulf states told to prepare for a post-midterm campaign) surfaced today. On diplomacy: Qatar is still awaiting Tehran's formal response to its draft deal, with some reporting suggesting it could land as late as Thursday (Jerusalem Post). This morning's split messaging — one official's "welcomes negotiations," a senior official's "unacceptable ideas, not an offer" — is unchanged: neither side has hardened or softened its position since this morning.

The structural read

Nothing resolved today, which itself is informative: a story with this much reported military movement (carrier groups, Marine units, a Patriot battery) generated zero official US confirmation or denial over a full news cycle, and Iran let a deadline-sensitive deal (Qatar's seven-day clock) sit without a formal answer. Bear case: silence from Washington on a leaked war-preparation story, instead of a clarifying statement either way, is consistent with the story being substantially true and simply not ready for public confirmation. Bull case: Tehran not rejecting the deal outright today, despite a senior official's harsh initial characterization, leaves the door open for Thursday's response to land softer than this morning's "unacceptable ideas" framing suggested — silence can cut either way, and a hard rejection would likely have been stated plainly by now.

Markets / Macro

Delta since this morning: the oil/equity divergence got two real stress tests today — a hawkish-split Fed and a messy Alphabet earnings headline — and survived both.

The FOMC held the funds rate at 3.75-4.00% as expected, but the vote was 10-2, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of an immediate 25bp hike — not the dovish-dissent pattern markets might have expected. Chair Warsh declined to give forward guidance for December, said politics played "no role" in the decision, and the statement's description of economic activity was upgraded from "solid" to "strong" (CNBC).

AI Ecosystem

Delta since this morning: tonight was the real test of whether the AI-capex rally survives contact with hyperscaler numbers — it came back bullish on demand, noisy on the headlines.

Microsoft's beat was decisive on every AI-relevant metric: Azure growth accelerated to 48% (vs. ~45% expected), and capex guidance was cut to ~$175B from ~$190B. One caveat worth flagging rather than taking at face value: part of that capex guide-down is an accounting change — extending the estimated useful life of data centers and buildings from 15 to 25 years, which shifts future spend from finance leases to operating leases and lowers reported capex without lowering actual investment. Jim Cramer's read was that this isn't the spending discipline it looks like on a headline (GuruFocus).

Alphabet's headline EPS miss looked damning but was driven almost entirely by a non-operating $8.7B unrealized mark-to-market loss on its SpaceX equity stake. Google Cloud itself grew 103% to $30.7B — the single best cloud-growth number of the night at either company. Shares still fell 7-10% after hours, suggesting the market priced the headline over the composition (Investing.com). CRWV/NBIS's October drawdown context (still down ~35%/~31% for the month) is unchanged; no new data on either name today. AI-bubble-burst Polymarket is carried forward at 8%/23% from this morning's fetch — no evening update obtained.

The structural read

Azure 48% and GCP 103% are demand-side numbers, not spending-side ones, and demand is the harder thing to fake — if hyperscaler customers are consuming that much more cloud/AI capacity, the capex behind it is being validated by usage, not just promised. That cuts against the "Anthropic S-1 is a grift" framing this file has been tracking, even though it doesn't directly address it. Bear case: an accounting change that flatters Microsoft's capex optics is itself a tell — companies don't restate depreciation schedules when the real number is comfortable to report as-is. Bull case: both companies' cloud growth came in at the high end of a demand signal that's much harder to manufacture than guidance language, and that's the number that should matter most to the underlying thesis. Thursday's Amazon earnings is the last leg of this test.
