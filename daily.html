AI Ecosystem

Delta since Thursday evening: Nvidia named a first-ever independent board chair; House Republicans want Huang to testify Nov 18. Anthropic's IPO terms were reaffirmed, not changed.

Nvidia's own 8-K, filed Nov 12 with the board action dated Nov 11, confirms CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while she retains her CFO title, and separately names Harvey C. Jones — a Nvidia director since 1993 and former CEO/Executive Chairman of Synopsys — as the board's first-ever independent Board Chair (SEC 8-K). The board's CEO search explicitly considers internal and external candidates, with no named timeline. Separately, House Republicans have called on Huang to testify before Congress on Nov 18 about the affair and the China-intelligence allegations (PC Gamer, Al Jazeera) — the same week Anthropic's ANT is targeted to begin trading, and Nvidia is one of seven strategic investors in Anthropic's $26B IPO-linked private placement. On Micron: Taiwan reporting this morning describes the union's Nov 6 session as ending without resolution and the union calling it a "defeat," with a fourth session tentatively on the calendar for Nov 17 even as the union has signaled it may not show (Taipei Times) — worth tracking as "still scheduled, status uncertain," not a confirmed skip. On Anthropic: this morning's reporting reaffirms, rather than changes, Thursday's terms — $70-78/share band, 825M shares, Nasdaq ticker ANT, pricing expected around Nov 18 with trading targeted for Nov 19, inside the standing goal of debuting before Thanksgiving (Nov 26); no amendment or price-range change found.

The structural read

Installing a first-ever independent board chair within 48 hours of a CEO resignation is itself a signal: boards move this decisively when they expect a prolonged governance process, not a quick return to business as usual. Bear case: a House testimony request landing the same week as Anthropic's debut means the leadership vacuum and the national-security question will both still be live when ANT starts trading — compounding, not separate, risks. Bull case: fast, decisive board action is exactly what a well-functioning board does to stabilize governance quickly, and should be read as competent crisis management rather than escalation. Standing watch: any news on a permanent CEO, how NVDA's Nov 17 earnings call handles questions with Kress as interim CEO and no permanent successor named, and whether Micron's tentative Nov 17 session actually happens.
