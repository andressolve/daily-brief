The bank-outlooks cross-check

Today's supplemental research note synthesizes roughly fourteen major banks' 2027 outlooks on AI capital expenditure. Estimates for 2027 global AI investment range from ~$1.4T (UBS) to ~$1.8T (Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs). The most useful piece is J.P. Morgan Private Bank's own reproducible fleet-breakeven model: at a 15% pre-tax cost of capital and the bank's assumed hardware lifespans, a 129-GW fleet by end-2029 needs $2.93 per GPU-hour at 71% rented utilization to clear its revenue hurdle — implying $2.87T in required annual end-customer revenue against a modeled $3.57T of potential revenue (27M software engineers at $3,228/month, plus 60% of 357M other knowledge workers at $539/month). That's a roughly $700B modeled surplus — but a 29% shortfall in enterprise revenue alone erases it entirely. Separately, BofA/Merrill estimates a 20% cut to 2027 AI capex would lower AI-semiconductor EPS by 35-40% and S&P 500 EPS by 7-8% before secondary effects.

This is bank research, not news, and it changes no probability on file — but it gives Andres's standing "bear case on every take" mandate a sharper, falsifiable target than aggregate capex totals: the thing to actually watch is realized enterprise-revenue retention and expansion on production AI deployments (the kind of hard data Dell's backlog or CoreWeave/Nebius utilization figures represent), not announced capex dollars, which several of the surveyed banks' own outlooks note can diverge from actual demand realization.

Bear case: the market has now had three full sessions since the second-affair story broke and still hasn't produced a resolution on NVIDIA's own governance risk, even as the broader sector rallies around it — that gap is the market's own uncertainty showing up in price, and the bank-outlooks synthesis gives a concrete number (a 29% enterprise-revenue shortfall) that would blow a hole in even the bullish capex case. Bull case: none of the governance reporting is yet company-confirmed, hard demand data (Dell's record $132.3B backlog, NVDA's own Q3 beat) remains untouched by any of this, and a rallying sector broadly suggests the market isn't pricing a capex pullback at all right now.
