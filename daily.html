AI Ecosystem

Delta since last run: a third (California) and fourth (Congress) body now examining NVIDIA; the source analyst's supplemental note draws the sharpest line yet between financing stress and demand strength; Anthropic's ANT round-tripped hard.

California's DOJ confirmed it served NVIDIA an investigative subpoena on Nov 19, seeking evidence on possible transfers of advanced chips or confidential information to China and on what NVIDIA told investors about that risk — a formal investigative step, no charge or finding, no new timetable on succession. This adds a third institution (alongside the SEC's reported civil probe and the DOJ's Megaspeed criminal probe) actively examining NVIDIA. New today: Reuters reports Democratic Representatives Gregory Meeks and Sydney Kamlager-Dove wrote Commerce Secretary Lutnick on Nov 19 seeking records of NVIDIA's interactions with Commerce officials investigating Megaspeed — a fourth, congressional-oversight angle focused on how Commerce itself handled enforcement.

The AI-ecosystem research desk's supplemental note draws a genuine split by evidence type, not a single verdict. Financing stress is real and now quantified: CoreWeave's 9.75% senior notes due 2031 yielded ~15.7% on Nov 19, and its Q3 net interest expense ($846M) ran 4.1x its operating income ($207M) even with revenue up 168% to $3.66B. Hardware demand is also real and strong: Taiwan's October export orders hit a record $123.8B, +78.3% y/y, and TSMC's October revenue was +59.7% y/y. The framing that matters: the selloff is testing who pays for the buildout, not whether the buildout is happening.

Anthropic's IPO formally closed — underwriters exercised their full greenshoe (147M additional shares), bringing the gross offering to $93.5B (~$91.6B net to Anthropic) plus a separate $26.0B strategic placement, both now closed. But ANT itself has round-tripped hard: from a $111.50 open and a $97.17 first-day close (+17.1%) down to $87.89 in Friday premarket (-9.6%) — a real give-back, now barely above its $83 offer.

Valuation reset: at Friday's $141.28 premarket price, NVDA trades at 9.4x the source analyst's modeled forward EPS ($15.05); the bear-case scenario (25% weight) implies a value near $99, the bull case (40% weight) implies $144 — Friday's actual price sits almost exactly at the bear-case fair value, not below it, which argues for an orderly repricing rather than a panic. A new subjective prediction, P054, assigns 35% probability that NVDA trades at or below $130 through Nov 30.

The strongest evidence of stress is in financing, while the latest evidence on hardware shipments and orders still points to strong near-term demand. — AI-ecosystem research desk, supplemental note, Nov 20

Private Credit

Delta since last run: the first hard, quantified credit-stress number on the AI-infrastructure financing story — and it's CoreWeave's own existing debt, not a new deal.

CoreWeave's 9.75% senior unsecured notes due 2031 yielded ~15.7% on Nov 19 — a distressed-adjacent level — with Q3 net interest expense running 4.1x operating income despite 168% revenue growth. This is a secondary-market quote on existing unsecured debt, distinct from the separately-reported $4B investment-grade loan (Morgan Stanley/MUFG, Meta-contract-backed) CoreWeave has been negotiating since Nov 11 — that loan remains unpriced, now 9+ days past its original timeline. The gap between a 15.7%-yielding unsecured bond and a still-unclosed investment-grade-targeted, contract-backed facility is exactly the two-tier financing structure this file's framework anticipated, now showing up as real market pricing.

FS KKR Capital's incentive-fee waiver extension (past its fourth consecutive quarter) has not been announced — an open question. No new redemption prints, NAV marks, or litigation developments on OWL, APO, or ARCC since yesterday.

Bottom Line

This is no longer a single-name story. A confirmed correction in the Nasdaq 100 and semis, bitcoin finally breaking down instead of hedging, gold not catching a bid, and firming Fed hike odds together describe a real, broadening risk-off episode — even though NVIDIA's own compounding legal overhang (now four distinct investigating/oversight bodies) remains the most-cited trigger.
