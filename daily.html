Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since Sunday morning: none. Third straight quiet morning on both the war/peace track and the Israel coalition track.

Nothing newer was found than the Nov 9–11 record on either track. Trump is still calling a renewed strike "highly unlikely" while the naval blockade stays in place; Qatar's mediation is still stuck on the same gap — Washington wants nuclear concessions first, Tehran wants sanctions relief and frozen-asset access first; and Iran's Rezaei is still publicly refusing to move "a single atom" of enriched uranium. Rubio's framing that the sides are "not at the verge of a breakthrough" is now a week old with no update, and no CENTCOM statement has directly addressed the pattern of low-grade tanker incidents.

The structural read

Three consecutive quiet mornings is starting to become a signal in its own right, not just an absence of one. On Israel, the more informative story is factional: Liberman's Nov 11 acquisition of the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee chairmanship is real personal leverage, not a procedural footnote, and it gives him every incentive to slow-walk Eisenkot's 28-day coalition clock, now at 20 days left with no visible progress reported. Bear case: a week-plus pause at the top does not mean the underlying conflict de-escalated — Iran's public rhetoric has hardened, not softened, even as analyst-estimated strike odds have fallen toward ~30%. Bull case: the longer both sides go without restating hardened positions or reporting a new tanker incident, the more this looks like a genuine stalemate-level de-escalation rather than a pause before the next flare-up. No Hormuz escalation this week also means no oil-shock pass-through to the energy-hedge thesis — consistent with markets pricing this morning's selloff as AI-governance-specific, not geopolitical.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since Sunday morning: the biggest delta of the morning. NVIDIA's succession question escalated from a settled interim arrangement to a live three-way fight, and SemiAnalysis cut its own 2030 AI-capex forecast by ~40%. AI analyst source reviewed: research/2026/2026-11-16-update.md (report date 2026-11-16), catching up on research/2026/2026-11-13-update.md (report date 2026-11-13) in the same pass.

The Financial Times reported Sunday (11/15) that Jensen Huang has discussed a possible return to NVIDIA with confidants since his Nov 11 resignation, with an ally organizing a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the board for his reinstatement (FT via Yahoo Finance). Elon Musk is among more than 176,000 signers of an open letter urging reinstatement (Forbes), on the theory that many of Huang's own supply-chain deals were handshake agreements that could unwind without him personally driving them. Pulling the other way, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has publicly called for a "dramatic shakeup" favoring an outside hire, analyst Dan Ives sees no realistic path back for Huang, and unconfirmed reporting floats Arm CEO Rene Haas as one candidate headhunters have sounded out. NVIDIA calls the succession speculation premature, with Colette Kress continuing as interim CEO and no board decision reported (CNBC).

Separately — and more consequential for the long-run thesis than the Huang drama — SemiAnalysis cut its own 2030 global AI-accelerator capex forecast from $1.531T to $923B (roughly 40%) in a 11/13 revision, attributing it to tighter capital markets and slower frontier-lab expansion. The important nuance, preserved from the source analyst's own framing: physical volumes fell far less than dollars — silicon area -15%, HBM bit demand -12%, power draw -34% — suggesting the cut leans more on falling chip prices and efficiency gains than a collapsing physical buildout. That's genuinely two-sided for the computational-abundance thesis: cheaper, more efficient compute is part of the logic even as it undercuts the dollar-capex figure this file has used as a demand proxy all year. Sourcing caveat, stated plainly: the primary, paywalled SemiAnalysis note has not been reached; the figures are corroborated only across three secondary outlets (404K Research, PANews, Futunn) — treat as strong but unverified, not confirmed.

Pulling the other way: Cerebras's Q3 print (Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle added as customers alongside OpenAI and G42; $26.1B in remaining performance obligations; stock +17% after hours per Cerebras's own release and CNBC) resolves the long-standing customer-concentration bear case for AI-inference demand. Also still unpriced: CoreWeave's reported $4B investment-grade loan, backed by a Meta contract (Bloomberg).

The structural read

Two things moved the furthest this morning, and they cut in different directions. The NVDA succession fight widening is governance noise with real near-term price impact — it tells you nothing new about chip demand — but it lands the day before an earnings call that was already a referendum on the ~70% FY2028 growth guide, and may now also become a referendum on interim leadership itself. Bear case: if the SemiAnalysis cut holds up under primary-source verification, it is the first credible, quantified sign that 2026–2027 hyperscaler capex raises are not a reliable guide to 2028–2030 spending — a real crack in the thesis this file has leaned on all year, and it still needs reconciling against two other SemiAnalysis-adjacent figures already tracked (a ~4% realized-2027-delay figure from Nov 5, and UBS's ~30%-of-2028-capacity-at-risk estimate from Nov 6) that use different methodologies and vintages. Bull case: Cerebras's customer diversification is real, verified, non-NVDA evidence that inference-compute demand is broad enough to support a second production-scale architecture — the strongest demand-side data point logged in weeks.
