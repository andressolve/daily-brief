The Oil Sanctions Waiver: Game-Changer or Propaganda?

Iran's semi-official Tasnim News Agency reported May 18 that the revised US text includes a temporary OFAC waiver on Iranian oil sanctions pending a final agreement. Bloomberg picked up the report, framing it as "Iranian media said." No US government confirmation.

If real, this is the single most important concession of the entire war. Sanctions relief is Iran's core demand. A temporary waiver would: (a) address Iran's top grievance, (b) release Iranian oil to global market, pressing prices down, (c) give Trump domestic political cover on $5+ gas prices. Oil eased immediately — Brent from >$111 to ~$110.

Structural read: This is classic Trump max-pressure negotiation — threaten annihilation on Truth Social while offering the biggest economic carrot via diplomatic text. The incoherence is the strategy: overwhelm Iran with simultaneous signals so they can't ignore either. But the incoherence also makes it harder for Tehran to take any single signal seriously, which is why Khamenei rejected the May 16 proposal within hours.

The Fundraising Model Is Broken

Non-listed BDCs returned ~$7B to investors in Q1 2026 while raising only ~$5B — the first time outflows have surpassed inflows on record. (Bloomberg) This is the net-negative fundraising tipping point. New capital cannot keep pace with redemptions. The sector cannot grow its way out of the problem.

PIK: The "Extend and Pretend" in Data Form

Average public BDC now receives 8% of investment income via payment-in-kind. Borrowers are paying interest with more debt instead of cash. This is exactly the pattern the SEC is investigating as "extend and pretend" — funds avoiding write-downs by modifying loan terms rather than recognizing losses. (Bloomberg)

Rate Path: Double-Edged Sword

Zero Fed cuts in 2026 is now consensus. This is positive for BDC floating-rate NII (higher rates = higher interest income) but negative for credit quality (higher debt service for borrowers). With PIK at 8%, the evidence says borrowers are already struggling. Net effect: default risk rises with every quarter of higher-for-longer.
