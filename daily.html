The structural read

The gap between "House Republicans want Huang to testify before Congress" (this morning's framing) and "three committee chairs want a closed-door briefing, and Huang tried to send his lawyer instead" (tonight's correction) matters: the latter reads as a company trying to manage exposure quietly, the former reads as a public reckoning. Lawmakers' visible irritation at the counsel offer suggests the quiet-management approach isn't working. Bear case: an active headhunter search confirms the board itself doesn't expect Huang's situation to resolve quickly enough to avoid a real succession process — that's a multi-month overhang, not a multi-week one. Bull case: a board moving decisively on both governance (independent chair) and succession (active search) within a week of the resignation is competent crisis management, and the market's dip-buying suggests some investors see it that way too.
