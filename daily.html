Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since last night: First concrete reopening proposal either side has floated — Iran's seven-day initiative, reportedly accepted by the US in principle. New IRGC cable-cutting threat. Hormuz Polymarket continues easing.

Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi told Iraqi broadcaster Al Ekhbariya that Tehran presented a seven-day initiative under which Hormuz would be fully reopened on the sixth day; Iran says Washington has accepted the plan in principle but is seeking additional guarantees before signing on (Gulf News, LaPresse). Secretary of State Rubio, speaking the same day, held the standing US red line — no deal that leaves Iran a path to a nuclear weapon — while explicitly welcoming Qatari and Pakistani mediation (Washington Times). This is the first time this file has logged a concrete, dated reopening mechanism that the US side hasn't simply dismissed.

New escalation vector: an unnamed IRGC official reportedly threatened to target undersea internet cables running through Hormuz if Iran's own infrastructure is attacked — a materially different threat category (civilian data infrastructure, not shipping) than anything tracked before (CBS News). Iran's IRGC also says a third tanker hit a mine transiting Hormuz since Oct 20 — the mine-strike cadence continues uninterrupted; IMF PortWatch transit data remains unrefreshed since Oct 18. Hormuz-normal-by-Dec-31 reads 11.5% Yes this morning (direct Gamma-API fetch, $15.39M volume) — down from last night's 12%, continuing the reversion, and for the first time in over a week moving in a direction consistent with an actual diplomatic development rather than just fading claims. Brent $110.13 (+1.2%), WTI $99.58 (+1.8%) — a modest uptick, not an extension of Monday/Tuesday's sharper Houthi-driven bounce.

The structural read

Today's real news is that a concrete Iranian reopening proposal and a US response that isn't flat rejection are both on the record on the same day — "accepted in principle, wants more guarantees" is meaningfully different from the stalemate this file has carried since the war's 39th day. Bear case: a "seven-day initiative" with the US still negotiating guarantees is not a deal, and a cable-cutting threat landing the same week as a reopening offer reads as Tehran negotiating from a position of leverage it intends to keep, not good-faith de-escalation. Bull case: this is the most concrete, dated reopening framework either side has put on the table since this file started tracking the blockade, and Polymarket's continued gentle easing is at least directionally consistent with a real, if fragile, opening.

Markets / Macro

Delta since last night: The Asia chip rout this file tracked all week reversed hard overnight — on a Super Micro earnings beat, not on Iran or credit news resolving.

Super Micro's preliminary Q1 FY2027 update (released after Tuesday's US close) raised revenue guidance to $18.5-19.5B from $14.5-15.5B and gross margin to 12.0-12.5% from 10.4-10.8% — beating its own prior guide by roughly 25% on AI-server demand (MarketScreener). SpaceX separately reported Starlink/Starshield revenue up 830% YoY to $7.6B, prompting Elon Musk to call AI-overcapacity concerns "ridiculous." The combination flipped the overnight Asia tape: Kospi closed +3.29% to 5,735.67 (Samsung +5.67-7.3%, SK Hynix +5.81-8%) — a full reversal of Tuesday's -3.73% close — reinforced by South Korea reporting record semiconductor exports (+222.5% YoY in the first 20 days of October) (Korea JoongAng Daily). US futures followed higher but less violently: Nasdaq 100 +0.6%, S&P 500 +0.24%, Dow flat; Super Micro itself +12-12.7% premarket (Investing.com/Reuters). Brent $110.13 (+1.23%), WTI $99.58 (+1.80%) — holding the ~$110 band. 10Y 5.12% (flat), VIX 17.45 (+0.8%). Meta reports Q3 after today's close — now landing the same day as this sharp AI-sentiment reversal, raising the stakes on its capex guide.

The structural read

This is the cleanest reversal this file has logged on the "Asia chip rout, US stabilization" divergence it's tracked all week — and it came from company-specific hard data (SMCI beating its own guide by ~25%), not from the Iran, credit, or financing threads this file has carried as the rout's cause. If the Asia selloff was genuinely about AI-infra financing stress, a single hardware-demand beat shouldn't fully reverse it — the fact that it did argues demand fear was doing more of the work in the prior selloff than financing fear was, or the two are more loosely coupled than recent framing assumed. Bear case: a single name's earnings surprise reversing a multi-day regional selloff in one session is the kind of overshoot that can partially unwind if tonight's Meta print disappoints. Bull case: this is the second time in two weeks (after TSM's Oct 15 record print) that hard demand data has landed in this complex — if Meta confirms tonight, that's two independent, large data points in the same direction within a week.

AI Ecosystem

Delta since last night: Google released TorchTPU, a second hyperscaler attacking the CUDA moat within 48 hours of Amazon. The AI-bubble-burst contract reversed hard.

Google open-sourced TorchTPU at the PyTorch Conference in San Jose, presented by Google engineering VP Bill Jia — a native PyTorch backend for Cloud TPUs requiring no code rewrite, just a torch.device("tpu") call, explicitly positioned alongside Amazon's TorchNeuron (released one day earlier) as a second hyperscaler attacking Nvidia's CUDA lock-in with the identical mechanism (TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE). Google says it's working directly with Meta, Hugging Face, and the vLLM/SGLang inference-serving projects to integrate TorchTPU — a materially broader coalition than Amazon assembled. Nvidia has still made no public response to either tool.

The AI-bubble-burst Polymarket contract reversed hard: 9.8% Yes this morning (direct fetch, $2.49M volume) — down from last night's "held the high" 11%, the first real reversal since the contract's Tuesday climb to its all-time high, landing the same morning Super Micro's and SpaceX's results reversed the Asia chip rout that had been cited as this contract's underlying driver (markets.md section above). Mid-2027 variant: 21.5% Yes, up from 19.5% — moving opposite the near-dated leg. CRWV and NBIS both +3% today as Musk called AI-overcapacity concerns "ridiculous" — sentiment, not company-specific news (24/7 Wall St.).

The structural read

TorchTPU is the more structurally important fact this morning. Amazon alone attacking the CUDA moat could be read as one hyperscaler's self-interested platform play; Google doing the identical thing 48 hours later, with Meta/Hugging Face/vLLM/SGLang partners already lined up, is much harder to dismiss as a single company's opportunism. The CUDA moat has always rested on switching costs being too high to bother — two zero-rewrite escape hatches landing in the same week is the sharpest test of that moat this file has tracked, even though Nvidia's stock hasn't reacted to either. The AI-bubble contract's reversal is the cleaner, faster-resolving story: last night's framing credited the contract's stickiness specifically because it was tied to a real hardware-selloff event — and the test of that claim was always whether it would move the other way on an equally real, opposite-direction event. It did, within 24 hours. Bear case: TorchNeuron and TorchTPU are both brand-new and unproven at production scale — CUDA has survived "this time it's different" threats before (ROCm, prior TPU pushes) without losing meaningful share. Bull case: the AI-bubble contract's clean, mirror-image reversal on a hard-data event is exactly the discriminating behavior that raises confidence in reading Polymarket levels as signal, not noise.
