Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since last night: the military track moved from reporting to incidents. The diplomatic and Israel-coalition tracks are essentially unchanged.

Iran's military spokesman declared the Strait of Hormuz under Iran's "strategic control," staying shut "until conditions are fully met." The IRGC's aerospace force claimed it shot down a 14th US MQ-1 drone near Sirik, Hormozgan province, and the IRGC Navy separately announced capturing a US MQM-170 drone in the western strait. Iran said three oil tankers struck mines and caught fire attempting to exit via an unauthorized route (House of Saud, WANA, AP via Click2Houston). The US Navy issued an advisory Oct 28 telling vessels without an essential need to avoid transiting the Gulf of Oman and the Strait of Hormuz. Washington reportedly set three conditions for Iran to avoid a strike: ship its highly enriched uranium to the US, halt regional attacks, and open the waterway (jfeed). Axios reports US-Israeli planning now centers on a week of "massive strikes" after the Nov 3 midterms if talks fail — more specific than, not a departure from, Wednesday's WSJ Gulf-states report. The UK is separately calling for Hormuz to reopen; Russia says it's ready to mediate if asked.

On diplomacy: Iran's rejection of Qatar's draft deal as "unacceptable ideas" held overnight, with no further hardening and no softening. Trump continues to leave the door open to new strikes. On Israel: with 85.6% counted, Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 seats to Eisenkot's 48, Arab parties 19 — unchanged in substance, but with new color: Eisenkot has left conditional room for Arab-party cooperation if those parties denounce "Hamas as a terrorist organization that must be destroyed," while Netanyahu has called an Arab-inclusive coalition "a shame and a disgrace" (NPR, Gulf News).

The structural read

Last night's framing — that the military and diplomatic tracks may be diverging rather than running in parallel — got its first hard, incident-level evidence overnight. Bear case, sharpened further: Washington's three stated conditions and Iran's stated nuclear-flexibility red line remain flatly incompatible, and real incidents are now accumulating inside the window before the stated Nov-3-midterms trigger — if either side treats last night's drone/mine incidents as justification to act early, that timeline could compress. Bull case: Eisenkot's conditional opening toward Arab-party cooperation is a genuine, if narrow, coalition off-ramp that didn't exist in yesterday's framing, and the UK's reopening push plus Russia's mediation offer are concurrent diplomatic tracks the pure-escalation narrative can undervalue. The sharpest open tension is calibration, not narrative: Hormuz Polymarket is still flat at ~12% even after literal drone shootdowns and mined tankers overnight — either the contract is the wrong instrument for this story, or the market is reading these incidents as consistent with an already-priced-in closed-strait baseline rather than new escalation.

Correction, per protocol

Last night's entry framed Microsoft's capex guidance as "discipline" — that was wrong. Q3 actual capex was $45.2B (including $7.4B finance lease), and Microsoft guided next quarter's capex to top $50B, an acceleration, because GPU-supplier delivery delays are pushing usable capacity — and the spending behind it — into H1 2027. Only the full calendar-year 2026 figure trimmed modestly (~$175B toward ~$170B), and that's against an accelerating near-term run-rate, not a real pullback (FourWeekMBA). This is filed here rather than silently fixed, per this file's own standing protocol.
