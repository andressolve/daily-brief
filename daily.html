Why NVDA couldn't hold its best backdrop in weeks

NVIDIA is itself a roughly $10 billion investor in Anthropic's IPO, so today was supposed to be the clean test of whether a historic, sentiment-positive event in its own ecosystem could lift the stock. It didn't — NVDA gave back an early 4.7% intraday gain to close down 3.3%, with coverage pointing to investors "weighing CEO transition fallout despite strong Q3 results." That is now a sixth consecutive losing session, spanning a beat-and-raise, an on-record board statement ruling out Jensen Huang's return, and today's historic IPO.

Bear case: if a historic IPO in NVIDIA's own backyard can't move the stock positively even for an hour, the standing "contained, company-specific, not systemic" framing needs to be held more loosely — something structural, not just succession noise, may be getting priced.

Bull case: Anthropic itself closed up 17% on debut — AI-sector demand is real and durable. Today's damage is concentrated in NVDA specifically and in the broader indices (partly Fed-rate-driven), not a repeat of a systemic AI-capex re-rating across the whole sector.

Iran / Middle East Geopolitics

Delta since this morning: The "paused" read lasted under a day. The exchange continued and widened to a new actor (Houthis) and a new target (Saudi Arabia's financial capital).

Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base was targeted again today — intercepted, no reported damage. Bahrain faced its fourth Iranian attack in six days — also intercepted. A tanker was reportedly struck again in or near the Strait of Hormuz; Iran claimed two tanker explosions per oil-market coverage, though this wasn't independently confirmed by a primary maritime-security source this run.

New and more significant: an Iran-aligned Houthi missile — not a direct IRGC strike — damaged Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund. Saudi civil defense reported blown-out windows and bent facade panels across up to eight floors; no injuries reported. This is a different axis entirely — Yemen-based Houthi action against Saudi Arabia — from the Bahrain/Qeshm/Kuwait pattern this file has tracked all week, and it broadens the set of actors and targets in play.

Qatar's foreign ministry called today's round "a dangerous escalation" risking diplomatic efforts. Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and the UAE all condemned the attacks. Trump said Thursday he is "not rushing into any decision" on further US action against Iran — a restraint signal, though notably not the same as ruling anything out.
